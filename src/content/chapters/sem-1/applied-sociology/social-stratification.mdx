## In simple words

Social stratification is the division of society into layers or strata based on caste, class, gender, race or religion. People in higher strata usually have more money, power and access to health care.

Stratification is unequal and often passes from one generation to the next. It creates differences in health and life expectancy.

## Forms of stratification

| Form | Basis | Indian example |
|---|---|---|
| Caste | Birth | Brahmin, Dalit, tribal groups |
| Class | Wealth, occupation, education | Rich, middle class, poor |
| Gender | Male, female, third gender | Men, women, transgender persons |
| Race | Physical features | Less common in India |

## Inequality and health

- Lower strata often live in crowded, unsanitary conditions.
- They may not afford treatment or medicines.
- They may face disrespect in health facilities.
- Women and girls often eat last and get less care.

## Social mobility

Social mobility is movement between strata — up or down. Education and jobs are the main routes upward in India.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Notice cost barriers** — a patient may skip medicines because of price; suggest cheaper options or schemes.
- **Treat all equally** — give the same respect and care to every patient regardless of caste, class or gender.
- **Respect dignity** — do not make assumptions based on how a patient dresses or speaks.
- **Report discrimination** — if a patient is treated unfairly, tell the senior nurse or doctor.
- **Use government schemes** — help eligible patients access free or low-cost care.

## 60-second revision

- Stratification = division into unequal layers.
- Bases: caste, class, gender, religion.
- Lower strata = less access to health care.
- Caste by birth; class by wealth/education/occupation.
- Nurses give equal care and notice cost barriers.
