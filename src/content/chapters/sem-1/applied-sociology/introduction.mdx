## In simple words

Sociology is the study of society, social relationships and social institutions. It looks at how people live together in groups — family, community, caste, class — and how society shapes health, illness and health behaviour.

While psychology studies the individual mind, sociology studies the group and the social forces around the person.

Nurses meet patients from many social backgrounds. Understanding society helps a nurse give respectful, fair care and see why some people cannot get treatment easily.

## Scope of sociology

| Area | What it studies |
|---|---|
| Social structure | How society is organised into groups and institutions |
| Culture | Beliefs, values, customs and ways of life |
| Socialisation | How people learn social norms and roles |
| Social stratification | Class, caste, gender and inequality |
| Social change | How societies change over time |
| Clinical sociology | Applying sociology in health settings |

## Sociology vs psychology

| Sociology | Psychology |
|---|---|
| Studies society and groups | Studies the individual mind |
| Focus on social institutions | Focus on behaviour and personality |
| Looks at class, caste, family | Looks at thinking, emotion, motivation |

  **Sociology is the scientific study of society, social relationships and social institutions.** It helps nurses understand how society affects health.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **See barriers** — cost, distance, caste, gender and education can stop a patient from getting care.
- **Respect culture** — food, modesty, family roles and beliefs about illness vary by community.
- **Involve the family** — in Indian society, family often decides about treatment.
- **Work with the community** — community health nurses use sociology to plan health education.
- **Treat fairly** — give the same good care to every patient regardless of caste, class or gender.
