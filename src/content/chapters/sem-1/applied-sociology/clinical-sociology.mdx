## In simple words

Clinical sociology applies sociology to health care. It sees the patient not just as a body with disease, but as a social person with family, work, culture and beliefs.

The hospital is a social system with its own hierarchy, roles and rules. Understanding it helps nurses work better and help patients cope.

## The patient as a social person

Illness affects more than the body:
- Work and income may be lost.
- Family roles change (a breadwinner may become dependent).
- Relationships may be strained.
- The patient may feel stigma or shame.

## The sick role (Talcott Parsons)

| Right | Obligation |
|---|---|
| Excused from normal duties | Try to get well |
| Not responsible for illness | Seek competent help |
| Receive care | Co-operate with treatment |

The sick role is temporary. Problems arise when a patient does not want to give up the role or refuses to accept being ill.

## The hospital as a social system

- Hierarchy: matron, doctors, nurses, aides, cleaners — each with a role.
- Rules: visiting hours, shifts, documentation.
- Communication: handovers, case notes, rounds.
- The patient enters as a stranger and must learn the hospital's rules.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Ask about work and family** — illness may have cost the patient their job or changed family roles.
- **Explain hospital rules** — visiting hours, shifts and routines are new to the patient.
- **Support the sick role** — allow rest and co-operation with treatment; do not blame the patient.
- **Watch stigma** — some illnesses (mental illness, HIV, TB) carry shame; offer privacy and respect.
- **Plan discharge** — consider whether the family can care for the patient at home.
