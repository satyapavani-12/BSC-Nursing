## In simple words

Social structure is the organised pattern of relationships and institutions that make up a society. It includes status (position), role (expected behaviour), groups and institutions such as family, education and health care.

A person's position in the social structure affects their access to food, housing, education and health care — and therefore their health.

## Status and role

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Position in society | Doctor, patient, mother, student |
| Role | Expected behaviour for a status | A nurse cares for patients |
| Achieved status | Earned through effort | Nurse, teacher |
| Ascribed status | Given by birth | Caste, gender, race |
| Role conflict | Clashing role demands | Nurse who is also a patient's relative |

Every status carries a role. Role strain happens when one role is hard to fulfil; role conflict happens when two roles clash.

## Groups

- **Primary group** — small, close, personal (family, friends).
- **Secondary group** — large, formal, impersonal (hospital staff, trade union).

Primary groups give emotional support; secondary groups get tasks done.

## Social institutions

Major institutions: family, marriage, religion, education, economy, polity, health care. Each has rules and roles. The health-care institution includes hospitals, clinics, PHCs and CHCs, with its own hierarchy and rules.

<Callout type="must" title="Status vs role">
  **Status is the position; role is the behaviour expected from it.** A patient's status carries the role of co-operating with treatment.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Understand the patient** — a patient's status and role affect how they behave in hospital.
- **Respect the sick role** — a sick person is excused from normal duties and is expected to seek help and get well.
- **Watch role conflict** — a nurse caring for a relative, or a mother who is also a patient, may face conflicting demands.
- **Use primary groups** — involve family in care and education; they give emotional support.
- **Know your own role** — work within the nursing role and refer when a task belongs to another professional.

<Callout type="note" title="Memory trick">
  Status = **S**tation (where you stand). Role = **R**ole (how you act). S = stand, R = act.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Social structure = organised relationships and institutions.
- Status = position; role = expected behaviour.
- Achieved = earned; ascribed = by birth.
- Primary group = close; secondary = formal.
- Sick role = excused from duties, expected to get well.
- Role conflict = clashing role demands.
