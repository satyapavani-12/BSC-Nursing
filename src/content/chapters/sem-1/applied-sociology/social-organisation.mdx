## In simple words

Social organisation is a stable, orderly society where people know their roles and institutions work well. Social disorganisation is the breakdown of that order — when families, communities or institutions fail to support people.

Disorganisation such as crime, family breakdown, unemployment or migration can harm health and make nursing care harder.

## Agents of social control

These keep society orderly:
- Family — teaches norms from childhood.
- School — teaches rules and roles.
- Religion — gives moral guidance.
- Law and police — enforce rules.
- Media — shows what is accepted.

## Social disorganisation

Causes: poverty, unemployment, rapid urbanisation, migration, family breakdown, war, natural disasters.

Effects: crime, substance abuse, neglect of children and elders, poor mental health, less access to care.

## Health effects

People in disorganised communities may live in unsafe housing, eat poorly, delay treatment and miss follow-up. Nurses should assess social support and link patients with welfare services.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Assess the home situation** — ask who lives with the patient and who can help after discharge.
- **Spot neglect** — poor hygiene, missed appointments or untreated illness may signal family breakdown.
- **Link to services** — tell patients about welfare schemes, shelters and counselling.
- **Protect vulnerable people** — children, older adults and people with disability need extra watch.
- **Report abuse** — if you suspect abuse, follow hospital policy and inform the right authority.
