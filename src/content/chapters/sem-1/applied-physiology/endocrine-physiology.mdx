## In simple words

The endocrine system uses hormones — chemical messengers released into the blood by ductless glands — to control metabolism, growth, stress response, reproduction and fluid balance.

Hormone levels are kept steady by **negative feedback**: a rising hormone level switches off its own stimulus. For example, high blood thyroxine tells the pituitary to reduce TSH.

## Key glands and hormones

| Gland | Hormone | Main action |
|---|---|---|
| Pituitary (front) | Growth hormone | Body growth |
| Pituitary (front) | TSH | Stimulates the thyroid |
| Pituitary (front) | ACTH | Stimulates the adrenal cortex |
| Pituitary (back) | ADH | Water retention by kidneys |
| Pituitary (back) | Oxytocin | Labour contractions, milk ejection |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine (T4, T3) | Sets metabolic rate |
| Parathyroid | Parathormone | Raises blood calcium |
| Adrenal cortex | Cortisol | Stress response, anti-inflammatory |
| Adrenal cortex | Aldosterone | Salt and water balance |
| Adrenal medulla | Adrenaline | Fight or flight |
| Pancreas | Insulin | Lowers blood glucose |
| Pancreas | Glucagon | Raises blood glucose |

The **hypothalamus** links the nervous and endocrine systems and controls the pituitary.

**Insulin** moves glucose into cells and lowers blood glucose. **Glucagon** makes the liver release stored glucose and raises blood glucose. They act like a thermostat to keep blood glucose steady.

  **Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon raises it.** Insulin is the only hormone that lowers blood glucose.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Diabetes care** — check blood glucose, give insulin as prescribed, recognise hypoglycaemia (sweating, tremor, confusion).
- **Thyroid illness** — watch for weight change, heat/cold intolerance and pulse changes.
- **Steroid therapy** — never stop suddenly; the adrenal cortex may be suppressed.
- **SIADH** — ADH excess causes water retention and low sodium; restrict fluids as ordered.
- **Patient teaching** — take hormone medicines at the same time each day.

## 60-second revision

- Hypothalamus controls pituitary.
- TSH → thyroid; ACTH → adrenal cortex.
- Thyroxine = metabolic rate.
- Insulin lowers glucose; glucagon raises it.
- ADH = water; oxytocin = labour/milk.
- Negative feedback keeps levels steady.
