## In simple words

Bone is living tissue that grows, repairs itself and stores calcium. Muscle contracts by sliding filaments past each other, pulling on bones through tendons. Joints allow movement in different amounts depending on their type.

Calcium and vitamin D are essential for strong bones. Weight-bearing exercise keeps bones dense.

## Bone physiology

Bone is constantly remodelled:
- **Osteoblasts** build new bone.
- **Osteoclasts** break down old bone.
- **Osteocytes** are mature bone cells.

Fracture repair happens in stages: bleeding forms a **haematoma**, a soft **callus** forms, then hard bone replaces it. Healing takes weeks to months and needs good blood supply, nutrition and immobilisation.

A long bone grows in length at the **epiphyseal plate** (growth plate). Growth stops when the plate closes after puberty.

## Muscle contraction

Skeletal muscle has **actin** (thin) and **myosin** (thick) filaments. When stimulated, myosin heads pull actin filaments inward — the **sliding filament theory**. This shortens the muscle and pulls the bone.

Energy comes from **ATP**. Calcium released inside the muscle cell triggers contraction; when calcium is pumped back, the muscle relaxes.

## Joint movements

At synovial joints:

| Movement | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Flexion | Bending, angle decreases | Bending the elbow |
| Extension | Straightening, angle increases | Straightening the elbow |
| Abduction | Moving away from midline | Raising arm sideways |
| Adduction | Moving towards midline | Lowering arm to side |
| Rotation | Turning around an axis | Turning the head |
| Circumduction | Circular movement | Swinging the arm in a circle |
| Supination | Palm turning up | Holding soup in the hand |
| Pronation | Palm turning down | Pouring from a cup |

Muscles work in pairs: an **agonist** contracts while the **antagonist** relaxes. Biceps flexes the elbow; triceps extends it.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Fracture** — immobilise the limb, check distal pulse and movement, and report pain or deformity.
- **Immobility** — turn the patient every 2 hours; do range-of-motion exercises to prevent contractures.
- **Osteoporosis** — encourage calcium-rich food, vitamin D and safe weight-bearing activity; fall precautions.
- **After joint replacement** — follow hip or knee precautions (no bending past 90°, no leg crossing).
- **Pain** — assess site, type and severity; give analgesia as prescribed.

## 60-second revision

- Osteoblasts build, osteoclasts break down.
- Growth plate closes after puberty.
- Fracture: haematoma → soft callus → hard bone.
- Actin + myosin slide; ATP = energy; Ca²⁺ triggers.
- Flexion bends; extension straightens.
- Abduction away; adduction towards.
- Tendons muscle→bone; ligaments bone→bone.
