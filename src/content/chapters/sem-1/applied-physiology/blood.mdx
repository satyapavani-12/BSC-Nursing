## In simple words

Blood is a connective tissue that carries oxygen, food, hormones and waste around the body, defends against infection and stops bleeding after injury. About 55% of blood is plasma and 45% is formed elements (cells and platelets).

An adult has about **5 litres of blood**. Blood has a slightly alkaline pH of about 7.35–7.45.

## Components of blood

| Component | Percentage | Main function |
|---|---|---|
| Plasma | About 55% | Carries cells, nutrients, hormones, waste and proteins |
| Red cells (erythrocytes) | About 45% of cells | Carry oxygen using haemoglobin |
| White cells (leukocytes) | Less than 1% | Defend against infection |
| Platelets (thrombocytes) | Less than 1% | Help blood clot |

Normal adult values:
- Haemoglobin: male 13–17 g/dL, female 12–16 g/dL
- Red cell count: about 4.5–5.5 million/mm³
- White cell count: 4,000–11,000/mm³
- Platelets: 150,000–450,000/mm³
- Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR): male 0–15 mm/hr, female 0–20 mm/hr

## Blood groups

The **ABO system** depends on antigens on red cells:

| Group | Antigen on red cells | Antibody in plasma |
|---|---|---|
| A | A | Anti-B |
| B | B | Anti-A |
| AB | A and B | None |
| O | None | Anti-A and anti-B |

The **Rh system** is based on the D antigen. Rh-positive has it; Rh-negative does not. Rh matters in pregnancy and transfusion.

Group O is the **universal donor** for red cells; group AB is the **universal recipient**.

## Clotting

When a vessel is injured:
1. Blood vessels constrict.
2. Platelets stick to the site and form a plug.
3. Clotting factors activate fibrinogen into fibrin threads.
4. Fibrin traps blood cells to form a clot.

Vitamin K is needed to make several clotting factors. Calcium is also essential. Clotting is kept inside the vessel by natural anticoagulants such as heparin.

<Callout type="must" title="Transfusion rule">
  **Give the correct blood group.** Group O can give to anyone; group AB can receive from anyone. Rh-negative must not receive Rh-positive blood.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Blood transfusion** — check the prescription, patient identity and blood group with two people; watch for fever, rash or back pain during the first 15 minutes.
- **Blood samples** — label at the bedside, use the correct tube, send promptly.
- **Anaemia** — watch for pallor, fatigue and breathlessness; report low haemoglobin.
- **Bleeding** — apply direct pressure, elevate the limb, note the time and amount.
- **IV fluids** — know which solutions are isotonic; never add drugs to blood.

## 60-second revision

- Blood: 55% plasma, 45% cells and platelets.
- RBC = oxygen; WBC = infection; platelets = clot.
- Hb: male 13–17, female 12–16 g/dL.
- ABO groups A, B, AB, O.
- O gives all; AB takes all.
- Rh D antigen; match Rh in transfusion and pregnancy.
- Platelets + fibrinogen → clot.
