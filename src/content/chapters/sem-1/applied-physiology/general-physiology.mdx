## In simple words

Every patient you care for is a collection of about 30 trillion cells, and each cell is a tiny factory surrounded by a fence. The fence is the **cell membrane**. It decides what comes in and what goes out — water, oxygen, glucose, sodium, waste. If the fence fails, the cell swells, stops working and dies.

Inside the cell, the **nucleus** holds DNA and gives orders. **Mitochondria** burn glucose with oxygen to make **ATP**, the cell’s energy currency. **Ribosomes** build proteins, the **endoplasmic reticulum** folds and ships them, and the **Golgi apparatus** packs them for delivery. **Lysosomes** are the recycling bins.

The body also keeps a steady internal state called **homeostasis**: temperature near 37 °C, blood pH 7.35–7.45, glucose 70–110 mg/dL, and fluid balance within tight limits. Every illness is, in one way or another, a failure of homeostasis — and every nursing action aims to restore it.

## The cell membrane and its transport

The membrane is a **phospholipid bilayer**: water-loving heads face outwards, fatty tails face inwards. Proteins float within it as channels, carriers and pumps.

| Type | Energy needed | Direction | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple diffusion | No | High to low | O₂, CO₂, water |
| Facilitated diffusion | No | High to low, via protein | Glucose into muscle |
| Osmosis | No | Water toward more solute | Water reabsorption in kidney |
| Primary active transport | Yes (ATP) | Low to high | Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump |
| Secondary active transport | Yes (indirect) | Low to high | Glucose with Na⁺ in gut |
| Endocytosis / exocytosis | Yes | Into / out of cell | Engulfing bacteria, releasing insulin |

The **sodium–potassium pump** is the single most examinable transport protein. For each ATP used, it pushes **3 Na⁺ out** and pulls **2 K⁺ in**. This keeps sodium high outside and potassium high inside, and it uses up to 30% of the body’s resting energy.

## Membrane potential and the action potential

All cells have a **resting membrane potential** of about **−70 mV** inside, because potassium leaks out and the pump keeps sodium outside. When a stimulus reaches the threshold of about **−55 mV**, sodium channels snap open, sodium floods in, and the inside becomes positive (**+30 mV**). This is **depolarisation**. Then potassium channels open, potassium leaves, and the cell returns to rest (**repolarisation**).

That brief electrical signal is the language of nerve and muscle. In the heart it produces the ECG; in nerve it carries pain, touch and movement.

## Fluid compartments and homeostasis

| Compartment | Share of body weight | Volume in a 70 kg adult |
|---|---|---|
| Total body water | 60% | about 42 L |
| Intracellular fluid | 40% | about 28 L |
| Extracellular fluid | 20% | about 14 L |
| Interstitial fluid | 15% | about 10.5 L |
| Plasma | 5% | about 3.5 L |

Homeostasis is maintained by **negative feedback**: a sensor detects change, a control centre (often the hypothalamus) compares it with the set point, and an effector reverses the change. Insulin lowering glucose after a meal is the classic example. **Positive feedback** is rare and short-lived — oxytocin in labour and clotting are the two examples to quote.

<Callout type="must" title="Core concept">
  Structure follows function at every level. If a cell membrane loses its pump, sodium and water enter, the cell swells, and the tissue stops working. Oedema and cell death begin here.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Fluid therapy:** isotonic fluids (0.9% saline, Ringer’s lactate) do not shift water into cells; hypotonic fluids do, and can cause cerebral swelling.
- **Oedema care:** in heart failure and low albumin, water leaks into the interstitium — hence daily weights are more reliable than a visual check.
- **Electrolyte monitoring:** the Na⁺/K⁺ pump explains why potassium shifts into cells after insulin and why serum K⁺ can fall dangerously fast.
- **Wound healing:** protein, oxygen, vitamin C and zinc are needed for collagen and new cell formation.
- **Explaining tests:** patients understand “cells need oxygen to make energy” better than “mitochondrial respiration”.
- **Recognising shock:** when ATP fails, the pump fails, cells swell and organs shut down in sequence.

## 60-second revision

- Membrane = phospholipid bilayer plus proteins; it is selectively permeable.
- Simple and facilitated diffusion need no ATP; active transport does.
- Na⁺/K⁺ pump: 3 sodium out, 2 potassium in, one ATP.
- Resting potential −70 mV, threshold −55 mV, peak +30 mV.
- Water is 60% of body weight; ICF 40%, ECF 20%, plasma 5%.
- Homeostasis works mainly by negative feedback.
- Mitochondria = ATP; ribosomes = protein; lysosomes = digestion.
