## In simple words

The kidneys filter the blood, remove waste, and control water, salt and acid–base balance. Each kidney has about one million nephrons, and each nephron forms urine by three steps:

1. **Filtration** — blood is filtered in the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule.
2. **Reabsorption** — useful substances (glucose, amino acids, most water and salts) are taken back into the blood.
3. **Secretion** — extra wastes and ions are added to the tubule from the blood.

Normal urine is about **95% water and 5% wastes**. Normal output is **0.5–1 mL/kg/hour**; urine specific gravity is about **1.010–1.025**.

## The nephron

Each nephron has a glomerulus inside Bowman's capsule, then the proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle, distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct.

- **Proximal tubule** — reabsorbs nearly all glucose, amino acids and most water and sodium.
- **Loop of Henle** — concentrates the urine.
- **Distal tubule** — fine-tunes sodium, potassium and pH under hormone control.
- **Collecting duct** — final water adjustment under ADH; carries urine to the renal pelvis.

## Hormonal control

- **ADH (antidiuretic hormone)** — makes the collecting duct more permeable to water; more water reabsorbed, urine concentrated. Released when the body is dehydrated.
- **Aldosterone** — makes the kidney reabsorb sodium and water and excrete potassium. Controls blood pressure and salt balance.
- **Renin** — released by the kidney when blood pressure falls; starts the renin–angiotensin system that raises blood pressure.

The kidneys also activate vitamin D and make erythropoietin, which stimulates red cell production.

<Callout type="must" title="Urine output watch">
  **Normal urine output is at least 0.5 mL/kg/hour.** For a 60 kg adult that is 30 mL/hour. Below this is oliguria — measure accurately and report.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Measure output** — empty and record the catheter bag each shift; never guess.
- **Oliguria** — can mean dehydration, shock or kidney failure; report low output with the patient's weight.
- **Fluid balance chart** — record all intake and output; investigate a large gap.
- **Specific gravity** — very dilute or very concentrated urine can indicate kidney or hormone problems.
- **Catheter care** — keep the drainage bag below bladder level and maintain asepsis.

## 60-second revision

- Nephron: glomerulus → Bowman's capsule → tubules → collecting duct.
- Filtration → reabsorption → secretion.
- Output: 0.5–1 mL/kg/hour; 30 mL/hour for 60 kg.
- ADH concentrates urine; aldosterone retains sodium.
- Kidneys activate vitamin D and make erythropoietin.
