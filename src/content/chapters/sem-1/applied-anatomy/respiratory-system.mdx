## In simple words

Breathing is not the same as respiration. **Breathing** (ventilation) moves air in and out of the lungs. **Respiration** is the whole process: taking in oxygen, using it in cells, and removing carbon dioxide.

Air travels along one road:

**Nose → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli**

The lungs do not have muscles of their own. They expand because the **diaphragm** and intercostal muscles pull the chest open, and air rushes in.

## The organs, one by one

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Nose | Warms, moistens and filters air; detects smell |
| Pharynx | Common passage for air and food (the “throat”) |
| Larynx | Voice box; guards the airway during swallowing |
| Trachea | Windpipe; C-shaped cartilage rings keep it open |
| Bronchi | Right and left main branches entering each lung |
| Bronchioles | Smaller branches with smooth muscle; can narrow in asthma |
| Alveoli | Tiny air sacs; the only place where gas exchange happens |
| Pleura | Double membrane around the lung; fluid between layers reduces friction |

## How breathing works

1. The diaphragm contracts and flattens, and the ribs move up and out.
2. The chest cavity gets bigger, so pressure inside falls.
3. Air rushes in (inspiration). This is active.
4. The diaphragm relaxes, the chest gets smaller, and air flows out (expiration). This is passive at rest.

## Numbers you must remember

| Measurement | Normal adult value |
|---|---|
| Respiratory rate | 12–20 breaths per minute |
| Tidal volume (air per normal breath) | about 500 mL |
| Vital capacity | about 4,500–5,000 mL |
| Oxygen in room air | 21% |
| Oxygen carried by haemoglobin | about 97% saturation |
| Carbon dioxide carried as bicarbonate | about 70% |

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Positioning:** sitting upright (Fowler’s) lets the diaphragm move down and improves breathing.
- **Oxygen therapy:** humidify oxygen, check the prescription, watch for dryness of the nose.
- **Suctioning:** clear secretions from the mouth, nose or trachea when the patient cannot cough.
- **Chest physiotherapy:** percussion, vibration and postural drainage move mucus toward the larger airways.
- **Incentive spirometer:** encourages deep breathing after surgery to prevent collapse of alveoli (atelectasis).

## 60-second revision

- Air path: nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.
- Right lung 3 lobes; left lung 2 lobes.
- Alveoli = gas exchange. O₂ in, CO₂ out.
- Diaphragm contracts → air in. Relaxes → air out.
- Pleura = two layers with fluid; reduces friction.
- Normal adult respiratory rate 12–20/min.
