## In simple words

The musculoskeletal system gives the body its shape, supports it and allows movement. Bones act as levers, joints act as hinges, and muscles pull on bones to move them.

Bone is living tissue — constantly broken down and rebuilt. A break in a bone is called a **fracture**. Bones store calcium and make blood cells in the red bone marrow.

## Bones and their types

| Shape | Examples | Main feature |
|---|---|---|
| Long | Humerus, femur, radius | Act as levers for movement |
| Short | Carpals, tarsals | Allow gliding movement |
| Flat | Skull, scapula, ribs | Protect organs; wide surface |
| Irregular | Vertebrae, pelvis | Complex shape, protection |
| Sesamoid | Patella | Embedded in tendons |

A long bone has a **diaphysis** (shaft) and **epiphyses** (ends). The shaft contains a central **medullary cavity** with marrow, covered by compact bone outside and spongy bone inside.

Bone cells: **osteoblasts** build bone; **osteoclasts** break bone down; **osteocytes** are mature bone cells.

## Joints

Joints are classified by how much they move:

| Type | Movement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Fibrous | None | Skull sutures |
| Cartilaginous | Slight | Vertebral discs, pubic symphysis |
| Synovial | Free | Knee, shoulder, hip, elbow |

Common synovial joints:
- **Hinge** — knee, elbow, ankle (one plane).
- **Ball-and-socket** — shoulder, hip (many planes).
- **Pivot** — atlas and axis (rotation).
- **Gliding** — carpals, tarsals (small sliding).
- **Saddle** — thumb (opposition).

## Muscles

Three types:

| Type | Where found | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Skeletal | Attached to bones | Voluntary |
| Cardiac | Heart | Involuntary |
| Smooth | Gut, blood vessels, bladder | Involuntary |

Skeletal muscle attaches to bone by a **tendon**. A muscle can only pull, never push — so muscles work in pairs: an **agonist** contracts while the **antagonist** relaxes. Example: biceps contracts, triceps relaxes, to flex the elbow.

  **Skeletal muscle can only pull — never push.** Muscles work in pairs: one contracts (agonist) while the other relaxes (antagonist).
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Fracture care** — check distal pulse, colour, warmth and movement below the injury; report any loss immediately.
- **Immobility** — turn bedridden patients every 2 hours to prevent pressure ulcers and deep vein thrombosis.
- **Range-of-motion exercises** — move each joint gently through its full range to prevent contractures.
- **Fall prevention** — weak muscles and poor balance increase fall risk, especially in older adults.
- **After joint replacement** — follow hip precautions: no bending past 90°, no crossing legs, no twisting.

## 60-second revision

- Bone shapes: long, short, flat, irregular, sesamoid.
- Osteoblasts build, osteoclasts break down.
- Joints: fibrous none, cartilaginous slight, synovial free.
- Hinge = knee/elbow; ball-and-socket = shoulder/hip.
- Muscle types: skeletal voluntary; cardiac and smooth involuntary.
- Tendons muscle→bone; ligaments bone→bone.
- Muscles only pull — work in pairs.
