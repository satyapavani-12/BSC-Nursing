## In simple words

The urinary system removes waste products and excess water and salts from the blood. The two kidneys filter the blood, make urine, and help control blood pressure and pH. The ureters carry urine to the bladder, which stores it until the urethra releases it.

A healthy adult makes about **1–2 litres of urine per day**, and normal urine output is **0.5–1 mL per kg per hour**.

## The urinary tract

| Organ | Position and function |
|---|---|
| Kidneys | Two bean-shaped organs, one each side of the spine at T12–L3; left kidney slightly higher |
| Ureters | Two tubes, about 25–30 cm, carry urine by peristalsis from kidney to bladder |
| Urinary bladder | Hollow muscular sac in the pelvis; stores urine (300–500 mL comfortably) |
| Urethra | Tube from bladder to outside; much shorter in females than males |

The kidneys sit behind the peritoneum (**retroperitoneal**). The right kidney is lower because the liver pushes it down.

## Inside the kidney

- **Cortex** — outer region where filtration begins.
- **Medulla** — inner region with pyramids that drain into the renal pelvis.
- **Renal pelvis** — funnel that collects urine into the ureter.
- **Hilum** — where the renal artery, vein and ureter enter or leave.

The **nephron** is the working unit. About one million nephrons are in each kidney. Each nephron has:

1. **Glomerulus** — a knot of capillaries inside Bowman's capsule.
2. **Bowman's capsule** — collects the filtrate.
3. **Proximal convoluted tubule** — reabsorbs most water, glucose and salts.
4. **Loop of Henle** — concentrates the urine.
5. **Distal convoluted tubule** — fine-tunes salts and pH.
6. **Collecting duct** — carries urine to the renal pelvis.

Urine forms by **filtration → reabsorption → secretion**. About 180 litres are filtered daily, but only about 1–2 litres leave as urine.

<Callout type="must" title="Watch the urine output">
  **Normal urine output is at least 0.5 mL/kg/hour.** Below that is oliguria — report it. A 60 kg adult should pass at least 30 mL per hour.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Measure urine** — empty and record the catheter bag each shift; never guess.
- **Oliguria** — less than 0.5 mL/kg/hour can mean dehydration, shock or kidney failure.
- **Catheter care** — keep the bag below bladder level, never clamp unless ordered, maintain asepsis.
- **Fluid balance chart** — record all intake and output; report a large difference.
- **Watch for retention** — lower abdominal pain and a palpable bladder after surgery need review.

## 60-second revision

- Tract: kidneys → ureters → bladder → urethra.
- Kidneys at T12–L3, retroperitoneal; right kidney lower.
- Output: 0.5–1 mL/kg/hour; 1–2 L/day.
- Nephron = glomerulus + Bowman’s capsule + tubules + collecting duct.
- Filtration → reabsorption → secretion.
- Bladder holds 300–500 mL.
- Female urethra shorter than male.
