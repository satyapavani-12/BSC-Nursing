## In simple words

Digestion breaks large food molecules into small ones that can pass into the blood. It happens along one long tube — about 9 metres from mouth to anus — helped by three organs: the **liver**, **gall bladder** and **pancreas**.

Food is pushed along by **peristalsis**, wave-like contractions of smooth muscle. The inner lining of the small intestine has finger-like folds called **villi**, which greatly increase the surface for absorption.

## The tract, part by part

| Organ | Main function |
|---|---|
| Mouth | Chewing; saliva amylase starts starch digestion |
| Oesophagus | Carries food to the stomach by peristalsis |
| Stomach | Stores and churns food; acid and pepsin start protein digestion |
| Duodenum | Receives bile and pancreatic juice; main chemical digestion |
| Jejunum | Main absorption of nutrients |
| Ileum | Absorbs vitamin B12, bile salts and remaining nutrients |
| Large intestine | Absorbs water and salts; forms faeces; bacteria make vitamin K |
| Rectum and anus | Stores and expels faeces |

The stomach wall has three muscle layers, which allow strong churning. Its lining secretes hydrochloric acid and pepsinogen, and mucus protects the wall from acid.

The gut wall has four layers in order: **mucosa → submucosa → muscularis → serosa**.

## Helper organs

- **Liver** — the largest gland. Makes bile, stores glycogen, detoxifies drugs and alcohol, makes plasma proteins and clotting factors.
- **Gall bladder** — stores and concentrates bile; squeezes it into the duodenum when fatty food arrives.
- **Pancreas** — sends amylase, lipase and trypsin into the duodenum; its endocrine part makes insulin and glucagon.

<Callout type="must" title="The one line that wins marks">
  Most digestion and absorption happens in the **small intestine**, especially the duodenum and jejunum. The stomach mainly stores, churns and starts protein digestion.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Before surgery** — keep the patient nil by mouth as ordered, because food in the stomach risks aspiration.
- **Vomiting** — turn the patient to the side, measure and record the amount, and watch for dehydration.
- **Liver disease** — watch for jaundice, bleeding and confusion, and limit protein only as prescribed.
- **Bowel sounds** — listen in all four quadrants; silence after surgery can mean paralytic ileus.
- **Constipation** — fibre, fluids and walking improve peristalsis.

## 60-second revision

- Tube: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus.
- Small intestine = main absorption; villi increase surface area.
- Liver = bile, glycogen, detox, proteins, clotting factors.
- Gall bladder stores bile; pancreas sends enzymes.
- Stomach acid kills germs and starts protein digestion.
- Gut wall: mucosa, submucosa, muscularis, serosa.
- Peristalsis moves food; ileum absorbs B12.
