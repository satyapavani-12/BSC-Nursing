## In simple words

Endocrine glands have no ducts. They pour hormones straight into the blood, and the blood carries them to target cells. A hormone is a chemical messenger that switches a body process on or off.

Hormone levels are kept steady by **negative feedback**: when the level of a hormone rises, it switches off its own production. For example, high thyroid hormone tells the pituitary to stop making TSH.

## The glands and their hormones

| Gland | Position | Main hormone | Main action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hypothalamus | Base of brain | Releasing hormones | Controls the pituitary |
| Pituitary (anterior) | Base of brain | GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin | Growth and controls other glands |
| Pituitary (posterior) | Base of brain | ADH, oxytocin | Water balance; labour and milk ejection |
| Thyroid | Front of neck | Thyroxine (T4, T3) | Controls metabolic rate |
| Parathyroid | Behind thyroid | Parathormone | Raises blood calcium |
| Adrenal cortex | On the kidney | Cortisol, aldosterone | Stress response; salt and water balance |
| Adrenal medulla | On the kidney | Adrenaline, noradrenaline | Fight or flight |
| Pancreas (islets) | Behind the stomach | Insulin, glucagon | Lowers and raises blood glucose |
| Ovaries | Pelvis | Oestrogen, progesterone | Female sex characters, menstruation |
| Testes | Scrotum | Testosterone | Male sex characters, sperm formation |
| Thymus | Upper chest | Thymosin | Maturation of T lymphocytes |
| Pineal | Brain | Melatonin | Sleep–wake rhythm |

  **Insulin lowers blood glucose; glucagon raises it.** Both come from the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas. Insulin is the only hormone that lowers blood glucose.
</Callout>

## Glands you must be able to describe

- **Pituitary** — called the master gland because its hormones control the thyroid, adrenal cortex and gonads. It sits in the sella turcica.
- **Thyroid** — needs iodine to make thyroxine. Too much gives weight loss, fast pulse and heat intolerance; too little gives weight gain, slow pulse and cold intolerance.
- **Parathyroid** — the only hormone that raises blood calcium. It draws calcium from bone and increases gut absorption.
- **Adrenal cortex** — cortisol helps the body cope with stress and suppresses inflammation; aldosterone keeps sodium and water in and potassium out.
- **Pancreas** — insulin moves glucose into cells; lack of it causes diabetes mellitus.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Diabetes care** — check glucose, give insulin as prescribed, watch for hypoglycaemia (sweating, trembling, confusion).
- **Thyroid surgery** — keep a tracheostomy set nearby and watch for hoarseness or tetany from parathyroid damage.
- **Steroid therapy** — never stop suddenly; the adrenal gland may have shrunk.
- **Fluid balance** — ADH retains water; aldosterone retains sodium.
- **Patient teaching** — explain that hormone tablets must be taken at the same time each day.

## 60-second revision

- Ductless glands → hormones → blood → target cells.
- Pituitary controls thyroid, adrenal cortex and gonads.
- Thyroid = metabolism; needs iodine.
- Parathormone raises calcium.
- Insulin lowers glucose; glucagon raises it.
- Cortisol = stress; aldosterone = salt and water.
- Negative feedback keeps levels steady.
