## In simple words

The heart is a muscular pump about the size of a fist, lying behind the sternum with its apex pointing to the left. It pushes blood through two loops:

- **Pulmonary circulation** — heart → lungs → heart (picks up oxygen).
- **Systemic circulation** — heart → body → heart (delivers oxygen and food).

Blood never flows backwards because four valves act as one-way doors.

## Inside the heart

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Right atrium | Receives deoxygenated blood from the body |
| Right ventricle | Pumps blood to the lungs |
| Left atrium | Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs |
| Left ventricle | Pumps blood to the whole body; thickest wall |
| Tricuspid valve | Between right atrium and ventricle |
| Pulmonary valve | Between right ventricle and pulmonary artery |
| Mitral (bicuspid) valve | Between left atrium and ventricle |
| Aortic valve | Between left ventricle and aorta |

The heart wall has three layers: **endocardium** (inner lining), **myocardium** (muscle), **pericardium** (outer sac with fluid). The left ventricle is thickest because it pumps against the highest pressure.

The **coronary arteries** supply the heart muscle itself. They branch from the aorta just above the aortic valve.

## Blood vessels

- **Arteries** — carry blood away from the heart; thick elastic walls; high pressure; no valves.
- **Arterioles** — small arteries that control flow into capillaries.
- **Capillaries** — one-cell-thick walls; exchange of gases, water and nutrients.
- **Venules and veins** — thin walls, low pressure, valves to stop backflow. Skeletal muscle squeezes them.

## Lymphatic system

Lymph is tissue fluid that has not returned through the capillaries. It is collected by lymph capillaries, filtered through **lymph nodes**, and returned to the blood through the thoracic duct into the left subclavian vein.

Lymph nodes contain lymphocytes and macrophages. Main groups: cervical, axillary, mediastinal, mesenteric, inguinal. The **spleen** filters blood, and the **thymus** matures T lymphocytes.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Pulse and BP** — the arterial wave tells you how well the heart is pumping.
- **Oedema** — right heart failure backs blood into the veins, causing ankle swelling and a raised JVP.
- **Breathlessness** — left heart failure backs blood into the lungs, causing pulmonary oedema.
- **Lymph nodes** — palpate cervical, axillary and inguinal nodes; a hard, fixed node needs medical review.
- **Post-operative leg care** — early walking and leg exercises prevent deep vein thrombosis.

## 60-second revision

- Right heart = lungs; left heart = body.
- Left ventricle thickest.
- Valves: tricuspid, pulmonary, mitral, aortic.
- Arteries away, veins back, capillaries exchange.
- Coronary arteries feed the heart muscle.
- Lymph → nodes → thoracic duct → left subclavian vein.
- Spleen filters blood; thymus matures T cells.
