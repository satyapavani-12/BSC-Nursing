## In simple words

The nurse–patient relationship is a professional, trusting relationship built to help the patient meet health goals. It is different from a friendship — it has boundaries and a purpose.

Communication is the heart of this relationship. Good communication helps assessment, teaching and comfort.

## Phases of the relationship (Peplau)

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Pre-interaction | Nurse prepares before meeting the patient |
| Orientation | Nurse and patient meet; roles and goals are set |
| Working | Patient's needs are identified and met |
| Termination | Relationship ends when goals are met |

## Therapeutic communication techniques

- Active listening — focus fully on the patient.
- Open questions — "How does the pain feel?"
- Silence — give the patient time to think.
- Reflection — "You seem worried about the surgery."
- Clarification — "I am not sure I understand; can you say more?"

## Barriers to communication

- Language difference, noise, lack of time.
- Patient fear, pain or unconsciousness.
- Nurse being rushed or judgmental.
- Medical jargon the patient does not understand.

<Callout type="must" title="Professional boundaries">
  **The nurse–patient relationship is professional, not a friendship.** It has boundaries, a purpose and ends when goals are met.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Build trust** — introduce yourself, keep promises, be honest.
- **Use therapeutic techniques** — listen, ask open questions, reflect feelings.
- **Watch barriers** — use an interpreter, reduce noise, avoid jargon.
- **Respect privacy** — talk where others cannot overhear.
- **End well** — summarise what was achieved and say goodbye clearly.

- Relationship = professional, trusting, goal-directed.

- Boundaries: not a friendship.
