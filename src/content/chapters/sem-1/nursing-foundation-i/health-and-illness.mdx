## In simple words

Health is not just the absence of disease. The World Health Organization defines health as complete physical, mental and social well-being.

Illness is a state in which a person's normal functioning is reduced — physically, mentally or socially. A person can have a disease but still feel healthy, or feel ill without a diagnosed disease.

Health and illness lie on a continuum — people move along it every day depending on their situation.

## Dimensions of health

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical | Body works well; free from disease |
| Mental | Able to think, feel and cope |
| Social | Able to relate to others and society |
| Spiritual | Purpose and meaning in life |

## Health–illness continuum

High-level wellness ← → Good health ← → Illness ← → Death

A person moves up and down this line. Good nursing helps a person move toward wellness.

## Factors affecting health

- Biology and genes
- Lifestyle — diet, exercise, sleep, smoking
- Environment — air, water, housing
- Health-care services
- Socioeconomic conditions — income, education

## Maslow's needs in health

Nurses use Maslow's hierarchy to prioritise care: physiological needs (air, food, water) come first, then safety, then emotional needs.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Assess the whole person** — body, mind, social life and spirit, not only the disease.
- **See where on the continuum** — is the patient moving toward wellness or away from it?
- **Prioritise with Maslow** — airway, breathing and circulation first; then safety and comfort.
- **Health teaching** — help the patient move toward wellness through lifestyle change.
- **Respect the illness experience** — two people with the same disease may feel very different.

## 60-second revision

- Health = physical, mental, social well-being (WHO).
- Illness = reduced functioning.
- Continuum: wellness → illness → death.
- Dimensions: physical, mental, social, spiritual.
- Maslow: physiological needs first.
