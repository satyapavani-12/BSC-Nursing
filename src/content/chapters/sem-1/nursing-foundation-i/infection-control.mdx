## In simple words

Infection control means preventing germs from spreading to patients, staff and visitors. The chain of infection shows how infection spreads — and where we can break it.

Asepsis means being free from germs. Medical asepsis (clean technique) reduces germs; surgical asepsis (sterile technique) removes all germs.

## Chain of infection

| Link | Meaning | How to break it |
|---|---|---|
| Infectious agent | The germ | Kill the germ |
| Reservoir | Where it lives | Clean the source |
| Portal of exit | How it leaves | Cover coughs, dispose of waste |
| Mode of transmission | How it spreads | Hand hygiene, PPE |
| Portal of entry | How it enters | Protect wounds, sterile technique |
| Susceptible host | The person at risk | Vaccinate, good nutrition |

## Hand hygiene

The single most important way to prevent infection. Wash with soap and water when hands are visibly dirty; use alcohol-based hand rub otherwise.

Five moments of hand hygiene:
1. Before touching a patient.
2. Before a clean/aseptic procedure.
3. After body fluid exposure risk.
4. After touching a patient.
5. After touching patient surroundings.

## Standard precautions & PPE

Treat every patient's blood and body fluids as potentially infectious. Use PPE:
- Gloves — for contact with blood/body fluids.
- Mask — protects nose and mouth.
- Gown — protects clothing.
- Goggles/face shield — protects eyes.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Hand hygiene** — before and after every patient contact and before clean procedures.
- **PPE** — wear gloves, mask and gown as needed; remove carefully and dispose safely.
- **Sharps safety** — never recap needles; drop in a puncture-proof container.
- **Waste segregation** — colour-coded bins; infectious waste goes in yellow bags.
- **Isolation** — follow the precautions on the isolation sign outside the room.
