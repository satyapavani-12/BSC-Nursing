## In simple words

Documentation is writing down what the nurse assessed, did and the patient's response. It is a legal record and a way to communicate with the whole health team.

Good documentation is accurate, complete, current and organised. If it is not written, it is not done.

## Purposes of records

- Communicate with the health team.
- Show the care given and the patient's response.
- Serve as a legal document.
- Help with audit, research and billing.
- Plan continuity of care.

## Methods of documentation

| Method | What it is |
|---|---|
| Narrative | Writing the story of care in paragraphs |
| SOAP | Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan |
| Problem-oriented | Listing problems, goals and progress |
| Charting by exception | Writing only abnormal or significant findings |

## Legal rules

- Write in ink, in black or blue.
- Date, time and sign every entry.
- Do not erase; cross out errors with one line and initial.
- Write facts, not opinions.
- Never chart ahead of time or for someone else.
- Record late entries as "late entry" with the actual time.

<Callout type="must" title="Legal rule">
  **If it is not written, it is not done.** Records are legal documents — write facts, date, time and sign every entry.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Nursing notes** — write after every procedure, observation or change in condition.
- **Incident reports** — record what happened, who was involved, and action taken.
- **Handover** — written notes help the next shift continue care safely.
- **Legal cases** — your chart may be used as evidence; keep it truthful.
- **Audit** — records show whether standards of care were met.

- Cross out errors with one line, initial and date.
