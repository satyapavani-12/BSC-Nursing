## In simple words

Communication is the exchange of information, ideas and feelings between people. It can be through words (verbal) or without words (non-verbal).

Good communication builds trust, helps assessment, and makes care safer. Poor communication causes errors, misunderstanding and complaints.

## Elements of communication

| Element | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sender | The person who sends the message | The nurse |
| Message | The information sent | "Take this medicine after food" |
| Channel | The way it is sent | Speaking, writing, phone |
| Receiver | The person who gets it | The patient |
| Feedback | The receiver's response | The patient nods or asks a question |
| Barrier | Anything that blocks understanding | Noise, language difference |

## Types of communication

- **Verbal** — spoken or written words.
- **Non-verbal** — body language, facial expression, touch, eye contact, posture.
- **Formal** — official records, reports, handover.
- **Informal** — casual talk with patients and colleagues.

Most meaning comes from non-verbal cues — tone, expression and gestures often matter more than words.

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Handover** — give clear, complete information to the next shift.
- **Patient teaching** — explain procedures and medicines in plain language.
- **Consent** — make sure the patient understands before agreeing.
- **Emotional support** — listen and respond to feelings, not just facts.
- **Documentation** — write clearly so the next person understands.

## 60-second revision

- Communication = exchange of information, ideas, feelings.
- Elements: sender → message → channel → receiver → feedback.
- Types: verbal, non-verbal, formal, informal.
- Barriers: noise, language, poor listening.
- Non-verbal carries much of the meaning.
