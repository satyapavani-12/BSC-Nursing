## In simple words

Writing in nursing means recording what you did, observed and planned. Records are legal documents — if it is not written, it is not done.

Good records are clear, accurate, dated, timed and signed. They protect the patient and the nurse.

## SOAP method

| Part | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| S – Subjective | What the patient says | "I have pain in my chest" |
| O – Objective | What you measure or see | "BP 150/90, pulse 96" |
| A – Assessment | Your conclusion | "Possible cardiac pain" |
| P – Plan | What you will do | "Inform doctor, give oxygen as ordered" |

## Legal rules for documentation

- Write in ink; never use pencil.
- Do not erase — draw one line through errors, initial and date.
- Write facts, not opinions ("patient cried" not "patient is difficult").
- Sign every entry with your name and designation.
- Record the time of every entry.
- Never write for someone else or pre-write ahead of time.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Nursing notes** — write after every important observation or procedure.
- **Incident reports** — record what happened, who was involved and what you did.
- **Handover notes** — give the next shift accurate, written information.
- **Discharge summaries** — write what the patient and family need to do at home.
- **Legal cases** — your record may be used in court; make it truthful and complete.

- Records are legal; if not written, not done.
- SOAP: Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan.
- Ink, date, time, sign every entry.
- Cross out errors with one line, initial and date.
- Facts not opinions.
