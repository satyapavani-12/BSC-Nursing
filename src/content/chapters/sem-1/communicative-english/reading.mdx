## In simple words

Reading is understanding written text. Nurses read charts, doctor's orders, medicine labels, reports and health education materials.

Different situations need different reading styles — skimming for the main idea, scanning for a fact, and careful reading for instructions.

## Reading styles

| Style | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Skimming | Read quickly for the main idea | Getting the gist of a report |
| Scanning | Look for a specific fact | Finding a medicine dose on a label |
| Intensive | Read carefully and fully | Reading a doctor's order or consent form |
| SQ3R | Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review | Studying a textbook chapter |

## SQ3R method

1. **Survey** — look at headings and pictures first.
2. **Question** — turn headings into questions.
3. **Read** — read to answer the questions.
4. **Recite** — say what you remember.
5. **Review** — go over it again later.

## Reading charts and labels safely

- Check the patient's name on every chart and label.
- Read medicine names, doses, routes and times carefully.
- Check the date and time of entries.
- If anything is unclear, ask — do not guess.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Doctor's orders** — read the full order before acting on it.
- **Medicine labels** — check name, dose and expiry before giving.
- **Patient charts** — read progress notes to know the patient's condition.
- **Health teaching** — read pamphlets aloud or explain them to patients who read poorly.
- **Reports** — skim for the main point, then read details if needed.

## 60-second revision

- Styles: skimming, scanning, intensive, SQ3R.
- Skim = main idea; scan = one fact; intensive = careful.
- SQ3R: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review.
- Labels: name, dose, route, time — check exactly.
- Unclear? Ask, don't guess.
