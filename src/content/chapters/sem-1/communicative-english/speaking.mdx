## In simple words

Speaking is sharing information through words. Clear speaking means using simple words, speaking at the right speed, with the right tone and pronunciation.

In nursing, how you say something matters as much as what you say. A calm, respectful tone helps a frightened patient feel safe.

## Features of clear speaking

| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| Simple words | Use words the patient knows |
| Right speed | Not too fast, not too slow |
| Clear pronunciation | Say each word correctly |
| Polite tone | Respectful and calm |
| Pauses | Give the patient time to understand |

## Effective conversation with a patient

- Greet the patient and introduce yourself.
- Explain what you are going to do before you do it.
- Ask open questions ("How does the pain feel?").
- Listen to the answer before speaking again.
- Thank the patient at the end.

## Tone and pronunciation

- A harsh tone can frighten a patient; a gentle tone calms them.
- Pronounce medicine names and instructions clearly — mistakes can cause errors.
- Avoid slang and jargon with patients.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Explaining procedures** — tell the patient what will happen, in simple words, before starting.
- **Giving instructions** — say how to take medicine, when to call for help, and what to expect.
- **Answering questions** — answer honestly; if you do not know, say so and find out.
- **Talking to colleagues** — speak clearly and respectfully during handover and rounds.
- **Comforting** — a gentle tone and patience reduce fear and pain.

## 60-second revision

- Clear speaking = simple words, right speed, clear pronunciation, polite tone, pauses.
- Tone matters — gentle calms, harsh frightens.
- Conversation: greet, introduce, explain, ask, listen.
- Open questions: how, what, when, where, why.
- Avoid slang and jargon with patients.
