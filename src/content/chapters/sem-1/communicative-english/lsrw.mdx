## In simple words

LSRW stands for Listening, Speaking, Reading and Writing — the four skills of using a language. Nurses need all four to understand patients, talk to colleagues, read charts and write records.

Good grammar makes writing clear and correct. Simple sentences with correct tenses and articles prevent mistakes.

## The four skills

| Skill | What it means | Nursing example |
|---|---|---|
| Listening | Hearing and understanding | Listening to a patient's symptoms |
| Speaking | Saying clearly | Explaining a procedure to a patient |
| Reading | Understanding written text | Reading a doctor's order or chart |
| Writing | Putting ideas on paper | Writing a nursing note or report |

## Basic grammar for nurses

- **Articles**: a, an (before consonants, before vowel sounds), the (specific).
- **Tenses**: present simple for habits ("I check BP every morning"), past for what happened ("She took medicine at 8").
- **Subject–verb agreement**: "The patient has fever" not "The patient have fever".
- **Prepositions**: "in the ward", "on the arm", "after food".

Avoid long, confusing sentences. Short, plain sentences are safer.

<Callout type="must" title="LSRW order matters">
  **We usually learn a language in this order: Listening → Speaking → Reading → Writing.** Listening comes first.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Listening** — hear the patient's full story before responding.
- **Speaking** — explain procedures in words the patient knows.
- **Reading** — read charts, medicine labels and doctor's orders carefully.
- **Writing** — write nursing notes that are complete, dated and signed.
- **Grammar** — correct grammar prevents misunderstanding in records and reports.

## 60-second revision

- LSRW = Listening, Speaking, Reading, Writing.
- Listening first; writing last.
- Nursing uses: symptoms, procedures, charts, notes.
- Grammar: articles, tenses, agreement, prepositions.
- Keep sentences short and plain.
