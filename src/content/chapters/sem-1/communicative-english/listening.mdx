## In simple words

Listening is not the same as hearing. Hearing is just sound reaching the ear; listening is paying attention and understanding.

Attentive listening means focusing fully on the speaker, showing you are listening, and checking that you understood. It makes patients feel respected and helps you gather accurate information.

## Types of listening

| Type | Meaning | Nursing example |
|---|---|---|
| Attentive | Fully focused | Listening to a patient's pain history |
| Casual | Relaxed, not detailed | Chatting during a break |
| Selective | Hearing only parts | Hearing only the words you expect |
| Empathic | Listening with feeling | Listening to a patient's fears |

## Barriers to listening

- Noise and interruptions.
- Thinking of your reply instead of listening.
- Prejudice or assumptions about the patient.
- Language difference.
- Being tired or rushed.

## How to listen well

- Face the patient and make eye contact.
- Do not interrupt.
- Use nods and small sounds ("mm-hmm").
- Repeat back what you heard ("So you mean the pain started last night?").
- Watch body language and feelings, not only words.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **History taking** — listen to the full story of symptoms before asking more.
- **Pain assessment** — listen to how the patient describes pain; do not assume.
- **Emotional distress** — a worried patient needs to be heard more than given advice.
- **Shift handover** — listen carefully so you do not miss important information.
- **Family talk** — relatives often know the patient's normal habits and concerns.

## 60-second revision

- Hearing = passive; listening = active attention.
- Attentive listening = focus fully.
- Types: attentive, casual, selective, empathic.
- Barriers: noise, reply-thinking, prejudice, language, rush.
- Listen well: face, don't interrupt, check, watch feelings.
