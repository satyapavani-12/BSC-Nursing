## In simple words

Psychological assessment gathers information about a person's thinking, feeling and behaviour to understand problems and plan help. It uses interviews, observation, questionnaires and standard tests.

Nurses do not diagnose mental illness — they observe, record, report and refer to the psychologist or psychiatrist. They also support patients through testing.

## Assessment methods

| Method | What it is |
|---|---|
| Interview | Talking to learn about history, feelings and problems |
| Observation | Watching behaviour, mood and appearance |
| Questionnaire | Standard written questions |
| Psychological test | Standard tool measuring a specific ability or trait |

## Common tests

- **IQ tests** — measure general intellectual ability (verbal and performance).
- **Personality tests** — measure traits such as anxiety, introversion or emotional stability.
- **Projective tests** — ambiguous stimuli (inkblots, pictures) reveal inner conflicts.
- **Neuropsychological tests** — memory, attention and brain function.

A good test is **reliable** (consistent results) and **valid** (measures what it claims).

## Nurse's role

- Observe and record behaviour accurately.
- Note mood, speech, sleep, appetite and social contact.
- Report changes to the doctor or psychologist.
- Support the patient before and after tests.
- Maintain confidentiality.

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Admission assessment** — note mood, behaviour, sleep and any psychiatric history.
- **Daily observation** — record changes in behaviour, speech or appearance.
- **Support during testing** — explain what will happen and reduce anxiety.
- **Confidentiality** — share information only with the care team and with consent.
- **Referral** — tell the doctor about concerning signs such as withdrawal, agitation or hopelessness.
