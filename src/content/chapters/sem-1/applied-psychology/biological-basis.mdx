## In simple words

Behaviour has a biological basis. The nervous system and endocrine system work together to receive information, make decisions and produce responses.

The basic unit is the **neuron**: it receives signals through dendrites, processes them in the cell body, and sends them down the axon. At the synapse, chemicals called neurotransmitters pass the signal to the next cell.

## The neuron and impulse

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Dendrites | Receive signals from other neurons |
| Cell body | Contains the nucleus; keeps the cell alive |
| Axon | Carries the impulse away from the cell body |
| Myelin sheath | Insulates the axon and speeds the impulse |
| Synapse | Gap where neurotransmitters pass the signal |

A nerve impulse is an electrical wave called an **action potential**. It travels down the axon and triggers neurotransmitter release at the synapse.

## The nervous system

- **Central nervous system (CNS)** — brain and spinal cord.
- **Peripheral nervous system (PNS)** — nerves from the CNS to the body.
- **Somatic** — voluntary movement of skeletal muscle.
- **Autonomic** — involuntary control of organs:
  - **Sympathetic** — fight or flight.
  - **Parasympathetic** — rest and digest.

## The brain

| Part | Function |
|---|---|
| Cerebrum | Thinking, memory, speech, voluntary movement |
| Cerebellum | Balance and coordination |
| Brainstem | Breathing, heart rate, blood pressure |
| Limbic system | Emotions and memory (amygdala, hippocampus) |

The endocrine system supports behaviour through hormones — for example, adrenaline prepares the body for action and cortisol is released during stress.

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Neurological checks** — limb movement, pupil response and level of consciousness reflect brain and nerve function.
- **Pain** — pain signals travel through nerves; understanding pathways helps with pain assessment and relief.
- **Stress response** — sympathetic activation raises heart rate and blood pressure; help the patient calm down.
- **Medication effects** — many drugs act on neurotransmitters; watch for side effects.
- **Spinal injury** — keep the spine still; damage below the injury loses movement and sensation.
