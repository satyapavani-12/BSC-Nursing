## In simple words

Self-empowerment means understanding yourself, valuing yourself and speaking up for yourself respectfully. For nurses, it supports confidence, good boundaries and resilience against burnout.

Self-awareness is knowing your own feelings, strengths and limits. Self-esteem is how much you value yourself. Assertiveness is expressing your needs clearly while respecting others.

## Communication styles

| Style | Behaviour | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Passive | Gives in, avoids conflict | "Whatever you want" |
| Aggressive | Pushes own views, ignores others | "Do it my way" |
| Assertive | States own needs, respects others | "I need a break; let's cover each other" |

Assertive communication uses "I" statements: "I feel…, I need…, I would like…".

## Self-esteem and self-awareness

- High self-esteem helps nurses cope with criticism and stress.
- Low self-esteem leads to self-doubt and difficulty saying no.
- Self-awareness helps a nurse recognise when they are stressed and seek help.

## Stress management for nurses

- Take breaks and stay hydrated.
- Use relaxation — deep breathing, mindfulness.
- Talk to a trusted colleague or supervisor.
- Maintain work–life balance.
- Seek counselling if stress becomes overwhelming.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Saying no** — decline extra tasks politely when overloaded: "I cannot take on more right now; let's redistribute."
- **Asking for help** — speak up when unsure about a procedure or patient.
- **Setting boundaries** — keep the nurse–patient relationship professional.
- **Managing burnout** — take breaks, use leave, talk to a supervisor.
- **Confidence** — speak up in ward rounds and handovers with accurate information.
