## In simple words

Motivation is what drives behaviour toward a goal. Emotion is a feeling state such as fear, joy or anger. Stress is the body's response to demands; coping is how a person manages it.

Nurses meet patients who are frightened, in pain or stressed — understanding motivation and emotion helps them care better.

## Maslow's hierarchy of needs

| Level | Need | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Physiological | Air, food, water, sleep |
| 2 | Safety | Security, freedom from fear |
| 3 | Love and belonging | Family, friends, relationships |
| 4 | Esteem | Respect, achievement, confidence |
| 5 | Self-actualisation | Reaching one's potential |

Lower needs must be mostly met before higher needs become urgent. A patient gasping for breath (physiological) cannot focus on self-esteem.

## Emotions

Basic emotions include happiness, sadness, fear, anger, surprise and disgust. Emotions have three parts:
- Physical changes (heart rate, hormones)
- Behaviour (facial expression, action)
- Feeling (how it is experienced)

## Stress and coping

Stress is the body's response to a threat or demand. The **general adaptation syndrome (Selye)** has three stages: alarm, resistance and exhaustion.

Coping styles:
- **Problem-focused** — tackle the cause directly.
- **Emotion-focused** — manage the feelings (talking, relaxation, prayer).

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Prioritise care** — airway, breathing and circulation first (physiological), then safety.
- **Acknowledge emotion** — name the feeling ("You seem worried") and listen.
- **Reduce stress** — explain procedures, give time, involve family, teach relaxation.
- **Motivate change** — link advice to what matters to the patient (family, work, independence).
- **Watch for exhaustion** — long stress leads to burnout in patients and staff; encourage rest and support.
