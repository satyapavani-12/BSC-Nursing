## In simple words

Developmental psychology studies how people grow and change from birth to old age — physically, cognitively, socially and emotionally.

Two theories examiners ask about are **Erikson's psychosocial stages** (crisis at each stage) and **Piaget's cognitive stages** (how thinking develops).

Nurses use this to match care, communication and teaching to the patient's age and stage.

## Erikson's psychosocial stages

| Stage | Age | Crisis | Healthy outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trust vs mistrust | 0–1 year | Caregiver reliability | Trust |
| Autonomy vs shame | 1–3 years | Doing things independently | Autonomy |
| Initiative vs guilt | 3–6 years | Planning and leading | Initiative |
| Industry vs inferiority | 6–12 years | Learning and achieving | Competence |
| Identity vs role confusion | 12–18 years | Who am I? | Identity |
| Intimacy vs isolation | Young adult | Close relationships | Love |
| Generativity vs stagnation | Middle adult | Contributing to next generation | Care |
| Integrity vs despair | Old age | Looking back on life | Wisdom |

## Piaget's cognitive stages

| Stage | Age | Main feature |
|---|---|---|
| Sensorimotor | 0–2 years | Learns through senses and actions; object permanence |
| Preoperational | 2–7 years | Symbols and language; egocentric thinking |
| Concrete operational | 7–11 years | Logical thinking about real objects |
| Formal operational | 11+ years | Abstract and hypothetical thinking |

## Nursing implications by age

- **Infant** — respond promptly to build trust; involve parents.
- **Toddler** — allow choices ("which arm first?"); short, simple explanations.
- **Preschooler** — use play; reassure that illness is not punishment.
- **School-age** — explain procedures honestly; encourage participation.
- **Adolescent** — respect privacy and identity; involve them in decisions.
- **Adult** — respect autonomy; teach self-management.
- **Older adult** — allow time; assume competence unless shown otherwise.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Match communication** — simple words for a preschooler; honest detail for a school-age child.
- **Involve the family** — parents are the main comfort for infants and toddlers.
- **Respect privacy** — adolescents need confidentiality and involvement in decisions.
- **Allow time** — older adults may process information more slowly.
- **Play** — use play to prepare children for procedures.

## 60-second revision

- Development = change from birth to old age.
- Erikson: Trust (0-1), Autonomy (1-3), Initiative (3-6), Industry (6-12), Identity (12-18).
- Piaget: Sensorimotor (0-2), Preoperational (2-7), Concrete (7-11), Formal (11+).
- Match care to age; involve family; respect adolescent privacy.
- Object permanence = sensorimotor milestone.
