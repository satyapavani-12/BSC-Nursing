## In simple words

Management is getting work done through people and resources, safely and on time. Nursing management applies the same ideas to people, care and safety.

## Functions of management

| Function | What it means in the ward |
|---|---|
| Planning | Set objectives and plan the duty roster, supplies and care plans |
| Organising | Group tasks, set roles, arrange equipment and staffing |
| Staffing | Recruit, train and allocate the right people |
| Directing | Guide, motivate, communicate and supervise |
| Controlling | Monitor standards, audits, budgets and quality |
| Coordinating | Align nursing, medical, laboratory and support services |

## Levels of management

- **Top-level** — policies, budgets, organisational goals
- **Middle-level** — nursing superintendent, deputy nursing superintendent; interpret policy for units
- **Front-line (first-line)** — ward in-charge, charge nurse; direct supervision of care

## Decision making steps

1. Identify and define the problem
2. Gather information and analyse
3. List possible options and weigh consequences
4. Decide and prepare an action plan
5. Implement, supervise and evaluate

## Delegation

- Assign the **right task** to the **right person** with **clear instructions**
- Delegate tasks, not accountability
- Explain, check understanding, give authority and support
- Review results and give feedback

<Callout type="must" title="Delegate the task, keep the accountability">
  When a nurse in-charge delegates a dressing or feeding, the **accountability for safe care stays with her**. Delegation means checking, supervising and giving feedback, not simply handing off duties.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Management: getting work done through people safely.
- Five functions: planning, organising, staffing, directing, controlling.
- Three levels: top, middle and front-line.
- Decision making: define, gather, choose, act, evaluate.
- Delegate the task but keep the accountability.
