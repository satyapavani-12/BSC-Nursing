## In simple words

Controlling compares what was planned with what actually happened and corrects the gap. In nursing this is quality assurance: standards, audits and indicators.

## Steps of control

1. Set standards — high quality care and records
2. Measure performance — counts, indicators, observation
3. Compare with the standard
4. Correct deviations
5. Follow up and re-evaluate

## Quality assurance in nursing

| Activity | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Nursing audit | Checks whether records meet standards |
| Peer review | Colleagues assess care against standards |
| Patient satisfaction survey | Voice of the patient |
| Outcome indicators | Clinically, falls, pressure ulcers, infection and mortality |
| Structural indicators | Staffing, equipment, facilities |

## Incident reporting

- Medication errors, falls, needle stick, equipment failure
- Report within the shift, with facts and no blame
- Data are used for learning, not punishment
- Blame-free reporting increases safety

<Callout type="must" title="Evidence-based practice keeps care current">
  Nursing practice is corrected **against evidence and current standards**, not habit. Guidelines, checklists and audits make the standard visible and measurable.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Control: standards, measure, compare, correct, follow up.
- Quality assurance: audit, peer review, indicators and patient satisfaction.
- Incident reporting: blame-free, facts and immediate.
- Evidence-based practice corrects habit.
- PDCA drives continuous improvement.
