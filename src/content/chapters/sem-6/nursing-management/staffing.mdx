## In simple words

Staffing is deciding **how many staff, with which skills, at which times** are needed for safe care. The duty roster turns this decision into a visible plan.

## Staffing estimation

| Method | Basis |
|---|---|
| Norms | Standard nurse-to-patient ratios prescribed per unit |
| Workload method | Minutes of care per patient multiplied by patient numbers |
| Work sampling | Sample observations to determine where time goes |
| Acuity rating | Severity of illness of each patient |

## Shift patterns

- **8-hour shifts** — morning, evening, night; commonest in India
- **12-hour shifts** — fewer handovers, long working day
- **Rotating shifts** — staff rotate; affects sleep and fatigue
- **ICUs and OT** — demanding; extra staff and rest planned
- Duty breaks, rest days and handover time are part of the roster

## Leave and staffing

- Staff leave and vacancy must be planned well before the day
- Float pool, pooling staff and recruiting temporary nurses cover shortages
- A unit should never run below safe staffing; if it does, record it
- Handover is included in duty, not squeezed into spare time

<Callout type="must" title="Understaffing is a patient safety risk">
  Running below safe staffing raises medication errors, falls and missed care. **Record understaffing and escalate; never let a shortage become the patient’s problem silently.**
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Staffing estimates by norms, workload and acuity.
- Roster: fair, leave-aware, with handover.
- Shift patterns: 8-hour, 12-hour, rotating.
- Manage leave with float pools and temporary staff.
- Understaffing: document and escalate.
