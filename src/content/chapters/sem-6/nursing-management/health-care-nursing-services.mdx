## In simple words

Health services in India are delivered by public and private agencies at three levels. Nursing services are the largest part of the workforce, and the nurse contributes at every level.

## Levels of health care

| Level | Provided by |
|---|---|
| Primary | Sub-centres, PHCs, community outreach, AYUSH |
| Secondary | District and CHC hospitals — internal medicine, general surgery, paediatrics |
| Tertiary | Medical college hospitals, specialty institutes |

## Agencies delivering nursing services

- **Government hospitals** — district, PHC, CHC and teaching hospitals
- **Private hospitals and nursing homes**
- **Missionary and voluntary agencies**
- **Defence, railways, police and industrial services**
- **International agencies** — WHO, UNICEF, Red Cross
- **Nursing regulatory bodies** — INC and State Nursing Councils

## Nurse’s career path

| Stage | Role |
|---|---|
| GNM / B.Sc. Nursing | Clinical care in ward and community |
| Post-basic B.Sc. | Advanced clinical practice |
| M.Sc. Nursing | Nurse educator, clinical specialist, administrator |
| Specialisation | Critical care, oncology, paediatric, community health |
| Leadership | Ward in-charge, nursing superintendent, matron, principal |

<Callout type="must" title="Nurse’s role in service delivery">
  The nurse is the **largest health work force**, the patient’s first and constant contact, and the link between doctor, family and community — and she also runs the quality systems of a unit.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Levels of care: primary, secondary, tertiary.
- Agencies: government, private, NGO, missions, industrial.
- Nursing staff run wards, ICUs and community clinics.
- Career: GNM to B.Sc. to M.Sc. to specialist and leadership.
- INC sets standards; state councils register.
