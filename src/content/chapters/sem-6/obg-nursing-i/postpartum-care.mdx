## In simple words

The **puerperium** is the 6 weeks after delivery. During this period the uterus returns to its normal state (involution), lactation begins and the mother recovers.

## Postpartum changes

| Change | Time |
|---|---|
| Uterus shrinks to non-pregnant size | 6 weeks |
| Uterine fundus is felt at or below the umbilicus | By day 2 |
| Lochia changes from red to serous to white | 3 weeks |
| Milk comes in after 3 days | 3-5 days |
| Menstruation returns later | 6-8 weeks, or later if breastfeeding |

## Assessing involution

- Fundus by day 1 at the umbilicus, by day 2 one finger below; then descends about a finger daily
- Firm, non-tender uterus that involutes is normal
- Uterus that is tender, soft or high suggests retained products or infection
- Lochia: rubra (red) for 3 days, serosa (brown/pink) for 7 days, alba (white) after 10 days, stopping by 3 weeks

## Breastfeeding

- **Colostrum** — first 2-3 days, rich in antibodies
- **Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months**, complementary foods from 6 months
- Correct attachment, frequent feeding, correct emptying of both breasts
- Frequent feeding stimulates milk; assume feeding on demand
- Watch for cracked nipples, engorgement and mastitis

## Nutritional advice

Iron and folic acid, calcium, vitamin A; plenty of fluids; protein-rich and calorie-dense foods; green leafy vegetables; iodised salt; dietary care for anaemia

<Callout type="must" title="Postpartum haemorrhage is the leading cause of maternal death">
  **Bleeding over 500 mL after birth, or a soft uterus with heavy lochia, is a medical emergency.** Give oxytocin, massage the uterus, call for help and maintain intravenous access.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Puerperium: 6 weeks of recovery.
- Uterus involutes; fundus descends daily.
- Lochia changes red, pink, white.
- Colostrum first; exclusive breastfeeding 6 months.
- Nutrition: iron, calcium, fluids, protein.
- Danger: heavy bleeding, fever, foul discharge, severe headache, sadness.
