## In simple words

Labour is the process by which the baby, placenta and membranes are expelled from the uterus. It has three stages: **dilatation, expulsion of the baby and expulsion of the placenta**.

## Signs of true labour

- **Dilating cervix** (main sign) — thick, bloody "show"
- **Dilation** increases on examination
- **Effacement** — thinning of the cervix
- **Painfu contractions** that become regular, stronger and longer
- **Formation of the bag of forewaters**

## Stages of labour

| Stage | Duration (primigravida) | Duration (multigravida) | Events |
|---|---|---|---|
| First — dilatation | Up to 12-16 hours | 6-8 hours | Cervix dilates to 10 cm; stage of pain and monitoring |
| Second — expulsion | 1-2 hours | 30 minutes | Pushing, delivery of the baby |
| Third — placental | 15-30 minutes | 5-15 minutes | Separation and expulsion of placenta |
| Fourth — 1-2 hours | Observation | Observation | Uterus contracts, bleeding controlled |

## Monitoring in labour — partograph

The **partograph** records cervical dilatation, descent of the head, fetal heart rate and uterine contractions against time. Crossing the alert or action line means referral and decision for intervention.

- Cervical dilatation plotted hourly after 4 cm
- Fetal heart rate every 30 minutes in first stage, every 5 minutes in second
- Normal fetal heart 110-160 per minute
- **Signs of obstructed labour:** prolonged labour, cervix not dilating, head not descending, moulding, fetal distress, rupture of uterus, constant pain, bandl’s ring

## Nursing care in labour

- **First stage:** admit and assess, shave and enema as per protocol, encourage ambulation and voiding, position, pain relief, monitor vital signs and fetal heart, nutrition and oral fluids
- **Second stage:** prepare delivery room, avoid pushing too hard, control the birth of the head, prevent perineal tear, check the cord around the neck, clear the airway, clamp and cut, keep the baby warm, skin-to-skin, start breastfeeding
- **Third stage:** active management with oxytocin, controlled cord traction, watch for bleeding, check placenta and membranes
- **Fourth stage:** monitor the uterus, bleeding and vital signs for 1-2 hours

## Signs of placental separation

- Gush of blood, cord lengthens, uterus becomes globular and firm
- Controlled cord traction and fundal support deliver the placenta

<Callout type="must" title="Keep the baby warm and start breastfeeding within one hour">
  Immediately after birth: dry and cover the baby, **skin-to-skin, cut the cord after 1-3 minutes where safe**, give vitamin K, and start breastfeeding within the first hour.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- True labour = progressive dilatation with regular contractions.
- Four stages: dilation, expulsion, placental, observation.
- Monitor fetal heart and maternal vitals; plot the partograph.
- Assist second-stage delivery safely; airway and warmth for the baby.
- Actively manage the third stage; massage the uterus.
- Watch bleeding in the first 1-2 hours after delivery.
