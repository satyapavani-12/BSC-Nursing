## In simple words

Communicable diseases spread from person to person. In children, clusters of fever with rash (exanthemata) and respiratory infections are common — and mostly preventable by vaccination.

## Common childhood communicable diseases

| Disease | Incubation | Rash or feature |
|---|---|---|
| Measles | 10-14 days | Maculopapular rash from hairline; three Cs |
| Chickenpox (varicella) | 14-16 days | Crops of vesicles; very itchy |
| Mumps | 16-18 days | Painful parotid swelling |
| Rubella | 14-21 days | Pink maculopapular rash; danger in pregnancy |
| Diphtheria | 2-5 days | Grey throat membrane; bull neck |
| Pertussis (whooping cough) | 7-14 days | Paroxysmal cough with whoop and vomiting |
| Polio | 3-35 days | Asymmetric flaccid paralysis |
| Roseola infantum | 5-15 days | High fever then sudden pink rash |

## Nursing care of infectious children

- **Isolation** according to route (airborne, droplet, contact)
- **Comfort** — light clothing, soft diet, cool room, prune nails to prevent scratching
- **Itch control** in chickenpox — cold compresses, trim nails, gloves at night
- **Monitor** temperature, feeding, swallowing and breathing
- **Prevent secondary infection** — mouth care, skin care, hand hygiene
- **Support the family** — explain home isolation, vaccination of siblings
- **Report** notifiable diseases to authorities

<Callout type="must" title="Never give aspirin in chickenpox or influenza">
  **Aspirin is avoided in children with viral illness** because of Reye syndrome (liver and brain swelling). Use paracetamol for fever.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Measles, chickenpox, mumps, rubella, roseola: characteristic rashes.
- Pertussis: paroxysmal cough, whoop, vomiting.
- Diphtheria: grey membrane; polio: flaccid paralysis.
- Care: isolate, comfort, itch control, fever control, mouth care.
- No aspirin; vaccinate; report notifiable diseases.
