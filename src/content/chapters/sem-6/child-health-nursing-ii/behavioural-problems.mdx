## In simple words

Behavioural problems are common, and most are managed by consistent, kind parenting. Some problems need specialist help — early identification protects learning and relationships.

## Common behavioural problems

| Problem | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| Temper tantrums | Screaming, striking the floor, 1-3 years | Ignore, stay calm, reward good behaviour |
| Breath-holding spells | Cry then stop breathing, brief loss of consciousness | Reassure, keep safe, iron if anaemic |
| Enuresis | Bedwetting beyond 5 years | Bladder training, night waking, rewards, alarm |
| Encopresis | Passing stool in inappropriate places | Toilet training, treat constipation |
| Nail biting and thumb sucking | Habit under tension | Distraction, gentle correction |
| Stuttering and stammering | Speech hesitations | Speak slowly, speech therapy |
| Truancy and aggression | Lying, fighting, stealing | Clear limits, counselling, family work |
| Learning difficulty | Poor school performance despite normal ability | Assessment, remedial teaching |

## ADHD and autism

| Condition | Features | Support |
|---|---|---|
| ADHD | Inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity for over 6 months across settings | Structured routine, short tasks, behaviour therapy, medicines |
| Autism spectrum disorder | Poor social communication, restricted interests, rigid routines, sensory sensitivity | Early intervention, special education, parent training, speech therapy |

## Family counselling and prevention

- Consistent rules; same response from both parents
- Praise desirable behaviour; ignore minor attention-seeking
- Limit screen time; encourage play, sports and sleep
- Avoid labelling the child as bad or lazy
- Support parents and refer for professional assessment when change does not occur

<Callout type="must" title="Never shame a child for enuresis">
  Bedwetting is not laziness or naughtiness. **Punishing a child for enuresis makes it worse.** Use reassurance, bladder training, reward systems and referral if it persists.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Habit, emotional and elimination problems are common and largely managed at home.
- Tantrums: consistent limits, ignore safely.
- Enuresis: reassurance, bladder training, rewards; never shame.
- ADHD and autism: early identification and specialist support.
- Family counselling and school cooperation.
