## In simple words

Organic brain disorders are mental problems caused by a disease of the brain itself. The two most important are **delirium** (sudden, reversible) and **dementia** (slow, often progressive).

## Delirium versus dementia

| Feature | Delirium | Dementia |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Sudden, over hours to days | Slow, over months to years |
| Duration | Fluctuates over the day | Persistent |
| Level of consciousness | Reduced, drowsy | Normal |
| Reversibility | Usually reversible | Often progressive |

## Dementia

| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| Alzheimer | Degenerative disease; most common |
| Vascular dementia | Repeated strokes |
| Lewy body dementia | Fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations |
| Frontotemporal dementia | Personality and language change |
| Mixed | Combination of causes |

**Features:** memory loss, confusion, disorientation, language difficulty, apathy, wandering, needing help with daily living.

## Delirium

- **Causes:** infection, dehydration, fever, medicines, drug withdrawal, head injury
- **Features:** sudden confusion, restlessness, hallucinations, reversal of sleep-wake cycle
- **Nursing:** find and treat the cause; good lighting, familiar people, reassurance, safety

## Amnesic and alcohol-related disorders

- **Korsakoff syndrome** — severe memory impairment, confabulation
- **Wernicke encephalopathy** — confusion, ataxia, eye signs; give thiamine immediately

<Callout type="must" title="Always rule out delirium first">
  Sudden confusion is often a **medical problem**, not a mental illness. Look for fever, urinary retention, low sodium, new medicines or pain before making a diagnosis of dementia.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Delirium: sudden, fluctuating, reversible; treat the cause.
- Dementia: slow, progressive; memory loss first; Alzheimer commonest.
- Lewy body and vascular dementia are other causes.
- Amnesic disorders: Wernicke and Korsakoff; give thiamine.
- Care: day refill orientation cues, safety, family support.
