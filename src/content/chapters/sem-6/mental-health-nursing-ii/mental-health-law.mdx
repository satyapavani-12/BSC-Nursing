## In simple words

The **Mental Healthcare Act, 2017** replaced the Mental Health Act, 1987. It protects the rights of people with mental illness and gives them the right to access mental health care with dignity.

## Types of admission

| Type | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Independent admission | A patient who can decide for themselves stays by choice |
| Supported admission | For patients with limited capacity; all admissions are supported under the Act |
| Emergency admission | Up to 72 hours when there is immediate danger |

## Rights under MHCA 2017

- Right to access mental health care
- Right to equality and non-discrimination
- Right to live in the community
- Right to be free from cruel, inhuman treatment
- Right to keep their property
- Right to an advance directive — a written instruction about treatment preferences
- Right to confidentiality and records
- Right to legal aid and to challenge decisions

## Other legal points

- **Attempted suicide** is decriminalised under MHCA 2017 — the person must be treated and supported
- **Consent** must be informed and, where possible, by the patient
- **Restraint and seclusion** are regulated; chains and beatings are forbidden
- **Discharge** requires a review and family involvement for supported admissions
- **Confidentiality** of the record is required, with limits for safety
- **Mental Health Review Boards** review admissions and grievances

<Callout type="must" title="Suicide is a treatment matter, not a crime">
  Under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, attempted suicide must be treated as an **emergency for care**, not an offence. Nurses must respond, protect and refer.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- MHCA 2017 replaced MHA 1987.
- Admission: independent, supported, emergency (72 hours).
- Rights: access, community living, dignity, property, records, legal aid.
- Advance directive guides future treatment.
- Nurses: take consent, document, avoid illegal restraint, support discharge.
