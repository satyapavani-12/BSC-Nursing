## In simple words

A psychiatric emergency needs immediate action to protect life and safety. The first priorities are always **safety, airway, breathing and circulation**, then the mental state.

## Common psychiatric emergencies

- **Suicide attempt** — poisoning, hanging, cutting
- **Violence and rage** — may be due to psychosis, intoxication or withdrawal
- **Catatonia** — mutism, rigidity, refusal of food and water
- **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome** — fever, rigidity, altered mind
- **Serotonin syndrome** — shivering, diarrhoea, clonus, high fever
- **Severe drug withdrawal** — alcohol, benzodiazepines
- **Puerperal psychosis** — restlessness, refusal to feed the baby, delusions

## Crisis intervention steps

1. **Ensure safety** — of the patient, staff and others
2. **Assess** — level of risk, cause, supports
3. **Listen actively** — let the person talk about what happened
4. **Stabilise** — find safe strategies, involve family
5. **Plan** — concrete steps, resources and follow-up
6. **Follow up** and evaluate

<Callout type="must" title="Suicide precautions">
  **Do not leave the patient alone, do not promise secrecy, and do not allow access to means.** Medicines, sharp objects, ropes, belts and windows are risks. One-to-one observation, remove means and regular documentation are mandatory.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Emergency room** — manage overdose, violence and self-harm
- **Ward** — observation rounds, restraint checks, prevent suicide methods
- **Perinatal unit** — screen and protect mothers with postpartum psychosis
- **Crisis situations** — family disputes, grief, disaster and accidents

## 60-second revision

- Emergencies: suicide, violence, catatonia, NMS, serotonin syndrome, withdrawal.
- First step in any emergency is safety.
- Suicidal patients are never left alone, and means are removed.
- Violence de-escalation: calm, distance, space, escort.
- Restraints only as last resort with monitoring and release checks.
