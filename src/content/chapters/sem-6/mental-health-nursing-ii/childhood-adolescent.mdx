## In simple words

Mental disorders begin in childhood or adolescence more often than we think. Early recognition protects learning, relationships and future mental health.

## Intellectual disability

| Level | IQ | Ability |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | 50-69 | Can learn skills and work with support |
| Moderate | 35-49 | Needs supervised work and daily living help |
| Severe | 20-34 | Needs constant supervision |
| Profound | Under 20 | Total dependence for care |

## Common childhood disorders

| Disorder | Features |
|---|---|
| ADHD | Inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity across settings |
| Autism spectrum disorder | Social communication deficit, restricted interests, need for sameness |
| Conduct disorder | Bending rules, aggression, cruelty, lying, theft |
| Learning difficulties | Poor reading, writing or arithmetic despite normal ability |
| Anxiety disorders | School refusal, separation fear, excessive worry |
| Enuresis and encopresis | Involuntary voiding after 5 years |

## Adolescent mental health

- **Depression** — low mood, irritability, loss of interest, self-harm and suicide risk
- **Eating disorders** — anorexia nervosa (low weight, fear of gaining weight), bulimia nervosa (binge and purging)
- **Self-harm** — cutting or burning as coping; always assess for depression
- **Substance use** — early experimentation; prevention programmes
- **Psychosis** — early schizophrenia may appear in adolescence
- **Behaviour problems** — aggression, antisocial behaviour, school dropout

## Nursing care

- Screen children and adolescents at every opportunity
- Talk at the child’s level; use play
- For anxiety, teach breathing and simple relaxation
- Correct beliefs and remove myths
- Protect from stigma; never label a child as bad
- Work with family and school
- Ensure safety; report neglect, abuse and criminal acts
- Refer to a specialist with clear follow-up

<Callout type="must" title="Adolescent suicide is prevented by talking">
  Ask calmly about depression and suicide. **Remove the means, tell the parents, and never promise secrecy.** Most adolescents who attempt have asked for help.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Intellectual disability: graded by IQ and support needs.
- ADHD, autism and learning difficulties are neurodevelopmental.
- Conduct disorder and adolescence problems need family and school work.
- Depression, self-harm, eating disorders and substance use appear in adolescence.
- Nurse’s role: screen, talk, protect, educate, refer.
