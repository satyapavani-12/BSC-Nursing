## In simple words

Statistics is the science of collecting, organising and making sense of numbers. It turns raw data into clear findings and supports decisions.

## Scales of measurement

| Scale | Example | What can be done |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal | Gender, blood group | Count only |
| Ordinal | Pain mild, moderate, severe | Rank or order |
| Interval | Temperature in degrees | Add and subtract, no true zero |
| Ratio | Height, weight, pulse | All arithmetic; true zero exists |

## Presenting data

- **Frequency table** — categories with counts and percentages
- **Bar chart** — compares categories
- **Histogram** — shows shape of a continuous variable
- **Pie chart** — shares of a total
- **Line graph** — trends over time
- **Scatter plot** — relationship between two numbers
- **Normal curve** — symmetrical bell-shape with mean, median and mode equal

## Central tendency

| Measure | Formula | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | Sum of values / number of values | Normal or interval data |
| Median | Middle value | Skewed data |
| Mode | Most frequent value | Nominal data |

## Dispersion

- **Range** = highest value minus lowest
- **Variance** = average squared distance from the mean
- **Standard deviation (SD)** = square root of variance
- About 95% of values fall within 2 SD of the mean in a normal distribution

## Normal distribution

- Symmetrical bell-shape; mean, median and mode coincide
- 68% within 1 SD, 95% within 2 SD, 99.7% within 3 SD
- Skewed data use the median; extreme values pull the mean

<Callout type="must" title="Choose the statistic from the level of data">
  Nominal data give mode; interval and ratio data give mean and SD. **The level of measurement decides which test is allowed** — using a mean for a nominal variable is meaningless.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Scales: nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio.
- Presentation: tables, bar, histogram, pie, line, scatter.
- Central tendency: mean, median, mode.
- Dispersion: range, variance, standard deviation.
- Normal distribution: 68-95-99.7 rule.
