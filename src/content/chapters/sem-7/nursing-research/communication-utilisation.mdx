## In simple words

Research that stays in a file is wasted. Reporting, presenting and applying findings complete the process.

## Structure of a research report

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Title | Short, clear and specific |
| Abstract | Summary of the whole study |
| Introduction | Background and need |
| Review of literature | What is known and the gap |
| Methodology | Design, sample, ethics, tools |
| Results | Findings in tables and graphs |
| Discussion | Meaning, comparison with literature, limitations |
| Conclusion and recommendations | Implications and suggestions |
| References | All sources cited |
| Appendices | Tools and supplementary material |

## Referencing

- Cite the source in the text and list it in the reference section
- **APA style:** Author, initials (year), Title, Edition, Publisher
- **Vancouver:** numbered citations in order of appearance
- Consistent style, no missing reference and no unreferenced citation
- Always avoid plagiarism

## Using research in practice

1. **Ask** a practice question (e.g. does hourly repositioning prevent pressure ulcers?)
2. **Acquire** evidence from research and guidelines
3. **Appraise** the quality and feasibility
4. **Apply** to the ward with evaluation
5. **Assess** the audit result and revise

<Callout type="must" title="A research report is a clinical tool">
  A well-written report tells the reader **what was done, what was found and what should change**. Present findings in plain words that colleagues in the ward can use.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Report: title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusion, references.
- Present at conferences, in journals and posters.
- Reference with APA or Vancouver; avoid plagiarism.
- Utilise evidence: ask, acquire, appraise, apply, assess.
- Dissemination and application complete the research.
