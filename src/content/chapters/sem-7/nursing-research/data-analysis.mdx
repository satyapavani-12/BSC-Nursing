## In simple words

Analysis turns numbers or words into findings. Preparation first: check for errors, missing answers and outliers. Then describe, compare and interpret.

## Steps in data analysis

1. **Editing** — check for completeness and errors
2. **Classification and coding** — group responses and assign numbers
3. **Tabulation** — make master tables
4. **Descriptive statistics** — summarise the sample
5. **Inferential statistics** — test differences and relationships
6. **Interpretation** — what do the findings mean

## Descriptive statistics

| Measure | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Frequency | How often things happen |
| Percentage | Share of the total |
| Mean | Average |
| Median | Middle value |
| Mode | Most frequent value |
| Range | Highest minus lowest |
| Standard deviation | Spread of values around the mean |

## Inferential statistics — choosing a test

| Purpose | Test |
|---|---|
| Compare two group means | Independent samples t-test |
| Compare paired means | Paired t-test |
| Compare two proportions (nominal) | Chi-square test |
| Compare three or more groups | ANOVA |
| Relationship between two numbers | Pearson r |
| Ranked or ordinal data | Mann-Whitney, Spearman |
| Predict from two or more factors | Regression |

**Level of significance:** most nursing studies use **p less than 0.05**. A p value is the probability that a result happened by chance. If p is less than 0.05, the result is called statistically significant.

<Callout type="must" title="Significance is not importance">
  A statistically significant result may be too small to matter clinically, while a meaningful result may fail to reach significance in a small sample. **Always discuss the actual size of the change, not only the p value.**
</Callout>

  Describe then compare: **D-M-S** — **D**escribe (mean, SD), **M**eans (t-test), **S**hares (chi-square).

## 60-second revision

- Prepare data: edit, code, tabulate.
- Describe: frequency, percentage, mean, median, mode, SD.
- Test: t-test, chi-square, ANOVA, correlation, regression.
- p under 0.05 is significant.
- Present in tables and graphs; interpret with limits.
