## In simple words

A good research question is clear, answerable, ethical and worth the effort. Objectives state what will be answered and hypotheses state what is expected.

## Sources of research problems

| Source | Example |
|---|---|
| Clinical experience | Frequent falls on a specific ward |
| Patient complaints | Poor sleep quality in the intensive care unit |
| Literature gaps | Areas not yet studied in India |
| Nursing theory | Testing a model of care |
| Priorities | IMPORTANT topic — infection, safety, pain |
| Nursing practice challenges | Improving discharge teaching |

## Criteria of a good problem

1. Interesting to the researcher and valuable to patients
2. Ethical, safe and feasible within time, money and tools
3. Specific, clear and measurable
4. Novel — fills a gap or repeats a study in a new setting
5. Not too small (obvious) or too large (unanswerable)

## Problem statement

A problem statement names the **population, the condition, and the outcome or relationship** studied.

Example: "Does morning repositioning every two hours prevent pressure ulcers among bed-ridden patients in the surgical ward over four weeks?"

## Variables

| Variable | Role |
|---|---|
| Independent | The factor that is manipulated or measured as a cause |
| Dependent | The outcome measured |
| Demographic | Age, gender, occupation, income |
| Intervening | Factors that influence the relationship |
| Confounding | Factors that distort the relationship and must be controlled |

## Objectives and hypotheses

- **Objectives** — specific, measurable, time-bound, with a population
- **Hypotheses** — a predictive statement about the relationship between two variables
- **Null hypothesis (H0):** no relationship or no difference
- **Alternative hypothesis (H1):** a relationship or difference exists

<Callout type="must" title="A vague problem leads to a failed study">
  "Improving patient satisfaction" is not a research problem. Make it specific: **who, what, how and when** — then it can be tested and measured.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Sources: practice, patients, literature, theory, prioritisation.
- Criteria: interest, ethics, feasibility, specificity, novelty.
- Variables: independent, dependent, demographic, confounding.
- Objectives measurable; hypotheses testable (H0 and H1).
- Problem statement says who, what, where and when.
