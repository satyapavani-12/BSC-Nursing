## In simple words

A high-risk newborn needs extra attention. Problems include birth asphyxia, low birth weight, jaundice, breathing difficulty, infection, cold stress and low blood sugar.

## Identifying the high-risk newborn

- **Birth weight** under 2.5 kg, or gestational age under 37 weeks
- **APGAR** below 7 at 5 minutes
- **Maternal risk** — diabetes, anaemia, hypertension, prolonged rupture of membranes
- **Family risk** — previous neonatal death
- **Birth difficulty** — obstructed labour, cord prolapse, meconium

## Neonatal resuscitation

- **Warm**, dry, clear the airway, stimulate
- If no breathing or heart rate under 100, give **positive pressure ventilation**
- If heart rate under 60 after ventilation, start chest compressions with 3:1 ratio
- Give oxygen only when needed; **avoid hyperoxia**
- The nurse prepares equipment, records events and supports the family

## Respiratory distress syndrome

- **Causes:** prematurity (surfactant deficiency), meconium aspiration, infection, pneumonia
- **Signs:** tachypnoea, grunting, chest indrawing, cyanosis
- **Management:** oxygen, CPAP, surfactant therapy, ventilator support, fluid balance

## Jaundice

- **Physiological jaundice** appears after 24 hours and settles by day 10
- **Pathological jaundice** appears within 24 hours, rises fast, lasts beyond 14 days or involves palms and soles
- **Treatment:** phototherapy and, in severe cases, **exchange transfusion**

## Neonatal sepsis and hypoglycaemia

- **Sepsis:** poor feeding, lethargy, temperature instability; cultures and antibiotics, umbilical catheter care
- **Hypoglycaemia** is common in preterm, low birth weight and diabetic mothers' babies; feed early and give breast milk

<Callout type="must" title="Keep the baby warm and feed early">
  Cold stress uses more oxygen and glucose, causing death in small babies. **Warm chain, early feeding and minimal handling are as important as medicines.**
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Identify high risk by weight, gestation, APGAR and maternal risk.
- Resuscitation: warm, airway, breathing, ventilation, compressions.
- Respiratory distress: oxygen, CPAP, ventilator care.
- Jaundice: physiological after 24 hours; phototherapy and exchange transfusion.
- Sepsis and hypoglycaemia need early detection and treatment.
