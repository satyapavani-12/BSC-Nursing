## In simple words

Ageing is a normal process, not a disease. Geriatric care aims to **maintain ability and dignity** and prevent disability.

## Ageing and common problems

| System | Changes | Common problems |
|---|---|---|
| Musculoskeletal | Bone loss, muscle weakness | Pain, falls, fractures, immobility |
| Cardiovascular | Stiffer arteries | Hypertension, heart disease |
| Nervous | Slower processing, reduced memory | Depression, dementia |
| Sensory | Poor vision, hearing loss, reduced taste | Isolation, accidents |
| Urinary | Weak bladder | Incontinence, frequency, retention |
| Skin | Thin, dry | Itching, pressure ulcers |
| Nutrition | Poor appetite, dental problems | Malnutrition, constipation |
| Mental | Grief and loss | Loneliness, suicide risk |

## Care of the elderly at home

- **Home safety:** non-slip mats, grab rails, good lighting, remove loose rugs
- **Medication review:** pill boxes, avoid confusion and polypharmacy
- **Nutrition:** soft, high-protein food, small frequent meals, plenty of fluids
- **Physical activity:** walking and range-of-motion exercises to prevent stiffness
- **Sensory:** spectacles, hearing aids, cataract and hearing assessment
- **Cognition and mood:** orientation, activities, social contact, screen for depression and dementia
- **Continence:** toilet training, pads, treat constipation and urinary infection
- **Skin:** inspect heels and sacrum, keep dry, prevent pressure ulcers
- **Immunisation and screening:** blood pressure, sugar and cancer checks

## Elder abuse and neglect

- Signs: unexplained injury, poor hygiene, apathy, neglected medical care, hoarded money, missing ration
- **Report through adult protective services** where such services exist; alert the local committee or the hospital
- Support the care giver: explain the illness, teach nursing care, arrange respite and home visits

<Callout type="must" title="Ability, not dependence">
  Every extra day in bed makes an older adult weaker. **Promote activity, independence and involvement in decisions; treat falls, incontinence and confusion as problems to solve, not as normal ageing.**
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Ageing affects bones, senses, memory, bladder and nutrition.
- Assess function, falls, vision, hearing, cognition and mood.
- Home care: safety, medicines, diet, exercise, skin and toileting.
- Detect and report elder abuse; support the family.
- Keep the older person active and independent.
