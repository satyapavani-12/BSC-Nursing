## In simple words

Demography is the study of population — size, structure and change. Vital statistics (births, deaths, marriages) and surveillance help us plan care and measure results.

## Basic terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Census | Counting the whole population every 10 years in India |
| Demographic cycle | Stages of population growth |
| Birth rate | Live births per 1000 population per year |
| Death rate | Deaths per 1000 population per year |
| Growth rate | Births minus deaths plus migration |
| Sex ratio | Females per 1000 males |
| Dependency ratio | Ratio of non-working to working population |
| Life expectancy | Average years a person is expected to live |

## Vital statistics registration

- **Birth registration:** unique identity, school entry and citizenship evidence
- **Death registration:** medical cause of death certificate, needed for property and insurance claims
- **Marriage registration:** legal proof
- The midwife or health worker registers events at the health centre or gram panchayat

## Common rates

| Rate | Formula |
|---|---|
| Birth rate | Live births / 1000 mid-year population |
| Death rate | Deaths / 1000 mid-year population |
| Infant mortality rate | Deaths under 1 year / 1000 live births |
| Maternal mortality ratio | Maternal deaths / 100 000 live births |
| Neonatal mortality rate | Deaths under 28 days / 1000 live births |
| Sex ratio | Females / 1000 males |
| Literacy rate | Literates / population above 6 years |

## Disease surveillance

- **Notifiable diseases** are reported immediately or weekly to authorities
- Weekly reports from sub-centre to PHC to district
- Epidemic detection: sudden increase over expected level
- Outbreak investigation steps: verify, define, control, report

<Callout type="must" title="Records are legal and planning documents">
  A birth certificate gives identity; a death certificate allows legal settlement. **Incomplete or late registration causes real harm. Nurses must complete and submit forms accurately and on time.**
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Demography: census, population structure, sex ratio, growth rate.
- Vital statistics: births, deaths, marriages registered.
- Rates: birth, death, IMR, MMR, neonatal mortality.
- Surveillance: notifiable diseases and outbreak signals.
- Nurses register and report accurately and on time.
