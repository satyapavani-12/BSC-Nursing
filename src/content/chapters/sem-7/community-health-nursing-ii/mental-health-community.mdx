## In simple words

Most people with mental illness live and recover with their families. Community mental health delivers care where they live and keeps them out of institutions.

## The National Mental Health Programme and DMHP

- **NMHP 1982** and revised **DMHP from 1996**
- DMHP services: outpatient clinics, inpatient care, outreach, and records
- Manpower: psychiatrist, clinical psychologist, psychiatric social worker and community nurse
- Mobile mental health teams reach villages
- Day-care and halfway homes support the transition back home

## Family burden

- Chronic care is often carried out by women at home
- Burden rises with disability, aggression and limited coping
- Assess burden at each visit; provide teaching, respite and counselling
- Watch for abuse of the patient or neglect by the family

## Stigma reduction

- Negative labels stop treatment and damage recovery
- Education, contact with recovered people and inclusion reduce stigma
- Awareness camps, school programmes and community dialogues are used
- The nurse uses respectful language in every interaction

<Callout type="must" title="Keep the person in the community">
  Community care maintains **dignity, family life and work** better than long institutional care. Rehabilitation means restoring the person’s role, not only reducing symptoms.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- NMHP and DMHP take mental health care to districts.
- Outreach visits find and support patients at home.
- Family burden is assessed and supported.
- Rehabilitation: skills, work, family integration.
- Stigma reduced by education, contact and inclusion.
