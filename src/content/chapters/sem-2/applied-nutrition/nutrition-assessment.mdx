## In simple words

Nutrition assessment finds out whether a person is well nourished, under-nourished or over-nourished. It uses anthropometry, clinical signs, dietary history and laboratory tests.

Nutrition education teaches patients and families how to choose and prepare healthy food within their means.

## Methods of nutrition assessment

| Method | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropometry | Body measurements | Weight, height, mid-upper arm circumference |
| Clinical | Signs of deficiency | Pallor, bleeding gums, bitot spots |
| Dietary | What the person eats | 24-hour recall, food frequency |
| Laboratory | Blood tests | Haemoglobin, serum albumin |

## Anthropometric measurements

- **Weight** — compared with height for age.
- **Height** — compared with age.
- **Mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC)** — quick screen for acute malnutrition in children.
- **Body Mass Index (BMI)** — weight (kg) ÷ height (m)².

## Nutrition education

- Use simple language and local examples.
- Show, don’t just tell — use food models or pictures.
- Involve the family.
- Give one or two key messages at a time.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Admission screening** — weigh and measure every patient; note deficiency signs.
- **Children** — measure MUAC; plot weight on growth charts.
- **Nutrition education** — teach balanced diet, hygiene and therapeutic diets simply.
- **Refer** — refer malnourished patients to the dietitian or doctor.
- **Follow-up** — re-assess to see if nutrition is improving.

## 60-second revision

- Nutrition assessment = anthropometry, clinical, dietary, laboratory.
- Measurements: weight, height, MUAC, BMI.
- MUAC screens child malnutrition.
- Deficiency signs: pallor, bleeding gums, bitot spots.
- Education: simple, local, family, few messages at a time.
