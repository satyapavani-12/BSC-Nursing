## In simple words

Proteins are body-building nutrients. They are made of amino acids and are needed for growth, repair of tissues, enzymes, hormones, antibodies and fluid balance.

One gram of protein gives about **4 kcal**. Proteins come from animal sources (complete) and plant sources (often incomplete).

## Sources of protein

| Type | Examples | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Animal | Milk, eggs, fish, meat, chicken | Complete protein |
| Plant | Dal, pulses, soya, nuts, groundnut | Often incomplete; combine with cereals |

Combining cereals (rice/wheat) with pulses (dal) improves protein quality — this is called supplementary protein action.

## Functions

- Growth and repair of body tissues.
- Formation of enzymes, hormones and antibodies.
- Maintenance of fluid balance (albumin).
- Energy when carbohydrates and fats are insufficient.

## Daily requirement

About **0.8–1 g per kg body weight per day** for adults. More is needed in pregnancy, lactation, illness, injury and surgery.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **High-protein diet** — encourage for wound healing, burns, surgery and infection, unless medically restricted.
- **Malnutrition** — assess muscle wasting and oedema; refer to dietetics.
- **Kidney disease** — protein may need to be restricted; follow the prescribed diet.
- **Patient teaching** — explain protein-rich foods: milk, eggs, fish, meat, dal, pulses, soya.
- **Supplementary proteins** — advise combining cereals with pulses for better quality protein.

- Functions: growth, repair, enzymes, hormones, antibodies, fluid balance.
