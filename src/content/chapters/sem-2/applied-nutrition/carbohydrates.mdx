## In simple words

Carbohydrates are the body’s cheapest and main source of energy. One gram gives about **4 kilocalories (kcal)**. They are found mainly in plant foods — cereals, roots, fruits and sugar.

Carbohydrates are classified by size: monosaccharides, disaccharides and polysaccharides. Complex carbohydrates (starch, fibre) are healthier than refined sugar.

## Classification

| Type | Examples | Food sources |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharides | Glucose, fructose, galactose | Fruits, honey |
| Disaccharides | Sucrose, lactose, maltose | Sugar, milk, malt |
| Polysaccharides | Starch, glycogen, cellulose | Rice, wheat, potatoes, vegetables |

## Functions

- Main source of energy for the body and brain.
- Spares protein so protein can be used for growth and repair.
- Fibre (a polysaccharide) helps bowel movement and prevents constipation.
- Helps store energy as glycogen in liver and muscle.

## Daily requirement

About **50–60% of total daily energy** should come from carbohydrates, mostly complex carbohydrates, not refined sugar.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Diabetic diet** — advise consistent carbohydrate intake; prefer complex carbs over sugar.
- **Energy needs** — ensure sick patients get enough carbohydrates to spare protein for healing.
- **Constipation** — encourage fibre-rich foods (whole grains, vegetables, fruits) and fluids.
- **Weight management** — excess refined sugar and starch leads to weight gain.
- **Patient teaching** — explain the difference between healthy carbs (whole grains) and unhealthy carbs (sweets, soft drinks).
