## In simple words

A balanced diet provides all nutrients in correct amounts and proportions. It includes cereals, pulses, milk, vegetables, fruits, fats and sugars in the right mix.

Meal planning means selecting and combining foods so that each day’s intake meets nutrient needs within the family budget.

## Food groups (ICMR)

| Food group | Examples | Main nutrients |
|---|---|---|
| Cereals | Rice, wheat, ragi, jowar | Carbohydrates, energy |
| Pulses | Dal, beans, soya | Protein |
| Milk and products | Milk, curd, paneer | Calcium, protein |
| Vegetables | Leafy greens, roots | Vitamins, minerals, fibre |
| Fruits | Banana, apple, orange | Vitamins, minerals |
| Fats and oils | Oil, ghee, nuts | Energy, fat-soluble vitamins |
| Sugar and jaggery | Sugar, jaggery | Energy |

## Food pyramid

The food pyramid shows proportions: eat most from the base (cereals, vegetables, fruits), less from the middle (milk, pulses, meat), and least from the top (fats, oils, sugar).

## Meal planning points

- Include all food groups every day.
- Use seasonal and local foods — cheaper and fresher.
- Consider age, activity, health and budget.
- Pregnant women, children and sick people need special planning.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Diet counselling** — explain balanced diet using the food pyramid; keep it simple and practical.
- **Hospital diets** — ensure the patient’s meal includes all food groups as prescribed.
- **Budget planning** — suggest low-cost nutrient-rich foods (seasonal vegetables, local pulses, eggs).
- **Special needs** — plan for pregnancy, lactation, infancy, old age and illness.
- **Patient teaching** — teach the “plate method”: half vegetables/fruits, quarter cereals, quarter protein.
