## In simple words

Cooking makes food safe, tasty and digestible, but it can also destroy nutrients. Using the right cooking methods preserves nutrients.

Food preservation keeps food safe to eat for longer by stopping or slowing the growth of microbes.

## Cooking methods

| Method | Effect on nutrients |
|---|---|
| Steaming | Best; minimal nutrient loss |
| Pressure cooking | Good; fast, less water |
| Boiling | Some water-soluble vitamins lost |
| Frying | Adds fat; some nutrient loss |
| Roasting/Baking | Some vitamin loss |

## Minimizing nutrient loss

- Wash vegetables before cutting, not after.
- Cut into large pieces; cook soon after cutting.
- Use minimum water; do not overcook.
- Use the cooking water in soups or gravies (it contains leached vitamins).
- Avoid adding baking soda to vegetables (destroys vitamin C).

## Food preservation methods

- **Drying/sun-drying** — removes water; microbes cannot grow.
- **Refrigeration** — slows microbial growth.
- **Freezing** — stops most microbial growth.
- **Canning** — heat-sealed; long shelf life.
- **Salting and sugaring** — draw out water; preserve.
- **Pasteurization** — heat treatment of milk to kill germs.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Patient meals** — advise gentle cooking to preserve nutrients in therapeutic diets.
- **Health teaching** — teach families to wash before cutting, avoid excess oil, and not overcook.
- **Food storage** — store cooked food properly; reheat only once.
- **Milk safety** — advise pasteurized or boiled milk.
- **Infection control** — ensure kitchen hygiene and safe food handling.
