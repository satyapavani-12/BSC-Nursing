## In simple words

Fats are a concentrated source of energy. One gram of fat gives about **9 kcal** — more than twice that of carbohydrates or protein. Fats also form cell membranes, carry fat-soluble vitamins and make hormones.

Fats are classified as saturated, unsaturated and trans fats. Saturated and trans fats raise heart disease risk; unsaturated fats are healthier.

## Types of fats

| Type | Examples | Health effect |
|---|---|---|
| Saturated | Ghee, butter, coconut oil, red meat | Raise blood cholesterol |
| Unsaturated | Vegetable oils, fish, nuts | Generally healthier |
| Trans | Vanaspati, fried snacks, baked goods | Worst for heart health |

## Functions

- Concentrated energy (9 kcal per gram).
- Form cell membranes and nerve sheaths.
- Carry fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K.
- Make hormones.
- Insulate and protect organs.

## Essential fatty acids

Linoleic acid and alpha-linolenic acid cannot be made by the body and must come from food (vegetable oils, fish, nuts).

## Daily requirement

About **20–30% of total daily energy** should come from fats, mostly unsaturated. Saturated fat should be less than 10% of energy.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Heart-healthy diet** — advise less ghee, butter and fried food; more vegetable oils, fish and nuts.
- **Weight management** — fat is energy-dense; excess leads to obesity.
- **Fat-soluble vitamins** — vitamins A, D, E and K need fat for absorption.
- **Patient teaching** — explain that not all fat is bad; choose unsaturated over saturated and trans fats.
- **Cholesterol** — high blood cholesterol raises heart disease risk; counsel on diet and medication.
