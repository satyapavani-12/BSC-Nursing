## In simple words

A therapeutic diet is a meal plan modified to treat a disease. It changes nutrients, texture, amount or frequency of food to support medical treatment.

Common therapeutic diets: diabetic, renal, cardiac, low-sodium, high-protein, soft and liquid diets.

## Common therapeutic diets

| Diet | Purpose | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetic | Control blood sugar | Regular meals, limited sugar, complex carbs |
| Renal | Reduce kidney workload | Restricted protein, sodium, potassium |
| Cardiac | Protect heart | Low saturated fat, low salt, low cholesterol |
| Low-sodium | Control blood pressure/oedema | Less salt, avoid processed foods |
| High-protein | Healing, growth | More milk, eggs, pulses, meat |
| Soft | Chewing/swallowing difficulty | Mashed, soft-textured food |
| Liquid | Cannot chew | Clear or full liquid; easy to swallow |

## Nurse’s role in diet therapy

- Explain the diet to the patient and family.
- Ensure the prescribed diet is served correctly.
- Monitor food intake and weight.
- Report poor intake or diet-related problems.
- Coordinate with the dietitian and kitchen.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Diabetic patient** — serve meals on time; never skip meals if on insulin; limit sweets.
- **Renal patient** — follow protein/sodium/potassium restrictions as prescribed.
- **Cardiac patient** — serve low-fat, low-salt meals; avoid fried food.
- **Post-surgery** — progress from liquid → soft → normal diet as tolerated.
- **Poor intake** — report and offer alternatives within the diet prescription.

## 60-second revision

- Therapeutic diet = modified to treat disease.
- Diabetic: limit sugar; Renal: restrict protein/salt; Cardiac: low fat/salt.
- High-protein = healing; Soft/liquid = texture.
- Nurse: explain, serve, monitor, report.
- Progress: liquid → soft → normal.
