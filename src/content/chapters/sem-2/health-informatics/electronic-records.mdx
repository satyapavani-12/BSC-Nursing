## In simple words

Electronic health records replace paper charts with digital records. They improve legibility, access and sharing of patient information — but they must be used carefully to protect privacy.

EHR documentation should be accurate, timely and complete — just like paper records.

## Advantages of EHR

| Advantage | What it means |
|---|---|
| Legibility | No handwriting errors |
| Access | Authorised staff can view from anywhere |
| Sharing | Easy to share between departments |
| Decision support | Alerts, reminders, drug interaction checks |
| Storage | Less physical space needed |

## Safe EHR documentation

- Log in with your own ID and password.
- Document in real time or as soon as possible.
- Do not copy-paste without checking accuracy.
- Chart only what you did or observed.
- Log out when finished.

## Privacy and security

- Access only records of patients in your care.
- Do not share passwords.
- Log off or lock the computer when away.
- Report privacy breaches immediately.
- Follow hospital policy and data-protection law.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Charting** — document in the EHR as you give care, not at end of shift.
- **Medication administration** — scan and check against the eMAR.
- **Viewing results** — check lab and imaging results promptly.
- **Privacy** — do not discuss patient information in public areas.
- **Breaches** — report any suspected privacy breach to the privacy officer.

## 60-second revision

- EHR = digital patient chart.
- Advantages: legible, quick access, sharing, decision support.
- Document in real time; chart only what you did.
- Access your patients’ records only.
- Lock computer, don’t share passwords, report breaches.
