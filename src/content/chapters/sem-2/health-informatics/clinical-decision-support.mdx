## In simple words

Clinical decision support (CDS) gives clinicians knowledge and patient-specific information at the right time, to improve decisions. It includes alerts, reminders, guidelines and drug interaction checks.

CDS supports — never replaces — clinical judgement. The nurse remains responsible for decisions.

## CDS tools

| Tool | Example |
|---|---|
| Alerts | Drug allergy, interaction warnings |
| Reminders | Due vaccines, overdue results |
| Guidelines | Evidence-based care pathways |
| Order sets | Standard orders for a condition |
| Calculators | Drug dosage, fluid calculators |

## Safe use of CDS

- Treat CDS as a support tool, not a replacement for judgement.
- Check patient-specific details (weight, allergies) before acting on a calculator.
- Do not ignore alerts without reason — review and document why.
- Report alert fatigue or faulty alerts.
- Use evidence-based resources for practice.

## Benefits

- Fewer medication errors.
- Better adherence to guidelines.
- Faster access to evidence.
- Improved patient outcomes.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Medication administration** — heed allergy and interaction alerts.
- **Vaccines and screening** — act on reminder alerts for due items.
- **Care plans** — use guidelines and order sets as a starting point, then individualise.
- **Drug calculations** — use calculators but double-check manually for high-risk drugs.
- **Evidence** — look up current guidelines before changing practice.
