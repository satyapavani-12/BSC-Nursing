## In simple words

Enzymes are proteins that speed up chemical reactions in the body. When cells are injured or die, enzymes leak into the blood. Measuring blood enzymes helps diagnose and monitor disease.

Each tissue has characteristic enzymes. Finding a tissue-specific enzyme in high amounts points to damage in that organ.

## Important diagnostic enzymes

| Enzyme | Main source | Clinical significance when raised |
|---|---|---|
| ALT | Liver | Liver cell damage (hepatitis) |
| AST | Liver, heart, muscle | Liver or heart damage |
| ALP | Liver, bone | Liver or bone disease |
| CPK / CK | Heart, skeletal muscle, brain | Muscle damage; myocardial infarction |
| LDH | Many tissues | General cell damage, haemolysis |
| Amylase | Pancreas, salivary glands | Pancreatitis |
| Lipase | Pancreas | Pancreatitis (more specific) |

## Enzymes in myocardial infarction

In a heart attack, heart muscle cells die and release enzymes:

- **CK-MB** — rises within 3–6 hours, peaks at 24 hours.
- **Troponin I/T** — rises within 3–6 hours, stays elevated for 7–10 days; most specific for MI.
- **LDH** — rises later, peaks at 48–72 hours.

Troponin is now the preferred test for diagnosing myocardial infarction.

## Enzymes in liver disease

- **ALT** is more specific for liver cell damage than AST.
- **AST** is also in heart and muscle.
- **ALP** rises in obstructive liver disease and bone disease.
- Raised ALT/AST with high ALP suggests cholestasis.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Blood samples** — collect enzyme samples at the right time; timing matters for interpretation.
- **Chest pain** — act fast; troponin results guide treatment of myocardial infarction.
- **Patient teaching** — explain why blood tests are repeated over hours in suspected heart attack.
- **Liver disease** — watch for jaundice, bleeding and confusion; report abnormal liver enzymes.
- **Medication effects** — some drugs (statins, paracetamol in overdose) raise liver enzymes.

## 60-second revision

- Enzymes leak from injured cells into blood.
- ALT/AST = liver; ALT more specific.
- Troponin = heart attack (most specific).
- Amylase/lipase = pancreatitis.
- ALP = liver or bone.
- Troponin rises 3–6 hours, stays 7–10 days.
