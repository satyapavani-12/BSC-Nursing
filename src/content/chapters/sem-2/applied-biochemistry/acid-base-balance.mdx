## In simple words

Acid–base balance keeps the blood pH in a narrow range. The normal blood pH is **7.35–7.45**. Below 7.35 is acidosis; above 7.45 is alkalosis.

The body uses buffers, the lungs and the kidneys to keep pH steady. Even small changes affect enzyme work and cell function.

## Buffer systems

Buffers resist pH change:

| Buffer | Where it acts | Main role |
|---|---|---|
| Bicarbonate (H2CO3 / HCO3-) | Blood (extracellular) | Main buffer; linked to lungs and kidneys |
| Proteins | Inside cells and blood | Bind or release hydrogen ions |
| Phosphate | Inside cells and urine | Important in renal tubules |
| Haemoglobin | Red blood cells | Binds hydrogen ions in blood |

## Regulation of pH

- **Lungs** — control carbon dioxide (CO2). Fast breathing blows off CO2 and raises pH; slow breathing retains CO2 and lowers pH. Acts in minutes.
- **Kidneys** — excrete or reabsorb hydrogen ions and bicarbonate. Acts over hours to days.
- **Buffers** — act immediately (seconds).

## Common imbalances

| Condition | pH | Main cause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Respiratory acidosis | Low | CO2 retention | Hypoventilation, COPD |
| Respiratory alkalosis | High | CO2 loss | Hyperventilation, anxiety |
| Metabolic acidosis | Low | Acid gain or bicarbonate loss | Diabetic ketoacidosis, diarrhoea |
| Metabolic alkalosis | High | Acid loss or bicarbonate gain | Vomiting, excess antacids |

## Where the nurse uses this

- **ABG sampling** — collect arterial blood gas as ordered; send promptly on ice.
- **Diabetic ketoacidosis** — metabolic acidosis; watch for deep fast breathing (Kussmaul), dehydration and confusion.
- **Vomiting** — loss of stomach acid causes metabolic alkalosis; monitor potassium.
- **COPD** — CO2 retention causes respiratory acidosis; give oxygen as prescribed, not freely.
- **Patient teaching** — explain why breathing pattern and urine matter in acid–base balance.

  Balance: **“Bicarb is base, CO2 is acid.”** Bicarbonate raises pH; carbon dioxide lowers pH.

## 60-second revision

- Normal blood pH: 7.35–7.45.
- Acidosis < 7.35; alkalosis > 7.45.
- Bicarbonate buffer; lungs control CO2; kidneys control bicarbonate.
- Respiratory acidosis = CO2 retention.
- Metabolic acidosis = acid gain or bicarbonate loss (DKA, diarrhoea).
