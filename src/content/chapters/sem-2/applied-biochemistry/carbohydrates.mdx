## In simple words

Carbohydrates are the body’s main source of energy. They are made of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, and are broken down into glucose, which cells use for fuel.

Carbohydrates come in three sizes: monosaccharides (single sugar), disaccharides (two sugars) and polysaccharides (many sugars). Most dietary carbohydrate is digested to glucose and absorbed into the blood.

## Types of carbohydrates

| Type | Examples | Main feature |
|---|---|---|
| Monosaccharides | Glucose, fructose, galactose | Single sugar units; simplest form |
| Disaccharides | Sucrose, lactose, maltose | Two sugar units joined together |
| Polysaccharides | Starch, glycogen, cellulose | Many sugar units; storage or fibre |

Common food sources:
- Starch: rice, wheat, potatoes, bread
- Sugar: table sugar, jaggery, honey, fruits
- Fibre: whole grains, vegetables, fruits

## Digestion and absorption

Carbohydrate digestion begins in the mouth with salivary amylase and continues in the small intestine with pancreatic amylase. Brush-border enzymes (maltase, sucrase, lactase) split disaccharides into monosaccharides.

Glucose, galactose and fructose are absorbed through the villi of the small intestine into the blood capillaries and travel to the liver through the portal vein.

## Blood glucose regulation

- **Insulin** (pancreas) lowers blood glucose by moving it into cells and storing it as glycogen.
- **Glucagon** (pancreas) raises blood glucose by making the liver break down glycogen.
- Normal fasting blood glucose: **70–110 mg/dL**.
- Persistently high blood glucose = diabetes mellitus.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Diabetic diet** — explain meal timing and carbohydrate consistency; refer to dietetics.
- **Blood glucose monitoring** — check as ordered, recognize hypoglycaemia (sweating, tremor, confusion).
- **IV fluids** — know which solutions contain glucose; never add drugs to blood.
- **Patient teaching** — teach signs of high and low blood glucose and when to seek help.
- **Fasting before tests** — ensure the patient stays nil by mouth when a fasting glucose is ordered.
