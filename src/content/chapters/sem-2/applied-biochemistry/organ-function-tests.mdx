## In simple words

Organ function tests use blood and urine to check how well an organ is working. They help diagnose disease, monitor treatment and assess organ health before surgery.

Common panels: renal (kidney), liver (hepatic) and cardiac function tests.

## Kidney function tests

| Test | Normal value | Indicates |
|---|---|---|
| Blood urea | 15–40 mg/dL | Kidney function, protein breakdown |
| Serum creatinine | 0.6–1.2 mg/dL | Kidney filtration |
| Serum uric acid | 3–7 mg/dL | Purine metabolism, gout risk |
| Urine output | 0.5–1 mL/kg/hour | Kidney perfusion |

## Liver function tests

| Test | Normal value | Indicates |
|---|---|---|
| ALT | below 40 units/L | Liver cell damage |
| AST | below 40 units/L | Liver, heart, muscle |
| Alkaline phosphatase | 30–120 units/L | Liver, bone |
| Serum bilirubin | 0.3–1.2 mg/dL | Liver, bile duct, haemolysis |
| Serum albumin | 3.5–5.0 g/dL | Liver synthesis, nutrition |

## Cardiac markers

| Marker | Significance |
|---|---|
| Troponin I/T | Myocardial infarction (most specific) |
| CK-MB | Muscle and heart damage |
| LDH | General cell damage |

<Callout type="must" title="Creatinine and kidney function">
  **Serum creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dL reflects kidney filtration.** Rising creatinine suggests worsening kidney function.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Sample collection** — collect blood or urine at the right time; label correctly; send promptly.
- **Fasting tests** — some tests (glucose, lipids) need fasting; instruct the patient.
- **Monitor trends** — a rising creatinine or bilirubin over days matters more than one value.
- **Patient teaching** — explain what the test is for and why results take time.
- **Safety** — follow standard precautions when collecting samples.

## 60-second revision

- Organ function tests: kidney, liver, heart.
- Kidney: urea, creatinine, uric acid.
- Liver: ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin, albumin.
- Heart: troponin, CK-MB, LDH.
- Creatinine 0.6–1.2; bilirubin 0.3–1.2 mg/dL.
