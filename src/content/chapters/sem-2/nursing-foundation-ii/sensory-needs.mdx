## In simple words

Sensory needs are the need for input from sight, sound, touch, taste and smell. Too little stimulation (sensory deprivation) or too much (sensory overload) can cause confusion, anxiety and distress.

Hospital patients are at risk — isolation, tubes, monitors and restricted movement affect sensory input.

## Sensory deprivation vs overload

| Type | Cause | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory deprivation | Too little input | Confusion, hallucinations, boredom, restlessness |
| Sensory overload | Too much input | Irritability, anxiety, inability to focus, fatigue |

## Nursing care

- **Sensory deprivation** — provide stimulation: talk, read, television, visitors, change of position, window view.
- **Sensory overload** — reduce stimulation: dim lights, lower noise, limit visitors, rest periods.
- **Assess** — hearing, vision, speech, response to stimuli.
- **Communication** — speak clearly, face the patient, check understanding.

<Callout type="must" title="Sensory balance">
  **Patients need balanced sensory input.** Too little causes deprivation (confusion, hallucinations); too much causes overload (irritability, anxiety).
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Isolated patients** — provide stimulation; encourage visits; talk during care.
- **ICU patients** — monitor for delirium; reduce alarms and noise at night; orient to time and place.
- **Hearing/vision impaired** — use glasses and hearing aids; speak clearly; face the patient.
- **Confused patients** — reorient frequently; keep familiar objects nearby.
- **Rest periods** — cluster care to allow uninterrupted rest.

## 60-second revision

- Sensory needs = input from sight, sound, touch, taste, smell.
- Deprivation = too little; overload = too much.
- Deprivation signs: confusion, hallucinations.
- Overload signs: irritability, anxiety.
- Care: stimulate if deprived; reduce if overloaded.
