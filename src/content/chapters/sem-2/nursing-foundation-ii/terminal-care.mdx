## In simple words

Terminal care is care for patients who are dying. The goal is comfort, dignity and relief from pain and distress — not cure. This is palliative care.

Nurses support the patient and family through dying and after death, including grief support.

## Signs of approaching death

- Decreased consciousness, more sleeping.
- Breathing changes (Cheyne–Stokes, long pauses).
- Cool hands and feet; mottled skin.
- Reduced intake of food and fluids.
- Loss of bladder and bowel control.

## Care after death

- Close the eyes; straighten the limbs.
- Wash and dress the body; position respectfully.
- Label the body correctly.
- Support the family; allow time with the body.
- Follow hospital and legal procedures (death certificate, autopsy consent).

## Grief support

- Listen; allow the family to express feelings.
- Do not say “I know how you feel.”
- Provide privacy and time.
- Refer to counselling if needed.

  **The goal of terminal care is comfort and dignity — not cure.** Relieve pain and distress; support the patient and family.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Pain and symptom control** — give analgesia as prescribed; keep the patient comfortable.
- **Mouth care** — keep the mouth moist; clean regularly.
- **Positioning** — turn regularly; use pillows for comfort.
- **Family presence** — allow the family to be with the patient; provide privacy.
- **After death** — provide respectful care of the body; support the family’s grief.

## 60-second revision

- Terminal care = comfort, dignity, pain relief (palliative).
- Signs of death: more sleeping, breathing changes, cool limbs, reduced intake.
- After death: close eyes, wash, dress, label, support family.
- Grief: listen, privacy, no judgement, refer if needed.
- Goal is not cure.
