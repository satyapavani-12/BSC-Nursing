## In simple words

Self-concept is how a person sees themselves — self-esteem, body image and role. Illness, injury and hospitalisation can damage self-concept.

Nurses support self-concept by respecting the patient, encouraging independence and promoting a positive body image.

## Components of self-concept

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Self-esteem | How much a person values themselves |
| Body image | How a person sees their own body |
| Role performance | How well a person performs their roles (parent, worker) |
| Personal identity | Who a person believes they are |

## Factors affecting self-concept

- Illness, injury, disability.
- Surgery (mastectomy, amputation, stoma).
- Ageing, pregnancy, menopause.
- Culture, religion, social status.
- Criticism or praise from others.

## Nursing care

- Encourage independence in self-care.
- Praise effort and progress.
- Respect privacy and dignity.
- Help the patient adapt to body changes (stoma, scar, amputation).
- Encourage expression of feelings.

<Callout type="must" title="Respect dignity">
  **Respect the patient’s dignity and privacy.** Encourage independence and praise effort — this protects self-concept during illness.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Body changes** — support patients with stomas, amputations, scars or mastectomy; teach self-care.
- **Independence** — encourage self-care as able; do not do everything for the patient.
- **Communication** — speak respectfully; avoid criticism; praise progress.
- **Privacy** — provide privacy during procedures and personal care.
- **Refer** — refer to counselling if the patient is severely distressed about body changes.
