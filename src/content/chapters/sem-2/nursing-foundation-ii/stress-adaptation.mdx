## In simple words

Stress is the body’s response to any demand or threat. A stressor is anything that causes stress — illness, pain, hospitalisation, noise, worry.

The body responds through the general adaptation syndrome (GAS): alarm, resistance and exhaustion. Coping helps the person adapt.

## General adaptation syndrome (Selye)

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Alarm | “Fight or flight” — heart rate up, adrenaline |
| Resistance | Body adapts; coping continues |
| Exhaustion | Resources depleted; illness or burnout |

## Coping strategies

- **Problem-focused** — tackle the cause directly.
- **Emotion-focused** — manage the feelings (talk, pray, relax).
- **Adaptation** — adjusting to a new health state (e.g., living with diabetes).

## Nurse’s role

- Identify stressors and help reduce them.
- Teach relaxation — deep breathing, music, rest.
- Support coping — listen, involve family, encourage.
- Watch for exhaustion — burnout in patients and staff.

  **Prolonged stress leads to exhaustion — illness, burnout or breakdown.** Recognise early and support coping before it reaches this stage.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Identify stressors** — pain, noise, worry, financial concerns, family issues.
- **Teach relaxation** — deep breathing, guided imagery, rest.
- **Support adaptation** — help the patient adjust to a new diagnosis (e.g., diabetes).
- **Watch staff burnout** — encourage breaks, teamwork and support for colleagues.
- **Refer** — refer to counsellors for severe stress or anxiety.

## 60-second revision

- Stress = response to demand or threat; stressor = cause.
- GAS: alarm → resistance → exhaustion.
- Alarm = fight or flight; exhaustion = depleted resources.
- Coping: problem-focused and emotion-focused.
- Adaptation = adjusting to new health state.
