## In simple words

Nursing theories give a framework for understanding what nurses do and why. They guide assessment, care planning and evaluation.

Major theorists: Nightingale (environment), Peplau (nurse–patient relationship), Orem (self-care), Henderson (14 basic needs), Roy (adaptation).

## Major nursing theories

| Theorist | Theory | Key idea |
|---|---|---|
| Nightingale | Environmental theory | Clean air, light, warmth, cleanliness, quiet, food |
| Peplau | Interpersonal relations | Nurse–patient relationship in phases |
| Orem | Self-care deficit | Nurses help when patients cannot care for themselves |
| Henderson | 14 basic needs | Assist with 14 fundamental needs |
| Roy | Adaptation model | Help the person adapt to changes |

## Orem’s self-care deficit theory

- **Self-care** — activities a person does to maintain health.
- **Self-care deficit** — when a person cannot meet their own needs.
- **Nursing system** — wholly compensatory, partly compensatory or supportive-educative.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Assessment** — use theories to organise data collection.
- **Care planning** — choose interventions based on theory (e.g., Orem: how much help does the patient need?).
- **Environment** — apply Nightingale: clean, quiet, well-ventilated wards.
- **Relationship** — apply Peplau: build trust through the phases of the relationship.
- **Evaluation** — check whether the patient’s needs are met.
