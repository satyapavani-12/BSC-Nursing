## In simple words

Elimination is the removal of body waste — urine and faeces. Nurses help patients who cannot eliminate independently and observe for abnormalities.

Normal urinary output is about 0.5–1 mL/kg/hour. Normal bowel pattern varies — from 3 times a day to 3 times a week.

## Normal patterns

| Elimination | Normal pattern |
|---|---|
| Urination | 0.5–1 mL/kg/hour; pale yellow urine |
| Bowel movement | 3 times/day to 3 times/week; formed stool |

## Assisting with elimination

- **Bedpan/urinal** — offer regularly; position correctly; provide privacy.
- **Urinary catheter** — insert as prescribed; keep bag below bladder; maintain asepsis.
- **Toilet/commode** — assist mobile patients; use grab bars.
- **Enema** — give as prescribed for constipation or bowel preparation.

## Common problems

- **Retention** — unable to empty bladder; report; may need catheterisation.
- **Incontinence** — involuntary loss; protect skin; use pads; toilet regularly.
- **Constipation** — hard, infrequent stools; fluids, fibre, mobility.
- **Diarrhoea** — frequent loose stools; prevent dehydration; record amount and character.

<Callout type="must" title="Catheter bag position">
  **Keep the urinary drainage bag below the bladder level at all times.** This prevents urine flowing back and causing infection.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Toileting schedule** — offer the bedpan or toilet regularly, not only when asked.
- **Catheter care** — keep the bag below bladder level; empty regularly; maintain asepsis.
- **Measure output** — record urine volume and faeces character.
- **Prevent constipation** — encourage fluids, fibre and mobility.
- **Skin care** — keep the perineal area clean and dry, especially in incontinence.

## 60-second revision

- Urinary output: 0.5–1 mL/kg/hour.
- Bowel: 3/day to 3/week normal.
- Assist: bedpan, urinal, commode, catheter.
- Catheter bag below bladder level.
- Problems: retention, incontinence, constipation, diarrhoea.
