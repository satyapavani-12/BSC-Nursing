## In simple words

Hygiene keeps the patient clean, comfortable and prevents infection. It includes oral care, hair care, nail care, skin care and bathing.

Pressure ulcers (bedsores) develop from prolonged pressure on skin, especially over bony areas. Prevention is easier than treatment.

## Pressure ulcer stages

| Stage | Appearance |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Redness that does not blanch |
| Stage 2 | Partial-thickness skin loss (blister or shallow crater) |
| Stage 3 | Full-thickness skin loss (deep crater) |
| Stage 4 | Tissue loss with exposed bone, tendon or muscle |

## Common pressure points

- Sacrum, heels, elbows, hips, back of head, shoulders, ankles.

## Prevention

- Turn the patient every 2 hours.
- Use pressure-relieving mattresses and cushions.
- Keep skin clean and dry; avoid moisture.
- Massage is NOT recommended over red areas.
- Check skin at least twice daily.

<Callout type="must" title="Turn every 2 hours">
  **Turn bedridden patients every 2 hours to prevent pressure ulcers.** Prevention is much easier than treatment.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Daily hygiene** — bed bath, oral care, hair combing, nail trimming.
- **Skin assessment** — check pressure points at least twice daily.
- **Incontinence care** — clean and dry skin immediately after soiling.
- **Mobility** — encourage movement and exercises; assist with walking.
- **Pressure-relieving devices** — use special mattresses, cushions and heel pads.

## 60-second revision

- Hygiene = clean, comfortable, infection prevention.
- Pressure ulcer stages 1–4.
- Pressure points: sacrum, heels, elbows, hips, head.
- Turn every 2 hours; keep skin clean and dry.
- No massage over red areas.
