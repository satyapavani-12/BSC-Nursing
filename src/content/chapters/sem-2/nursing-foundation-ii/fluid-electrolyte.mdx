## In simple words

Fluid and electrolyte balance keeps the body’s water and salts at the right levels. IV fluids replace lost fluid and electrolytes. Imbalances can cause serious problems and must be corrected quickly.

Fluid is inside cells (intracellular) and outside cells (extracellular — blood plasma and interstitial fluid).

## Common IV fluids

| Fluid | Type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Normal saline 0.9% | Isotonic | Dehydration, blood loss |
| Dextrose 5% | Isotonic (becomes hypotonic) | Provides water, some energy |
| Ringer’s lactate | Isotonic | Dehydration, shock, burns |
| Dextrose saline | Isotonic | Dehydration + energy |

## Electrolytes

| Electrolyte | Normal value | Main role |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium (Na+) | 135–145 mEq/L | Fluid balance, nerve impulses |
| Potassium (K+) | 3.5–5.0 mEq/L | Heart rhythm, muscle contraction |
| Calcium (Ca2+) | 8.5–10.5 mg/dL | Bones, clotting, muscle |
| Magnesium (Mg2+) | 1.7–2.5 mEq/L | Enzyme function, heart |

## Imbalance signs

- **Hyperkalaemia** (high K+) — dangerous heart rhythm changes; watch ECG.
- **Hypokalaemia** (low K+) — weakness, constipation, heart rhythm changes.
- **Hyponatraemia** (low Na+) — confusion, seizures.
- **Dehydration** — dry mouth, poor skin turgor, low urine output.

<Callout type="must" title="Potassium is dangerous">
  **High potassium (hyperkalaemia) can cause fatal heart rhythm changes.** Check ECG and report immediately; never give IV potassium rapidly without monitoring.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **IV therapy** — check the fluid, rate and site; watch for infiltration and phlebitis.
- **Electrolyte replacement** — give as prescribed; monitor response.
- **Dehydration** — recognise signs (dry mouth, poor turgor, low urine); report.
- **Potassium** — never give IV push; dilute and infuse slowly with ECG monitoring.
- **Intake-output chart** — record all fluids in and out.

## 60-second revision

- Fluid compartments: intracellular + extracellular.
- IV fluids: normal saline, D5%, Ringer’s lactate, dextrose saline.
- Na+ 135–145; K+ 3.5–5.0 mEq/L.
- High K+ = dangerous heart rhythm; never IV push potassium.
- Dehydration: dry mouth, poor turgor, low urine output.
