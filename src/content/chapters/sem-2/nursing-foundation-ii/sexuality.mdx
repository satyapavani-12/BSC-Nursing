## In simple words

Sexuality is a core part of being human — it includes gender identity, sexual orientation, intimacy, relationships and reproduction. It continues throughout life, including during illness and older age.

Illness, surgery, medicines and hospitalisation can affect sexuality. Nurses should address sexuality with respect, privacy and without judgement.

## Sexuality across the lifespan

| Stage | Focus |
|---|---|
| Childhood | Learning about body and privacy |
| Adolescence | Puberty, identity, relationships |
| Adulthood | Intimacy, reproduction, relationships |
| Older age | Continued intimacy; may need adaptation |

## Effects of illness on sexuality

- Surgery (mastectomy, hysterectomy, prostate surgery) — body image changes.
- Medicines — may reduce libido or cause erectile dysfunction.
- Pain, fatigue and anxiety — reduce sexual interest.
- Disability — may need positioning aids or new ways of intimacy.

## Nurse’s role

- Provide privacy for couples.
- Answer questions honestly and respectfully.
- Do not assume all patients are heterosexual — avoid assumptions.
- Refer to counsellors or doctors when needed.
- Keep information confidential.

<Callout type="must" title="Respect and privacy">
  **Treat sexuality with respect and privacy.** Do not make assumptions about a patient’s orientation or activity; keep all discussions confidential.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Privacy** — provide privacy for married couples; knock before entering.
- **Assessment** — ask about sexual health concerns when relevant; do not judge.
- **Education** — give information on safe sex, contraception or sexual changes after illness.
- **Referral** — refer to doctors or counsellors for sexual problems.
- **Confidentiality** — never discuss a patient’s sexual matters with others.
