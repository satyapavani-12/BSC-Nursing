## In simple words

Oxygenation is the process of getting oxygen into the blood and tissues. Patients with breathing problems may need oxygen therapy, suctioning or chest physiotherapy.

Oxygen is a drug — it must be given as prescribed, with the right flow rate and device.

## Oxygen delivery devices

| Device | Flow rate | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Nasal cannula | 1–6 L/min | Mild hypoxia, long-term |
| Simple face mask | 5–10 L/min | Moderate hypoxia |
| Venturi mask | Fixed % | Precise oxygen concentration |
| Non-rebreather mask | 10–15 L/min | Severe hypoxia |

Normal SpO2: **95–100%**. Give oxygen as prescribed; do not change flow without orders.

## Suctioning

Suction removes secretions from the mouth, nose or airway when the patient cannot cough. Use asepsis, pre-oxygenate, suction for less than 15 seconds, and monitor.

## Chest physiotherapy

- **Percussion** — clapping over lung segments to loosen mucus.
- **Vibration** — gentle shaking during expiration.
- **Postural drainage** — positions to drain specific lung areas.
- Helps after surgery, in pneumonia and COPD.

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Check SpO2** — monitor regularly; report below 90%.
- **Humidify oxygen** — prevents dryness of the nose and airway.
- **Position** — Fowler’s position improves breathing.
- **Suctioning** — aseptic technique, pre-oxygenate, limit suction time.
- **Fire safety** — no smoking or flames near oxygen.

## 60-second revision

- Oxygenation = oxygen into blood and tissues.
- Oxygen is a drug — give as prescribed.
- Nasal cannula 1–6 L/min; simple mask 5–10; non-rebreather 10–15.
- Normal SpO2: 95–100%.
- Humidify; Fowler’s position; suction under 15 seconds; no fire near oxygen.
