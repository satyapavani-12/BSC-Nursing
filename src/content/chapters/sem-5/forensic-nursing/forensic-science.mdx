## In simple words

Forensic science applies medical knowledge to legal problems. **Forensic nursing** cares for the victim and the evidence together — without destroying what the court will need.

## Branches of forensic science

- Forensic pathology and toxicology
- Forensic medicine (clinical)
- Criminalistics — fingerprints, ballistics, DNA
- Forensic odontology and anthropology
- Forensic psychology and psychiatry

## Medico-legal cases

- Unnatural death (accident, suicide, homicide)
- Assault and physical violence
- Sexual offences and abuse
- poisoning
- Burns and suspicious injuries
- Custodial deaths, torture and police cases
- Death during or after procedures

## Types of injuries

| Injury | Features |
|---|---|
| Abrasion | Superficial scrape of skin |
| Bruises (contusion) | Blunt force, blood under skin; colour changes |
| Laceration | Tear of skin with ragged edges |
| Incised wound | Clean cut, longer than deep |
| Stab wound | Deep, narrow puncture |
| Firearm injury | Entry and exit wounds |
| Burns | Scalds, flame, electrical, chemical |
| Fractures | Bone injury with or without displacement |

## Violence

- Domestic violence, child abuse, elder abuse
- Sexual violence and trafficking
- Occupational and mob violence
- Nurse’s role: identify, care, protect, document, refer to counselling and legal aid

<Callout type="must" title="Care first, evidence second">
  **Treat life-threatening problems first.** Then, before cleaning, dressing or removing clothes, preserve evidence. Patient care and evidence collection must both be documented with time and signature.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Forensic science branches: pathology, toxicology, criminalistics, odontology.
- Medico-legal cases: unnatural death, assault, abuse, poisoning, burns.
- Types of injuries: abrasion, bruise, laceration, incised, stab, firearm.
- Preserve evidence before cleaning and dressing.
- Document time, findings, signature; never alter.
