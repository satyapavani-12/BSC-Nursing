## In simple words

**Forensic nursing** is the care of patients who are involved in violence, crime or trauma, combined with the skilled preservation of evidence for investigation. The nurse is often the first professional the victim meets.

## Roles of the forensic nurse

- Identify victims of crime and violence early
- Provide care, comfort and safety
- Preserve, collect and document evidence correctly
- Give truthful documentation and testimony
- Support recovery, counselling and legal follow-up
- Participate in a multidisciplinary forensic team
- Advocate for the victim’s rights and dignity

## Chain of custody

Evidence is worthless if its chain is broken. Record for every item:

1. What was collected, from where, by whom
2. Date and time of collection
3. Who received it, when, and where it was stored
4. Any transfer with signatures and seals

## Death certification

- **Medical certificate of cause of death** — issued by a doctor for natural death; used for registration
- **Medico-legal postmortem** — ordered in unnatural or suspicious death; helps in death certificate
- The nurse never issues a death certificate, but assists in documentation

## Care of the body for postmortem

- Confirm and record death; maintain dignity and privacy
- Do not clean or remove artefacts if medico-legal (unless required by protocol)
- Keep tubes, catheters and dresses in place as instructed
- Seal and label all specimens; complete forms
- Hand over the body with documentation to the mortuary

<Callout type="must" title="Broken chain loses the case">
  If a sample is not sealed, labelled, timed and signed, **the court may reject it**. The nurse protects the victim by protecting the chain of custody.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Forensic nursing = victim care plus evidence preservation.
- Roles: identify, care, preserve, document, testify, advocate.
- Chain of custody must be unbroken.
- Death certificate issued by a doctor; nurses document and assist.
- Care of the body for postmortem preserves all evidence.
