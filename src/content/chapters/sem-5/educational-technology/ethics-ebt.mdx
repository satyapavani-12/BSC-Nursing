## In simple words

Ethics guides the teacher. Students learn honesty, respect and safety from what the teacher does, more than from what she says.

## Ethical principles in teaching

| Principle | What it means in class |
|---|---|
| Honesty | Marks only what the student actually did |
| Fairness | Same rules for every student |
| Respect | Respect for students, patients and staff |
| Confidentiality | Student performance and patient care are private |
| Justice | No favouritism, bias or discrimination |
| Integrity | Follow the curriculum and university rules |
| Accountability | The teacher owns the quality of teaching |

## Common problems and correct action

| Situation | Correct action |
|---|---|
| Pressure for higher marks | Mark honestly, record, explain the rule |
| Student unable to do a skill | Continue supervised practice; do not certify unsafe practice |
| Question paper repeats | Set fresh papers from the blueprint |
| Appraisal disagreement | Give evidence-based, respectful feedback |
| Student plagiarism | Address formally with the appropriate authority |
| Bias for a student | Apply the same standard as for all |

## Evidence-based teaching

- Ask what teaching problem exists and what evidence says about it
- Use past results, student feedback and education research
- Try a change, collect data, evaluate and keep what works
- Continually update content as knowledge changes

<Callout type="must" title="Model what you teach">
  Students copy the teacher. If marks are honest, records are true and patients are respected, students learn those habits. **A teacher’s behaviour is a hidden lesson plan.**
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Ethical teaching: honest marks, fairness, respect, confidentiality.
- Correct actions for misconduct and unsafe certification.
- Evidence-based teaching: problem, evidence, trial, evaluate.
- Reflective practice and feedback.
- The teacher is a role model.
