## In simple words

Clinical teaching is education in the real ward, where students learn to care for patients. Its most important outcomes are patient safety and safe, competent practice.

## Clinical teaching methods

| Method | What happens |
|---|---|
| Bedside clinic | Teacher and a few students at the bedside discussing a patient |
| Process recording | Student records an interaction and reflects on it |
| Case presentation | Student presents a patient’s care plan |
| Pre- and post-conference | Before and after clinical practice — plan and review |
| Observation of care | Teacher watches the student giving care |
| Clinical conference | Group discussion on a clinical topic |

## Preparation for clinical posting

- **Pre-conference** — review objectives, theory and patients to be assigned
- **Orientation** — ward layout, equipment, staff, emergency facilities
- **Documents** — uniform, ID, thermometer, stethoscope, notebook, clinical record forms
- **Patient assignment** — match to learning needs; change as the student progresses

## Clinical objective sheet

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Patient details | Identification and consent |
| Medical diagnosis | Context for care |
| Nursing diagnosis | Problems to be solved |
| Objectives for that posting | What the student must achieve |
| Interventions and evaluation | Care given and its effect |
| Student and teacher signature | Accountability for practice |

## Clinical evaluation tools

- **Checklists** — each step performed or not
- **Anecdotal records** — description of observed behaviour
- **Rating scales** — scale of 1 to 5 against criteria
- **Clinical logs** — number and type of procedures
- **Objective structured clinical examination** — stations with scenarios

<Callout type="must" title="Supervision level by level">
  A first-year student **observes and assists**; a final-year student performs with supervision. **Never let a student do what has not been taught and practised** — patient safety is non-negotiable.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Clinical methods: bedside clinic, case presentation, process recording, conferences.
- Prepare: pre-conference, orientation, documents.
- Assign patients by level and safety.
- Evaluate with checklists, ratings and clinical logs.
- Supervise; student performs only what has been taught.
