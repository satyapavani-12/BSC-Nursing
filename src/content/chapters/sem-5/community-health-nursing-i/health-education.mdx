## In simple words

Health education turns knowledge into action. It is a process where people learn to take responsibility for their own health — through information, practice and support.

## Communication

| Element | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Source | Sender with clear ideas and credibility |
| Message | What must change in the learner |
| Channel | Words, pictures, demonstration, actions |
| Receiver | The audience — their language, literacy and beliefs |
| Feedback | Does the learner understand and act? |

- One-way communication: lecture, notice, radio — little feedback
- Two-way communication: discussion, counselling — important feedback
- **Barriers:** language, deafness, blindness, illiteracy, shy audience, wrong timing, distrust, unsigned matter

## Health education process

1. Assess needs: what the family already knows and believes
2. Define the changes to be achieved
3. Select the audience, content and media
4. Implement in the language and setting of the learner
5. Reinforce with practice and follow-up
6. Evaluate: knowledge, behaviour and outcome

## Teaching methods

| Method | Best for |
|---|---|
| Lecture | Large groups, new information |
| Demonstration | Skills such as handwashing or ORS preparation |
| Group discussion | Attitude change and problem solving |
| Role play | Sensitive topics like family acceptance |
| Flip charts, posters | Low-literacy groups and clinics |
| Puppetry and street plays | Illiterate and rural audiences |
| Radio and television | Wide reach |
| Counselling | Individual change |

<Callout type="must" title="Two-way beats one-way">
  Choose discussion, demonstration and role play over lecture. **People believe what they practise, not what they are told.**
</Callout>

  Good health talk: **P-P-R** — **P**resent the need, **P**ractise the skill, **P**raise the change.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Communication elements: source, message, channel, receiver, feedback.
- Process: assess, plan, teach, reinforce, evaluate.
- Methods: lecture, demonstration, discussion, role play, media.
- Two-way methods are more effective than one-way.
- Match language, literacy and culture of the audience.
