## In simple words

The community health nurse reaches people where they live and work. The **home visit** is the basic tool; the survey finds out who needs what, and volunteers keep the care going.

## Home visit

**Purpose:** assess the home and family, provide care, teach, find new problems and establish trust.

**Process:**

1. **Planning** — set priority (newborn, high risk, referral follow-up), time and route
2. **Before entry** — comb hair, wear clean uniform and shoes, greet the family
3. **Introduction** — introduce yourself and state the purpose in simple words
4. **Observation** — people, house, water source, food, sanitation, environment
5. **Nursing care** — examination, teaching, demonstration and recording
6. **Summary** — agree on what the family will do and when you will return
7. **Closure** — thank the family, sign the visit, and record

## Other community approaches

| Approach | What the nurse does |
|---|---|
| Community survey | Lists families, maps needs, sets priorities |
| Health camps | Mass screening, immunisation and referral |
| School health | Immunisation, anaemia and eye checks, hygiene classes |
| Industrial health | Occupational hazard checks, first aid, health education |
| Home-based care | Bed-ridden, elderly and palliative patients |

## Working with volunteers

- **ASHA** — first contact, mobilises families, gives essential medicines
- **Anganwadi worker** — nutrition, preschool and growth monitoring
- **Mahila groups and self-help groups** — support, loans, health education
- **Village health committee** — local decisions and resources
- Nurses train, supervise and never replace these workers

<Callout type="must" title="Do not break the trust of the home">
  Keep every promise, speak in the family’s language, keep what you learn confidential and never compare families unfavourably. Trust is the tool that makes future visits work.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Home visit: plan, enter, observe, assess, teach, close, record.
- Priority: newborns, high-risk mothers, referral follow-up.
- Other approaches: survey, camps, school and industrial health.
- Volunteers: ASHA, Anganwadi, self-help groups, village committee.
- Trust and confidentiality are essential.
