## In simple words

The family, not the individual, is the **basic unit of health care**. Illness of one member affects the whole family, and care works best when the whole family is involved.

## Family types

- **Nuclear family** — husband, wife and children
- **Joint family** — relatives living together with common kitchen
- **Extended family** — nuclear family plus other relatives
- **Single parent / blended / childless** — other accepted forms

## Stages of the family life cycle

1. Marriage (family formation)
2. Childbearing — births in the first years
3. Child rearing — school age and adolescence
4. Launching — children leave home, marriage
5. Empty nest to retirement
6. Aging and death

## Family assessment

| Domain | What to record |
|---|---|
| Structure | Members, ages, education, occupation, married status |
| Housing | Rooms, ventilation, water source, animals, overcrowding |
| Health | Illness, disability, food, medicines, screening due |
| Roles and relationships | Decision-making, communication, support |
| Resources | Income, assets, food availability, transport |
| Health behaviour | Cleanliness, food habits, harmful habits |
| Problems identified | Ranked by priority |

## Family health nursing process

1. Assess the family as a whole
2. Identify and rank health problems
3. Plan care with the family — the family decides
4. Implement: teach, treat, refer, mobilise resources
5. Evaluate and follow up; record in the family folder

<Callout type="must" title="Care for the whole family">
  If the mother has anaemia, treat the whole family’s food habits; if a child is malnourished, teach the family, not just the mother. **The family is the unit of both illness and care.**
</Callout>
