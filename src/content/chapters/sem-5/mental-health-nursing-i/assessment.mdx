## In simple words

The psychiatric assessment answers three questions: **What is the patient’s problem, why now, and how dangerous is it?** The nurse collects the history, examines the mental status and always checks risk.

## Psychiatric history — order

| Part | What is asked |
|---|---|
| Identification | Name, age, sex, education, occupation, religion |
| Presenting complaints | In the patient’s own words, with duration |
| History of present illness | Onset, course, stressor, treatment taken |
| Past psychiatric history | Previous episodes, admissions, suicide attempts |
| Medical history | Fever, injury, drugs, substances, illness |
| Personal history | Birth, childhood, schooling, marital, sexual, work |
| Premorbid personality | Before illness: social, habits, coping |
| Family history | Similar illness in family, family structure |
| Mental status examination | Appearance to thought (see below) |

## Mental status examination (MSE)

| Area | What to observe |
|---|---|
| Appearance and behaviour | Grooming, posture, eye contact, agitation, movement |
| Speech | Rate, volume, tone, pressure of speech, mutism |
| Mood and affect | Stated mood (subjective) and observed affect (objective) |
| Thought form | Flight of ideas, tangentiality, thought block |
| Thought content | Delusions, obsessions, suicidal ideas, paranoid ideas |
| Perception | Hallucinations, illusions — in any of the five senses |
| Cognition | Orientation, attention, concentration, memory |
| Insight and judgement | Does the patient know he is ill? Can he decide safely? |
| Risk | Suicide, violence, self-neglect, vulnerability |

## Risk assessment

**Suicide risk** — ask directly: "Do you feel life is not worth living? Have you thoughts of ending it?" Higher risk with: previous attempt, plan, means available, hopelessness, isolation, male, elderly, command hallucinations.

**Violence risk** — past violence, psychosis with persecutory delusions, intoxication, command hallucinations, agitation, access to weapons.

**Protective factors** — family support, reasons for living (children, faith), responsibility, future plans, good rapport.

## Common rating scales

| Scale | What it measures |
|---|---|
| MMSE | Cognitive function / orientation |
| GDS (Geriatric Depression Scale) | Depression in older adults |
| HAM-D (Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression) | Severity of depression |
| BPRS (Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale) | General psychopathology in psychosis |
| YMRS (Young Mania Rating Scale) | Severity of mania |

<Callout type="must" title="Always ask about suicide">
  Asking about suicide does not plant the idea. **Ask directly and calmly every time** the patient is low, hopeless or has attempted before. Then remove the means and never leave the patient alone.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Admission** — full history, MSE and risk assessment within 24 hours
- **Ward** — continuous observation, shift reports, documentation changes
- **Follow-up** — mood, sleep, appetite, adherence, side effects
- **Community** — screening, referral and family assessment

## 60-second revision

- History: complaints, onset, past, medical, personal, family.
- MSE: appearance, speech, mood/affect, thought, perception, cognition, insight, judgement.
- Risk: suicide, violence, self-neglect; ask directly.
- Scales: MMSE, GDS, HAM-D, BPRS, YMRS.
- Documentation: objective, quoted, timed and signed.
