## In simple words

Schizophrenia is a chronic mental illness that affects thinking, feeling and behaviour. The patient loses contact with reality. It usually starts in late adolescence or early adulthood, and with treatment many people lead useful lives.

It is **not** split personality, and it is **not** madness in the film sense.

## Symptoms

**Positive symptoms (added)**
- Delusions: persecutory, grandiose, reference, control
- Hallucinations: most commonly auditory — voices commenting or commanding
- Disorganised speech: loose associations, word salad, neologisms
- Bizarre or catatonic behaviour

**Negative symptoms (lost)**
- Flat or blunted affect
- Poverty of speech (alogia)
- Social withdrawal
- Lack of motivation (avolition)
- Poor self-care

## Causes

- Genetic (10% risk with a first-degree relative; higher with identical twins)
- Neurochemical — excess dopamine activity
- Brain structure — enlarged ventricles, reduced frontal lobes
- Psychosocial — stress, family conflict, migration, urban living
- Perinatal — maternal infection, malnutrition, obstetric complications

## Treatment

- **Antipsychotics** — first generation (chlorpromazine, haloperidol) and second generation (risperidone, olanzapine)
- **Long-acting injections** for poor adherence
- **ECT** in catatonia or severe agitation
- **Psychosocial** — family education, vocational training, psychoeducation, compliance
- Never believe that stopping medicines early is a small error — relapse risk is high

## Nursing care

- Build a low-stress, structured routine
- Do **not** argue or agree with delusions; state reality calmly: "I do not hear the voice, but I know it is real to you."
- Encourage reality orientation; do not pretend hallucinations are real
- Watch for command hallucinations — act on them risking harm
- Monitor side effects, weight and metabolism
- Support self-care, nutrition and hydration
- Involve the family; teach early warning signs and adherence

<Callout type="must" title="Never argue with a delusion">
  Do not confirm, and do not challenge. **Do not argue, do not laugh and do not pretend you hear it.** Acknowledge the feeling, redirect to reality and report.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Chronic psychotic illness of thought, feeling and behaviour.
- Positive symptoms added; negative symptoms taken away.
- Delusions and auditory hallucinations are common.
- Causes: genetic, dopamine, structural, psychosocial.
- Types: paranoid, catatonic, disorganised, undifferentiated.
- No arguing with delusions; safety first; support adherence.
