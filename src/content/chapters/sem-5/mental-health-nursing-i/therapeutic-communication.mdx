## In simple words

Therapeutic communication is a purposeful conversation that helps the patient — it shares information, builds trust and reduces anxiety. It is the most important tool the mental health nurse has.

## Phases of the nurse-patient relationship

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| Pre-interaction | Nurse prepares — reviews records, examines own feelings |
| Orientation | Introductions, roles, contract, first assessment |
| Working phase | Trust grows; problems explored; interventions used |
| Termination | Review, discharge planning, parting without abandonment |

## Therapeutic techniques

| Technique | Example |
|---|---|
| Active listening | Attending fully with leaning posture and eye contact |
| Silence | Allowing quiet time to think and feel |
| Open questions | "Tell me what has been troubling you?" |
| Clarifying | "You said you feel unsafe. Where do you feel unsafe?" |
| Reflecting | Repeating the last word to encourage more |
| Paraphrasing | Restating the content in simpler words |
| Summarising | Bringing together what was said |
| Conveying acceptance | "I am here. Please take your time." |
| Broad opening | "Where would you like to start today?" |
| Focusing | "It sounds like the night shift was hardest." |

## Non-therapeutic responses (blocks)

- **False reassurance** — "Do not worry, everything is fine"
- **Giving advice** — "If I were you, I would just leave him"
- **Judgemental comments** — "You should not behave like that"
- **Changing the subject** — avoiding the patient’s concern
- **Parroting** — repeating the last word mechanically
- **Defensiveness** — arguing with a delusion
- **Personal comparisons** — "When I was in hospital..."

## Key skills in practice

- **Empathy** — understanding the patient’s feelings without becoming overwhelmed
- **Active listening** — listen to words, tone and body language
- **Genuineness** — be yourself; avoid professional masks
- **Warmth** — a tone and posture that show respect
- **Self-awareness** — know your own reactions before you speak
- **Confidentiality** — share the patient’s information only in care

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Admission interview** — orientation, assessment, contract
- **Individual and group therapy** — use open questions, silence, reflecting
- **Difficult patients** — accept the feeling, address the cause
- **Termination** — summarise, plan follow-up, farewell

<Callout type="must" title="Empathy is not sympathy">
  **Sympathy** feels sorry for the patient; **empathy** understands the patient’s world. Empathy helps; sympathy can make the patient feel weaker.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Relationship phases: pre-interaction, orientation, working, termination.
- Techniques: silence, open questions, clarifying, reflecting, paraphrasing, summarising.
- Blocks: false reassurance, advice, judgement, changing subject.
- Empathy vs sympathy: understand vs feel sorry.
- Be genuine, warm, confidential and self-aware.
