## In simple words

Children collapse mainly because of **breathing problems**, not heart problems. So in paediatric life support (PLS) the first priority is always **airway and breathing** — and early oxygen and ventilation save lives.

The same ABCDE approach is used for any seriously ill child: **A**irway, **B**reathing, **C**irculation, **D**isability, **E**xposure.

## ABCDE approach

| Step | What to check | Immediate action |
|---|---|---|
| A — Airway | Crying, speaking, secretions, foreign body | Suction, head tilt-chin lift, jaw thrust |
| B — Breathing | Rate, effort, indrawing, grunting, colour, SpO2 | Oxygen, position, suction |
| C — Circulation | Pulse, capillary refill, blood pressure | Chest compressions if no pulse, fluids |
| D — Disability | AVPU, pupils, posture, glucose | Treat convulsions, give glucose |
| E — Exposure | Rash, wounds, burns, fever, trauma | Warm the child, control bleeding |

## Choking (foreign body airway obstruction)

**Infant under 1 year:** give **5 back blows** between the shoulder blades, turn the baby and give **5 chest thrusts** with two fingers on the lower sternum. Never do abdominal thrusts on an infant.

**Child over 1 year:** **5 back blows** then **5 abdominal thrusts** (Heimlich manoeuvre) with a fist above the navel.

Alternate 5 strikes and 5 thrusts until the object comes out or the child becomes unresponsive — then start CPR.

## Convulsions

- Call for help, protect from injury, place in the recovery position
- Give oxygen, suction secretions; check glucose
- **Drugs:** diazepam/midazolam per rectum or buccal midazolam; phenytoin infusion for status epilepticus
- Never restrain a convulsing child; never put anything in the mouth
- **Febrile convulsion** is common in 6 months to 5 years with rapid temperature rise — cool the child, antipyretics, reassure the family

## Other emergencies

| Emergency | Key nursing action |
|---|---|
| Shock | Oxygen, IV fluid bolus 20 mL/kg, keep warm, monitor |
| Burns | Stop burning, cool 20 minutes, fluid resuscitation by formula |
| Drowning | Rescue, ventilate, warm; hypothermia protection |
| Poisoning | Identify agent, do not induce vomiting, antidotes |
| Anaphylaxis | Oxygen, IM adrenaline, antihistamine, fluids |
| Dehydration | Plan C fluids, monitor urine and weight |

<Callout type="must" title="CPR ratios in children">
  **15 chest compressions to 2 breaths** with two-rescuer infant CPR; 30:2 with a single rescuer. Rate 100–120 per minute; depth about one third of the chest.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Emergency room** — ABCDE triage, immediate oxygen, choking algorithm
- **Ward** — recognise deterioration early; monitor respiratory rate and level of consciousness
- **Convulsion management** — position, safety, oxygen, drug administration
- **Family teaching** — first aid, choking, fever control, safe play

## 60-second revision

- ABCDE: airway, breathing, circulation, disability, exposure.
- Choking: infant back blows + chest thrusts; child back blows + abdominal thrusts.
- Convulsions: safety, oxygen, buccal or rectal midazolam; never restrain.
- Shock: 20 mL/kg fluid bolus; keep warm.
- Burns: cool 20 minutes; fluid formula; watch urine output.
- CPR: 15:2 with two rescuers; 100-120 per minute.
