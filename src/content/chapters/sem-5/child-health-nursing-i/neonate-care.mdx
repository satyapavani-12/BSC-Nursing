## In simple words

The first hours of life decide how well the newborn adapts to life outside the womb. **Essential Newborn Care (ENBC)** is a set of simple, low-cost steps that almost every baby needs — and it prevents most newborn deaths.

Newborn = birth to 28 days. The first 7 days are the **neonatal period** with the highest risk of death.

## Steps of essential newborn care

| Step | What is done | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Airway | Wipe mouth then nose, position on the back | Clears secretions |
| Breathing | Dry and stimulate; check crying and breathing | Stimulates breathing |
| Circulation | Check cord pulsation, colour, heart rate | Shows perfusion |
| Warmth | Dry, skin-to-skin, cover head, delay bath 24 hours | Prevents hypothermia |
| Cord | Clamp and cut with sterile blade, keep dry | Prevents infection |
| Eyes | Tetracycline/erythromycin eye ointment | Prevents gonococcal eye infection |
| Vitamin K | 1 mg IM (0.5 mg if under 1500 g) within 1 hour | Prevents haemorrhagic disease |
| Feeding | Breastfeed within 1 hour, colostrum first | Immunity and warmth |
| Identification | ID band, footprints, mother’s name | Prevents mix-ups |
| Examination | Weigh, measure, head-to-toe check | Detects abnormalities |

## Apgar score (at 1 and 5 minutes)

| Sign | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate | Absent | Slow (under 100) | Over 100 |
| Respiratory effort | Absent | Slow or irregular | Good cry |
| Muscle tone | Limp | Some flexion | Active movement |
| Reflex irritability | None | Grimace | Cry, sneeze, cough |
| Colour | Blue or pale | Body pink, limbs blue | Fully pink |

**8–10 = normal · 4–7 = needs stimulation and oxygen · 0–3 = needs resuscitation immediately**

## Newborn danger signs

- Not feeding well or refusing feeds
- Breathing difficulty: grunting, chest indrawing, fast breathing (over 60 per minute)
- Cold to touch (hypothermia) or fever
- Convulsions or abnormal movements
- Jaundice on day 1 or palms and soles yellow
- No urine or stool in the first 48 hours
- Lethargy or reduced movement
- Umbilical redness or pus

<Callout type="must" title="Keep the newborn warm">
  **Hypothermia is a killer.** Warm chain: warm delivery room, immediate drying, skin-to-skin contact, warm transport, and delayed bathing for 24 hours.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Delivery room** — assist with airway, drying, cord and Apgar scoring
- **Postnatal ward** — warmth, feeding support, weight monitoring, cord and eye care
- **Discharge teaching** — exclusive breastfeeding, immunisation schedule, danger signs, cord care
- **Any newborn contact** — look, listen and feel before touching

## 60-second revision

- ENBC: airway, breathing, warmth, cord, eyes, vitamin K, feeding, identification.
- Apgar at 1 and 5 minutes; 8-10 normal, 0-3 resuscitate.
- Skin-to-skin, dry, cover head, delay bath 24 hours.
- Cord: sterile cut, keep dry. Eyes: tetracycline ointment.
- Colostrum within 1 hour; exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months.
- Danger signs: poor feeding, fast breathing, cold or hot, jaundice day 1, convulsion, no urine in 48 hours.
