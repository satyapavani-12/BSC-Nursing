## In simple words

Five conditions cause most childhood illness in India: **diarrhoea, pneumonia, measles, malnutrition and anaemia**. All are preventable, and all respond to simple nursing care when caught early.

## Diarrhoeal disease

- **Cause:** rotavirus and contaminated water or food
- **Signs:** loose stools, vomiting, thirst, sunken eyes, reduced urine
- **Assessment:** dehydration — some (thirsty, sunken eyes, skin pinch slow) to severe (lethargy, no pulse, cold)
- **Treatment:** **ORS** (Plan A/B/C by severity), **zinc for 14 days**, continue feeding; IV Ringer lactate in severe cases
- **Prevention:** exclusive breastfeeding, safe water, handwashing, rotavirus vaccine, sanitation

<Callout type="must" title="ORS saves lives">
  Give **ORS after every loose stool** — 50–100 mL under 2 years, 100–200 mL over 2 years. Continue breastfeeding and food. Never give rice water alone or "loose" drinks.
</Callout>

## Pneumonia

- **Cause:** bacterial (pneumococcus) or viral; follows measles and malnutrition
- **Signs:** cough, fast breathing, fever; chest indrawing or grunting = severe
- **Treatment:** amoxycillin; oxygen for severe disease; supportive care
- **Nursing care:** propped-up position, nasal and oral suction, oxygen, fluids, temperature control, watch respiratory rate

## Measles (rubeola)

- **Cause:** measles virus, highly infectious by droplets
- **Order:** fever and cough, coryza, conjunctivitis (three Cs) → Koplik spots (buccal, white) → maculopapular rash from hairline downwards → brownish fading
- **Complications:** pneumonia, diarrhoea, otitis media, encephalitis, malnutrition, blindness from vitamin A deficiency
- **Treatment:** vitamin A, fluids, antipyretic; isolation; MR vaccine at 9 months

## Malnutrition

| Type | Signs |
|---|---|
| Marasmus | Severe wasting, "old man" face, alert and hungry |
| Kwashiorkor | Oedematous feet, flaky paint skin, moon face, apathy |
| Marasmic kwashiorkor | Both wasting and oedema |

-**Assessment:** weight for age, weight for height, MUAC (under 11.5 cm = severe), bilateral oedema
- **Treatment:** therapeutic feeding (RUTF), F-75 then F-100 diet, micronutrients, treat infections

## Anaemia, worms and other conditions

| Condition | Key points |
|---|---|
| Iron deficiency anaemia | Pallor, tiredness; iron and folic acid syrup; Hb check |
| Hookworm | Anaemia, blood in stool; albendazole; footwear, sanitation |
| Roundworm | Worms in stool, abdominal pain; albendazole |
| Scabies | Itching at night; permethrin; treat whole family |
| Conjunctivitis | Red, sticky eyes; hand hygiene, tetracycline ointment |

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Ward and OPD** — early recognition of dehydration, respiratory distress and severe malnutrition
- **Nutrition teaching** — weaning foods, protein-rich foods, F-75 and F-100 feeding
- **Isolation and infection control** — measles, scabies and conjunctivitis
- **Immunisation** — MR, rotavirus, pneumococcal vaccines
- **Health education** — handwashing, safe water, exclusive breastfeeding, footwear

## 60-second revision

- Diarrhoea: ORS + zinc 14 days, continue food; severe = IV Ringer lactate.
- Pneumonia: fast breathing, indrawing, grunting; amoxycillin, oxygen.
- Measles: 3 Cs → Koplik spots → hairline rash; vitamin A; MR vaccine.
- Malnutrition: marasmus (wasting), kwashiorkor (oedema); MUAC, F-75, F-100.
- Anaemia: iron + folic acid; worms: albendazole and footwear.
- Prevention: vaccines, breastfeeding, clean water, sanitation.
