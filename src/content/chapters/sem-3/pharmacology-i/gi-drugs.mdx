## In simple words

GI drugs act on the digestive system to relieve symptoms like acidity, nausea, vomiting, constipation and diarrhoea. Nurses give these drugs and monitor their effects.

Common classes: antacids, antiemetics, laxatives, antidiarrhoeals, antispasmodics.

## Common GI drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Antacids | Aluminium hydroxide, magnesium hydroxide, calcium carbonate | Neutralise stomach acid — heartburn, gastritis |
| H2 blockers / PPIs | Ranitidine, omeprazole, pantoprazole | Reduce acid production — ulcers, GERD |
| Antiemetics | Ondansetron, domperidone, promethazine | Prevent/treat nausea and vomiting |
| Laxatives | Bisacodyl, senna, lactulose, ispaghula | Treat constipation |
| Antidiarrhoeals | Loperamide, oral rehydration salts | Control diarrhoea (ORS prevents dehydration) |
| Antispasmodics | Dicyclomine, hyoscine | Relieve abdominal cramps |

## Nurse responsibilities

- Give antacids at least 1–2 hours apart from other drugs (they can interfere with absorption).
- Give antiemetics before nausea occurs (e.g., before chemotherapy).
- Encourage fluids and fibre for constipation; laxatives short-term only.
- In diarrhoea, prioritise ORS to prevent dehydration — especially in children.
- Monitor for side effects (e.g., constipation from aluminium antacids, diarrhoea from magnesium).

  **In diarrhoea, the priority is oral rehydration salts (ORS) to prevent dehydration** — especially in children. Antidiarrhoeals are secondary.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Antacids** — give 1–2 hours after meals and at bedtime; separate from other drugs.
- **Antiemetics** — give before chemotherapy, surgery or travel to prevent nausea.
- **Laxatives** — encourage fluids, fibre and activity first; use laxatives short-term.
- **Diarrhoea** — start ORS early; avoid antidiarrhoeals in bloody diarrhoea or fever unless ordered.
- **Monitoring** — ask about relief of symptoms and any side effects.

## 60-second revision

- GI drugs: antacids, antiemetics, laxatives, antidiarrhoeals, antispasmodics.
- Antacids neutralise acid; PPIs reduce acid production.
- Antiemetics prevent/treat nausea and vomiting.
- Laxatives = constipation; antidiarrhoeals = diarrhoea.
- ORS = priority for diarrhoea (dehydration prevention).
- Antacids separate from other drugs by 1–2 hours.
