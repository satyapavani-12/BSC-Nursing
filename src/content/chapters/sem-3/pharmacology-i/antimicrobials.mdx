## In simple words

Antimicrobials kill or inhibit microbes — antibacterials (antibiotics), antifungals, antivirals and antiparasitics. Antitubercular drugs treat tuberculosis (TB), a major public health problem in India.

Nurses give antimicrobials on time, monitor for allergy and resistance, and ensure patients complete the full course — especially TB treatment (DOTS).

## Common antimicrobial classes

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Penicillins | Amoxicillin, ampicillin, benzyl penicillin | Gram-positive infections |
| Cephalosporins | Ceftriaxone, cephalexin | Broad-spectrum infections |
| Macrolides | Azithromycin, erythromycin | Respiratory infections; penicillin allergy |
| Fluoroquinolones | Ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin | Urinary, respiratory, GI infections |
| Aminoglycosides | Gentamicin, amikacin | Serious gram-negative infections |
| Antifungals | Fluconazole, amphotericin B | Fungal infections |
| Antivirals | Acyclovir, oseltamivir | Viral infections |

## Antitubercular drugs (first-line)

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| Isoniazid (H) | Kill TB bacteria |
| Rifampicin (R) | Kill TB bacteria |
| Pyrazinamide (Z) | Kill TB bacteria |
| Ethambutol (E) | Prevent resistance |
| Regimen HRZE | Standard first-line TB treatment (2 months HRZE, then 4 months HR) |

## Nurse responsibilities

- Give antibiotics on time — missed doses promote resistance.
- Collect cultures before starting antibiotics when possible.
- Monitor for allergy (rash, anaphylaxis) and side effects (gentamicin: hearing/kidney).
- TB: observe treatment (DOTS) — watch the patient swallow every dose.
- Patient teaching: complete the full course even if feeling better; never share antibiotics.

<Callout type="must" title="Complete the full course">
  **Always complete the full antimicrobial course** — even if the patient feels better. Stopping early promotes resistance and relapse. This is critical in TB treatment (DOTS).
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Give antibiotics on time** — missed doses reduce effectiveness and promote resistance.
- **Collect cultures before antibiotics** when possible (blood, urine, sputum).
- **Monitor for allergy** — rash, itching, anaphylaxis (penicillin allergy is common).
- **Monitor gentamicin** — hearing (ototoxicity) and kidney function (nephrotoxicity).
- **TB DOTS** — observe the patient swallowing every dose; ensure treatment completion (6–9 months).

## 60-second revision

- Antimicrobials kill/inhibit microbes — antibacterials, antifungals, antivirals, antiparasitics.
- Penicillins (gram-positive), cephalosporins (broad spectrum), macrolides, fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides.
- Anti-TB: HRZE — isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol.
- Give antibiotics on time; complete full course (DOTS for TB).
- Monitor allergy and side effects (gentamicin: hearing/kidney).
