## In simple words

Endocrine drugs treat hormone-related disorders — diabetes (insulin, oral hypoglycaemics), thyroid disorders (thyroxine, antithyroid drugs) and inflammation/adrenal disorders (corticosteroids).

Nurses administer insulin, monitor blood glucose, and teach patients about hypoglycaemia and steroid use.

## Common endocrine drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Insulin | Regular, NPH, glargine, aspart | Diabetes mellitus |
| Oral hypoglycaemics | Metformin, glibenclamide, gliclazide | Type 2 diabetes |
| Thyroxine | Levothyroxine | Hypothyroidism |
| Antithyroid | Carbimazole, propylthiouracil | Hyperthyroidism |
| Corticosteroids | Prednisolone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone | Inflammation, allergy, adrenal insufficiency |

## Insulin administration

- Rotate injection sites (abdomen, thigh, arm, buttock) to prevent lipodystrophy.
- Check blood glucose before giving (as ordered).
- Do not mix insulins unless compatible and ordered.
- Rotate sites; inject at 90 degrees (or 45 degrees in thin patients).
- Recognise hypoglycaemia: sweating, trembling, hunger, confusion, weakness — treat with fast-acting sugar.

<Callout type="must" title="Hypoglycaemia recognition">
  **Hypoglycaemia = blood glucose too low (sweating, trembling, hunger, confusion, weakness).** Treat immediately with fast-acting sugar (glucose, juice, sugar water) and recheck.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Insulin administration** — check dose and type; rotate sites; check blood glucose before giving.
- **Oral hypoglycaemics** — give with/after meals (metformin with food to reduce GI upset).
- **Monitor blood glucose** — before meals and at bedtime; record and report abnormalities.
- **Hypoglycaemia treatment** — fast-acting carbohydrate; recheck in 15 minutes; repeat if needed.
- **Corticosteroid teaching** — do not stop suddenly (adrenal suppression); take with food; watch for infection, high blood sugar.
- **Thyroxine** — give on empty stomach in the morning; lifelong therapy for hypothyroidism.

## 60-second revision

- Endocrine drugs: insulin, oral hypoglycaemics, thyroxine, antithyroid, corticosteroids.
- Insulin for diabetes; metformin for type 2 diabetes.
- Thyroxine for hypothyroidism (empty stomach, morning).
- Corticosteroids for inflammation — do not stop suddenly.
- Hypoglycaemia = sweating, trembling, hunger, confusion — treat with fast sugar.
- Rotate insulin sites; check blood glucose before giving.
