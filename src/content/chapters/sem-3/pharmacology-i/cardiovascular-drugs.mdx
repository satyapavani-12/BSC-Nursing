## In simple words

Cardiovascular drugs treat diseases of the heart and circulation — hypertension, angina, arrhythmias, heart failure and clotting disorders. Blood drugs include anticoagulants (prevent clots) and antiplatelets (stop platelet clumping).

Nurses monitor blood pressure, pulse and bleeding risk when giving these drugs.

## Common cardiovascular drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Antihypertensives | Amlodipine, enalapril, propranolol, hydrochlorothiazide | Lower blood pressure |
| Antianginals | Glyceryl trinitrate, isosorbide dinitrate | Relieve angina chest pain |
| Antiarrhythmics | Amiodarone | Treat irregular heart rhythms |
| Diuretics | Furosemide, spironolactone | Remove fluid — heart failure, oedema |
| Anticoagulants | Warfarin, heparin | Prevent/treat blood clots |
| Antiplatelets | Aspirin, clopidogrel | Stop platelets clumping — heart attack, stroke prevention |

## Key nursing points

- **Antihypertensives** — check BP before giving; hold if BP too low (as ordered); rise slowly to avoid dizziness/falls.
- **GTN (sublingual)** — for angina chest pain; sit the patient down (can cause hypotension); repeat up to 3 doses 5 minutes apart if pain persists, then seek emergency help.
- **Anticoagulants** — monitor INR (warfarin) or aPTT (heparin); watch for bleeding (gums, urine, stools, bruising).
- **Diuretics** — monitor potassium (furosemide lowers potassium); give in morning to avoid night-time urination.
- **Aspirin** — give after food; watch for gastric irritation and bleeding.

<Callout type="must" title="GTN for angina">
  **GTN sublingual for angina: sit the patient down, give one tablet under the tongue; if pain persists after 5 minutes, repeat (up to 3 doses), then call for emergency help.**
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Check BP before antihypertensives** — hold and inform doctor if BP is too low (per protocol).
- **Monitor bleeding risk** with anticoagulants — report bleeding gums, blood in urine/stools, unusual bruising.
- **Monitor pulse** before beta-blockers (e.g., propranolol) — hold if pulse too slow (per protocol).
- **Diuretic timing** — give in the morning to avoid disrupting sleep with night-time urination.
- **Patient teaching** — rise slowly (avoid falls from low BP); avoid alcohol; report side effects.

## 60-second revision

- Cardiovascular drugs: antihypertensives, antianginals, antiarrhythmics, diuretics, anticoagulants, antiplatelets.
- GTN for angina — sit patient down; repeat up to 3 doses.
- Warfarin → INR; heparin → aPTT; watch bleeding.
- Furosemide lowers potassium; give diuretics in morning.
- Aspirin after food; watch bleeding/gastric irritation.
