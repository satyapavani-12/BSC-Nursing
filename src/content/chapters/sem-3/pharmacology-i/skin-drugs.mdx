## In simple words

Skin drugs (dermatological drugs) are applied to the skin to treat skin conditions — eczema, fungal infections, acne, psoriasis and itching. They come as creams, ointments, lotions, powders and solutions.

Nurses apply topical drugs correctly and teach patients self-application.

## Common skin drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Topical corticosteroids | Hydrocortisone, betamethasone, clobetasol | Reduce inflammation — eczema, dermatitis |
| Antifungals | Clotrimazole, miconazole, terbinafine | Treat fungal infections — ringworm, candidiasis |
| Antiseptics | Chlorhexidine, povidone-iodine | Clean wounds, prevent infection |
| Emollients | Liquid paraffin, moisturisers | Soften skin — eczema, dry skin |
| Keratolytics | Salicylic acid | Remove scales — warts, psoriasis |
| Topical antibiotics | Mupirocin, fusidic acid | Treat skin infections |

## Safe application

- Wash hands before and after application.
- Apply a thin layer — more is not better.
- Avoid eyes, mouth and broken skin (unless ordered).
- Wear gloves when applying to infected areas.
- Do not use potent topical steroids on the face or for long periods without review.
- Occlusive dressings increase absorption — use only as ordered.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Apply topical drugs** — wash hands, clean the area, apply thin layer, wash hands after.
- **Wear gloves** — when applying to infected or exuding wounds.
- **Patient teaching** — show how to apply; emphasize not sharing topical drugs; avoid eyes.
- **Monitor** — watch for skin irritation, rash, worsening; report to doctor.
- **Steroid caution** — potent topical steroids should not be used on the face or for long periods without medical review (skin thinning risk).

## 60-second revision

- Skin drugs = creams, ointments, lotions, powders, solutions.
- Topical steroids reduce inflammation (eczema, dermatitis).
- Antifungals treat fungal infections (ringworm, candidiasis).
- Emollients soften dry skin; keratolytics remove scales.
- Thin layer; wash hands; avoid eyes; gloves for infected areas.
- Potent steroids — not on face/long term without review.
