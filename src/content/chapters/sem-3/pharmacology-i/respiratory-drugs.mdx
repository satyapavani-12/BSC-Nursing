## In simple words

Respiratory drugs treat diseases of the airways and lungs — asthma, COPD, bronchitis, allergies and cough. They open airways, reduce inflammation, dry secretions or loosen mucus.

Nurses administer these drugs (often by inhaler), teach correct technique and monitor response.

## Common respiratory drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Bronchodilators | Salbutamol, terbutaline, ipratropium | Open airways — asthma, COPD |
| Steroids (inhaled) | Beclometasone, fluticasone | Reduce airway inflammation — asthma |
| Antihistamines | Chlorpheniramine, cetirizine, loratadine | Allergy, runny nose, sneezing |
| Decongestants | Xylometazoline, pseudoephedrine | Blocked nose — short-term |
| Expectorants | Guaifenesin, ammonium chloride | Loosen mucus |
| Antitussives | Codeine, dextromethorphan | Suppress dry cough |

## Inhaler technique (key points)

- Shake the inhaler before use.
- Breathe out fully, then inhale slowly and deeply as you press.
- Hold breath for 5–10 seconds after inhaling.
- Rinse mouth after steroid inhaler (prevents oral thrush).
- Use a spacer with metered-dose inhalers, especially in children.

<Callout type="must" title="Inhaler technique matters">
  **Incorrect inhaler technique means the drug never reaches the lungs.** Teach every patient: shake, breathe out, inhale slowly and deeply, hold breath 5–10 seconds.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Bronchodilators** — give before physiotherapy or exercise to open airways; monitor heart rate (salbutamol can cause tachycardia).
- **Inhaled steroids** — teach mouth rinsing after use to prevent oral thrush.
- **Oxygen and bronchodilators** — in COPD, give oxygen as prescribed (controlled flow) with bronchodilators.
- **Antihistamines** — watch for sedation (first-generation) — caution with driving/elders.
- **Patient teaching** — demonstrate inhaler technique; ask patient to demonstrate back; emphasize spacer use in children.

## 60-second revision

- Respiratory drugs treat asthma, COPD, allergies, cough.
- Bronchodilators open airways; steroids reduce inflammation.
- Antihistamines for allergy; decongestants for blocked nose.
- Expectorants loosen mucus; antitussives suppress dry cough.
- Inhaler technique: shake, breathe out, inhale deeply, hold breath.
- Rinse mouth after steroid inhaler.
