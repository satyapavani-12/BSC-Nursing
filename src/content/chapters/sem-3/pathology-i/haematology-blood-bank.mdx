## In simple words

Haematological tests examine blood and blood-forming tissues. Blood bank services provide safe blood for transfusion. Nurses collect samples, monitor transfusions and recognise transfusion reactions.

## Common haematological tests

| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Haemoglobin (Hb) | Detect anaemia |
| Total leucocyte count (TLC) | Detect infection |
| Differential count (DLC) | Identify white cell types |
| Platelet count | Assess clotting ability |
| Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) | Detect inflammation |
| Bleeding time (BT) / Clotting time (CT) | Assess clotting |
| Prothrombin time (PT) / INR | Monitor warfarin therapy |
| Blood grouping (ABO, Rh) | Safe transfusion |
| Cross-matching | Confirm donor-recipient compatibility |

## Blood grouping (ABO and Rh)

- **ABO groups**: A, B, AB, O — based on antigens on red cells.
- **Rh factor**: positive (D antigen present) or negative.
- **Group O negative** = universal donor; **AB positive** = universal recipient (emergency only).
- **Cross-matching** — mixes donor and recipient blood to confirm compatibility before transfusion.

## Blood transfusion — nurse responsibilities

- **Before**: verify prescription, consent, patient identity (2 identifiers), blood group and cross-match report.
- **During**: start slowly (15 minutes), stay with patient for first 15 minutes, monitor vitals.
- **After**: monitor for reaction, document, discard bag as per policy.
- **Transfusion reaction signs**: fever, chills, rash, itching, breathlessness, back pain, dark urine — STOP transfusion, inform doctor.

<Callout type="must" title="Two identifiers before transfusion">
  **Always check two patient identifiers (name + ID band) and match them to the blood bag before starting transfusion.** A wrong-patient transfusion can be fatal.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Sample collection** — label blood samples correctly with two identifiers; send promptly.
- **Pre-transfusion checks** — verify consent, blood group, cross-match report, expiry, bag integrity.
- **Administration** — use blood administration set; start slow (15 min); stay with patient first 15 minutes; record vitals.
- **Reaction management** — STOP transfusion at first sign of reaction; inform doctor; keep IV line open with normal saline; save bag and tubing.
- **Documentation** — record blood group, cross-match, batch number, start/end times, vitals, reaction if any.

  Universal donor/recipient: **“O gives all, AB takes all.”** O negative = universal donor; AB positive = universal recipient.
</Callout>

## 60-second revision

- Haematological tests: Hb, TLC, DLC, platelets, ESR, BT/CT, PT/INR.
- ABO groups: A, B, AB, O; Rh: positive/negative.
- O negative = universal donor; AB positive = universal recipient.
- Cross-match confirms compatibility.
- Two identifiers before transfusion; monitor for reaction.
- Reaction: STOP transfusion, inform doctor, save bag.
