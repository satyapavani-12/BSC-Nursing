## In simple words

Pathology is the study of disease — its causes, mechanisms, structural changes and effects on the body. General pathology covers the basic processes that underlie most diseases: cell injury, inflammation, healing and repair.

Understanding these basics helps nurses understand what happens inside the body during illness.

## Cell injury

Cells are injured by hypoxia (lack of oxygen), toxins, infections, physical agents (heat, cold, radiation) and immune reactions.

- **Reversible injury** — cell can recover if the cause is removed (e.g., swelling, fatty change).
- **Irreversible injury** — cell dies (necrosis or apoptosis).

- **Necrosis** — cell death due to injury (swelling, rupture, inflammation).
- **Apoptosis** — programmed cell death (normal, controlled, no inflammation).

## Inflammation

Inflammation is the body's response to injury or infection — redness, heat, swelling, pain and loss of function (the 5 cardinal signs).

- **Acute inflammation** — sudden, short-term (neutrophils dominant).
- **Chronic inflammation** — long-term (lymphocytes and macrophages dominant).

## Healing and repair

- **Primary intention** — wound edges close together (clean surgical incision) — fast healing, minimal scarring.
- **Secondary intention** — wound left open (large wound, infection) — slower, more scarring.
- **Tertiary intention** — delayed closure (infected wound cleaned then closed).

Wound healing stages: haemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodelling.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Wound care** — clean wounds heal by primary intention; infected wounds may heal by secondary intention.
- **Inflammation signs** — watch for redness, heat, swelling, pain and loss of function; report early.
- **Healing factors** — nutrition, blood supply, infection control and mobility affect healing.
- **Patient teaching** — explain wound care, signs of infection (increasing redness, pus, fever) and when to seek help.
- **Positioning and mobility** — prevent pressure injury (a form of cell injury from prolonged pressure).

## 60-second revision

- Pathology = study of disease.
- Cell injury: reversible or irreversible; necrosis or apoptosis.
- Inflammation: redness, heat, swelling, pain, loss of function (PRISH).
- Acute = sudden; chronic = long-term.
- Healing: primary (clean surgical), secondary (open wound).
- Wound healing: haemostasis → inflammation → proliferation → remodelling.
