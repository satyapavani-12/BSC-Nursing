## In simple words

The International Patient Safety Goals (IPSG) are six global goals from Joint Commission International to make healthcare safer. Hospitals use them worldwide to prevent harm.

The six goals: identify patients correctly, improve communication, improve medication safety, ensure correct-site surgery, reduce healthcare-associated infection, reduce patient harm from falls.

## The six IPSG goals

| Goal | What nurses do |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify patients correctly | Use two identifiers (name + ID band) before every procedure, medication, transfusion |
| 2. Improve communication | Clear handover (SBAR); read back verbal/telephone orders; report critical results |
| 3. Improve medication safety | Five rights; label medications; extra care with high-alert drugs |
| 4. Ensure correct surgery | Mark the surgical site; time-out before surgery (patient, site, procedure) |
| 5. Reduce HAI | Hand hygiene; aseptic technique; standard precautions |
| 6. Reduce falls | Fall risk assessment; assist mobility; call bell within reach; non-slip footwear |

## Communication tools

- SBAR: Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation — for clear handover.
- Read-back: repeat verbal and telephone orders to confirm.

<Callout type="must" title="Two identifiers">
  **Always use two patient identifiers (name and ID band) before giving medication, blood or doing a procedure.** This is IPSG Goal 1 and prevents wrong-patient errors.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Every patient interaction** — check name and ID band before medication, procedures, blood transfusion.
- **Handover** — use SBAR; read back verbal and telephone orders.
- **Medication** — five rights; double-check high-alert drugs.
- **Surgery** — participate in time-out; confirm surgical site marking.
- **Infection and falls** — hand hygiene, asepsis, fall-risk assessment, assist mobility.
