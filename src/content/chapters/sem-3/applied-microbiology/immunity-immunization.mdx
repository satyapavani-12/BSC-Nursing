## In simple words

Immunity is the body's ability to resist infection. It can be innate (born with it) or acquired (developed during life). Vaccines train the immune system to recognise and fight specific germs without causing the disease.

Nurses give vaccines, explain them to parents, and keep immunisation records.

## Types of immunity

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Innate | Present from birth | Skin, stomach acid, fever |
| Acquired | Developed during life | After infection or vaccination |
| Active | Body makes its own antibodies | Infection or vaccine |
| Passive | Antibodies from another source | Mother to baby, antiserum |

## How vaccines work

A vaccine contains a weakened, killed or partial form of a germ. It stimulates the immune system to make antibodies and memory cells. If the real germ attacks later, the body responds faster and prevents or reduces disease.

## Key vaccines (India, Universal Immunisation Programme)

| Vaccine | Disease prevented | When given |
|---|---|---|
| BCG | Tuberculosis | Birth |
| Hepatitis B | Hepatitis B | Birth, 6, 10, 14 weeks |
| OPV/IPV | Polio | Birth, 6, 10, 14 weeks |
| Pentavalent | Diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, Hib, Hep B | 6, 10, 14 weeks |
| Rotavirus | Diarrhoea | 6, 10, 14 weeks |
| PCV | Pneumonia | 6, 10, 14 weeks |
| MR/MMR | Measles, rubella | 9–12 months, 16–24 months |
| TT | Tetanus | Pregnant women, adolescents |

<Callout type="must" title="Vaccines protect">
  **Vaccines stimulate the body to make antibodies and memory cells without causing the disease.** They protect the individual and the community (herd immunity).
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Immunisation** — give vaccines as per schedule; check age and contraindications.
- **Cold chain** — keep vaccines at the correct temperature from manufacture to use.
- **Catch-up vaccination** — identify missed doses and complete the schedule.
- **Records** — record every vaccine given (date, batch, site, next due).
- **Parent education** — explain benefits, minor side effects (fever, sore arm) and when to seek help.

## 60-second revision

- Immunity = body's ability to resist infection.
- Innate = born with it; acquired = during life.
- Active = body makes antibodies; passive = from another source.
- Vaccine = weakened/killed germ → antibodies + memory.
- Key vaccines: BCG, Hep B, polio, pentavalent, rotavirus, PCV, MR/MMR, TT.
