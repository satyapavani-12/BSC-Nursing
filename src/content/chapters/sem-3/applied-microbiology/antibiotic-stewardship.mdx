## In simple words

Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) happens when microbes (especially bacteria) change so that antibiotics no longer kill them. Resistant infections are harder to treat, last longer and can be deadly.

Antibiotic stewardship means using antibiotics correctly — only when needed, the right drug, dose, route and duration.

## How resistance develops

- Overuse and misuse of antibiotics (for viral infections like colds).
- Not completing the full course.
- Wrong dose or wrong drug.
- Spread of resistant microbes between patients (poor hand hygiene).
- Use in animals and agriculture.

## Common resistant organisms

| Organism | Resistance | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| MRSA | Methicillin | Skin, wound, blood infections |
| VRE | Vancomycin | Enterococcal infections |
| ESBL E. coli | Many beta-lactams | Urinary, blood infections |
| Carbapenem-resistant | Carbapenems | Last-resort drugs failing |

## Nurse's role in stewardship

- Give antibiotics exactly as prescribed — right drug, dose, route, time, duration.
- Never skip doses or stop early when the patient feels better.
- Collect cultures before starting antibiotics when possible.
- Monitor for allergy and side effects.
- Educate patients: antibiotics do not treat colds or flu (viruses).
- Follow hand hygiene and isolation to stop resistant spread.

<Callout type="must" title="Antibiotics don't treat viruses">
  **Antibiotics kill bacteria, not viruses.** Colds, flu and most sore throats are viral — antibiotics do not help and increase resistance.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Administration** — give antibiotics on time; never skip or delay doses.
- **Culture before antibiotics** — when ordered, collect specimens before the first dose.
- **Monitor** — watch for allergy, diarrhoea (C. difficile), kidney and hearing effects.
- **Patient teaching** — finish the full course; never share or save antibiotics.
- **Hand hygiene** — stop resistant organisms spreading between patients.

## 60-second revision

- AMR = antibiotics no longer kill the bacteria.
- Causes: overuse, misuse, incomplete courses, poor infection control.
- MRSA, VRE, ESBL, carbapenem-resistant are key resistant organisms.
- Antibiotics = bacteria only; not viruses.
- Give as prescribed; finish full course; never share.
