## In simple words

Hand hygiene is cleaning hands to remove germs. It is the single most effective way to prevent healthcare-associated infections. Nurses perform it before and after every patient contact.

There are two methods: handwashing with soap and water, and handrubbing with alcohol-based rub.

## WHO 5 moments of hand hygiene

| Moment | When to do it |
|---|---|
| 1 | Before touching a patient |
| 2 | Before a clean/aseptic procedure |
| 3 | After body fluid exposure risk |
| 4 | After touching a patient |
| 5 | After touching patient surroundings |

## Soap and water vs alcohol rub

| Method | When to use |
|---|---|
| Soap and water | Hands visibly dirty, after caring for C. difficile or norovirus, before eating, after toilet |
| Alcohol-based rub | Hands not visibly dirty, most routine clinical moments |

## Correct technique

- Apply product to cover all surfaces.
- Rub palms, backs of hands, between fingers, backs of fingers, thumbs, fingertips, wrists.
- Rub for 20–30 seconds (rub) or 40–60 seconds (wash).
- Dry with disposable towel; use towel to turn off tap.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Before and after every patient contact** — even if you wore gloves.
- **Before aseptic procedures** — inserting IVs, wound dressing, catheter care.
- **After body fluids** — after emptying catheters, wound care, handling specimens.
- **After patient surroundings** — after touching bed rails, charts, equipment.
- **After glove removal** — always clean hands after removing gloves.

## 60-second revision

- Hand hygiene = #1 infection prevention.
- 5 WHO moments: before touch, before procedure, after body fluids, after patient contact, after surroundings.
- Soap & water = visibly dirty; alcohol rub = not visibly dirty.
- Rub 20–30 sec; wash 40–60 sec.
- Clean hands after glove removal.
