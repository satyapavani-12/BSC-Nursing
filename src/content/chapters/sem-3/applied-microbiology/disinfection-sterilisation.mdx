## In simple words

Disinfection kills most harmful microbes on surfaces and equipment but not necessarily all spores. Sterilisation destroys all microbial life, including spores. Nurses choose the right method based on how the item contacts the patient.

## Disinfection vs sterilisation

| Feature | Disinfection | Sterilisation |
|---|---|---|
| Kills | Most microbes (not all spores) | All microbes including spores |
| Use on | Surfaces, non-critical items | Surgical instruments, implants |
| Methods | Chemicals, heat (low) | Autoclave, dry heat, gas, radiation |

## Spaulding classification

| Level | Risk | Items | Processing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | High | Surgical instruments, implants, urinary catheters | Sterilise |
| Semi-critical | Medium | Endoscopes, respiratory devices | High-level disinfection |
| Non-critical | Low | Stethoscope, BP cuff, bedrails | Low/intermediate disinfection |

## Sterilisation methods

- **Autoclaving (steam under pressure)** — 121°C for 15–20 minutes; most reliable; for heat-stable items.
- **Dry heat** — 160–170°C for 1–2 hours; for powders, oils, glassware.
- **Chemical sterilisation** — ethylene oxide, hydrogen peroxide plasma; for heat-sensitive items.
- **Radiation** — gamma radiation for disposable supplies.

## Disinfection levels

- **High-level** — kills all microbes except some spores (e.g., glutaraldehyde, hydrogen peroxide).
- **Intermediate** — kills most bacteria, fungi, viruses (e.g., alcohol, chlorine).
- **Low** — kills most bacteria and some viruses (e.g., quaternary ammonium compounds).

<Callout type="must" title="Critical items must be sterile">
  **Critical items (enter sterile tissue or bloodstream) must be sterile.** Surgical instruments, implants and urinary catheters — sterilise before every use.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Instrument processing** — send surgical instruments for sterilisation; never reuse single-use items.
- **Equipment cleaning** — disinfect stethoscopes, BP cuffs, thermometers between patients.
- **Surface cleaning** — disinfect bedrails, tables and equipment after patient use.
- **Autoclave logs** — check steriliser records; use only properly sterilised packs.
- **Sterile technique** — maintain sterility when opening sterile packs and setting up sterile fields.

## 60-second revision

- Disinfection = most germs, not spores; Sterilisation = all germs including spores.
- Critical = sterilise; Semi-critical = high-level; Non-critical = low-level.
- Autoclave = 121°C, 15–20 min (most reliable).
- Single-use items never reused.
- Check steriliser logs and indicators.
