## In simple words

Microbiology is the study of microorganisms — living things too small to be seen without a microscope. These include bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa and helminths (worms).

Some microbes are helpful (they help digest food and make vitamins); others cause disease (they are called pathogens). Nurses must understand microbes to prevent and control infection.

## Types of microbes

| Type | Size | Example | Disease it can cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bacteria | Very small | Staphylococcus aureus | Skin infection, pneumonia |
| Viruses | Smallest | Influenza virus | Flu, common cold |
| Fungi | Small to medium | Candida albicans | Thrush, skin infection |
| Protozoa | Microscopic | Plasmodium | Malaria |
| Helminths | Worms (large) | Ascaris lumbricoides | Intestinal worm infection |

## How microbes cause disease

A microbe must:
1. Enter the body (portal of entry — skin break, airway, gut, blood).
2. Multiply in a suitable site.
3. Produce toxins or damage tissues.
4. Spread to other sites or people.

The body defends itself through skin, mucus, stomach acid, white cells and antibodies.

## Why microbiology matters in nursing

- **Hand hygiene** — the simplest way to stop germs spreading.
- **Aseptic technique** — keeps germs out of wounds and devices.
- **Isolation** — stops infectious patients spreading germs to others.
- **Antibiotic stewardship** — using antibiotics correctly to prevent resistance.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Hand hygiene** — before and after every patient contact.
- **Aseptic technique** — for wounds, catheters and injections.
- **Standard precautions** — treat all blood and body fluids as infectious.
- **Isolation** — follow isolation signs for infectious patients.
- **Antibiotic stewardship** — give antibiotics only as prescribed, on time, full course.

## 60-second revision

- Microbiology = study of microbes (too small to see).
- Types: bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, helminths.
- Pathogens cause disease; some microbes help the body.
- Microbes enter, multiply, damage tissues, spread.
- Hand hygiene stops spread.
