## In simple words

Specimen collection is obtaining body fluids or tissues for laboratory testing and culture. Correct collection is vital — a contaminated or wrongly labelled specimen gives wrong results and wrong treatment.

The nurse collects, labels, transports and documents the specimen.

## Collection methods

| Specimen | Method | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Blood | Venipuncture into correct tube | Aseptic technique; correct tube |
| Urine | Midstream clean-catch | Clean genital area first; midstream |
| Sputum | Deep cough into sterile container | Not saliva; early morning best |
| Stool | Clean container; no urine/water | Collect from different areas |
| Wound swab | Swab the wound bed, not the edge | Clean wound first if possible |

## Preventing contamination

- Clean the site before collecting.
- Do not touch the inside of the container or the swab tip.
- Collect before antibiotics when possible.
- Transport promptly to the lab.

## Labelling and transport

Label with: patient name, ID number, date/time, specimen type, test requested. Send to lab quickly — some specimens (blood cultures, blood gases) are time-critical.

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Blood culture** — collect before antibiotics; use aseptic technique; correct blood culture bottles.
- **Midstream urine** — clean the genital area; discard first stream; collect midstream.
- **Sputum** — instruct patient to cough deeply from the chest, not spit saliva; collect early morning.
- **Wound swab** — swab the wound bed (pus/tissue), not the edge or dry crust.
- **Labelling** — two identifiers; date and time; send to lab without delay.

## 60-second revision

- Specimen = body fluid/tissue for testing.
- Blood: aseptic, correct tube, before antibiotics.
- Urine: midstream clean-catch.
- Sputum: deep cough, not saliva, early morning.
- Wound swab: wound bed, not edge.
- Label: name, ID, date/time, type, test; send promptly.
