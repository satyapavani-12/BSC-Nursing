## In simple words

Isolation precautions stop germs spreading from infectious patients to others. Standard precautions apply to every patient. Transmission-based precautions (contact, droplet, airborne) are added for specific infections.

PPE (gloves, mask, gown, goggles) protects the nurse. The order of putting on and taking off matters for safety.

## Standard precautions (every patient)

- Hand hygiene before and after contact.
- Gloves when touching blood, body fluids, mucous membranes.
- Mask and eye protection if splashes are likely.
- Gown if clothing may be soiled.
- Safe sharps handling and disposal.

## Transmission-based precautions

| Type | Spread by | Example | Key measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contact | Touch | MRSA, C. difficile | Gown + gloves for room entry |
| Droplet | Large droplets (cough, talk) | Influenza, pertussis | Mask within 1 metre |
| Airborne | Small particles stay in air | TB, chickenpox, measles | N95 respirator, negative pressure room |

## PPE order

Donning: gown → mask → goggles → gloves.
Doffing: gloves → goggles → gown → mask (mask last, outside room). Perform hand hygiene between steps and at the end.

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Every patient** — standard precautions: hand hygiene, gloves for body fluids.
- **Infectious patient** — follow the isolation sign (contact, droplet or airborne).
- **PPE** — put on before entering, remove correctly before leaving.
- **Sharps** — never recap needles; use puncture-proof containers.
- **Patient placement** — airborne infections need negative pressure rooms.

## 60-second revision

- Standard precautions = every patient.
- Contact = gown + gloves; Droplet = mask within 1m; Airborne = N95 + negative room.
- Donning: gown → mask → goggles → gloves.
- Doffing: gloves → goggles → gown → mask (last, outside room).
- Hand hygiene between steps and at end.
