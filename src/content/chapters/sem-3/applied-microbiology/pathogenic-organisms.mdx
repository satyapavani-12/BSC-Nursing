## In simple words

Pathogenic organisms are microbes that cause disease. Nurses must recognise the common pathogens and the illnesses they cause so they can spot symptoms, collect specimens correctly and apply the right precautions.

## Common pathogenic bacteria

| Bacteria | Disease | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Staphylococcus aureus | Skin infection, wound infection, toxic shock | Can be MRSA (resistant) |
| Streptococcus pyogenes | Strep throat, scarlet fever, skin infection | Spreads by droplets |
| Escherichia coli | Urinary infection, diarrhoea | Gut bacteria; some strains dangerous |
| Salmonella | Food poisoning, typhoid | Contaminated food/water |
| Mycobacterium tuberculosis | Tuberculosis | Spreads by air droplets |
| Clostridium tetani | Tetanus | From spores in soil/wounds |

## Common pathogenic viruses

| Virus | Disease | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Influenza virus | Flu | Seasonal; spreads by droplets |
| Hepatitis B virus | Hepatitis B | Blood and body fluids |
| Hepatitis C virus | Hepatitis C | Blood; chronic liver disease |
| HIV | AIDS | Weakens immunity; blood/fluids |
| SARS-CoV-2 | COVID-19 | Respiratory; spreads easily |
| Rabies virus | Rabies | Animal bites; almost always fatal |

## Common pathogenic fungi

| Fungus | Disease | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Candida albicans | Thrush, nappy rash | Yeast; in mouth, vagina, skin folds |
| Aspergillus | Lung infection | From mould spores in air |
| Cryptococcus | Meningitis | From bird droppings; in soil |
| Ringworm fungi | Skin, nail infection | Not a worm; fungal skin infection |

  **MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus) is resistant to many antibiotics.** It needs contact precautions and strict hand hygiene.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Specimen collection** — label swabs, urine, sputum and blood correctly so the lab identifies the pathogen.
- **Standard precautions** — gloves, hand hygiene and safe sharps for every patient.
- **Isolation** — follow the isolation sign for infectious patients (contact, droplet or airborne).
- **Antibiotic stewardship** — give antibiotics as prescribed; never skip doses or stop early.
- **Immunisation** — encourage vaccination for influenza, hepatitis B and COVID-19.

## 60-second revision

- Pathogens = disease-causing microbes.
- Bacteria: Staph, Strep, E.coli, TB, tetanus.
- Viruses: flu, Hep B/C, HIV, COVID.
- Fungi: Candida (thrush), Aspergillus.
- MRSA = resistant Staph; needs contact precautions.
- Standard precautions for every patient.
