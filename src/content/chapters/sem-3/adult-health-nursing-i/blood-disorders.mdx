## In simple terms

Blood disorders affect the blood cells or clotting system — anaemia (low red cells/haemoglobin), leukaemia (cancer of white cells), and bleeding/clotting disorders. Nurses monitor blood counts, give transfusions and prevent bleeding.

## Common blood disorders

| Disorder | Cause/Features | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| Anaemia | Low Hb/red cells (iron, B12, folate deficiency; chronic disease) | Pallor, fatigue, breathlessness, pallor of conjunctiva |
| Iron deficiency anaemia | Low iron (diet, blood loss, absorption) | Pallor, fatigue, koilonychia (spoon nails), pica |
| Leukaemia | Cancer of white blood cells | Fever, infections, bleeding, bruising, bone pain |
| Thrombocytopenia | Low platelets | Bleeding, bruising, petechiae, nosebleeds |
| Haemophilia | Inherited clotting factor deficiency | Prolonged bleeding, joint bleeds |
| DIC | Disseminated intravascular coagulation | Widespread clotting then bleeding |

## Nursing care

- **Anaemia** — give iron/oral iron as ordered (with vitamin C, not tea/coffee); monitor Hb; encourage iron-rich diet; rest periods for fatigue.
- **Transfusion** — two identifiers, check blood group/cross-match, start slowly, monitor for reaction (fever, chills, rash, breathlessness, back pain).
- **Bleeding precautions** — avoid IM injections, use soft toothbrush, avoid rectal temperatures, apply pressure to venepuncture sites.
- **Infection protection (leukaemia)** — hand hygiene, avoid crowds, fresh fruit/vegetables restrictions as ordered, monitor temperature (fever = emergency).

## Nursing care points

- **Bleeding** — apply direct pressure, elevate limb, monitor vital signs, notify doctor.
- **Petechiae/purpura** — document location and size; report new bleeding.
- **Fatigue** — plan rest periods, prioritise activities.
- **Diet** — iron-rich foods (red meat, leafy greens, legumes); vitamin C helps iron absorption; avoid tea/coffee with meals.

<Callout type="must" title="Transfusion reaction">
  **Stop transfusion at the first sign of reaction** (fever, chills, rash, breathlessness, back pain, dark urine); inform doctor; keep IV line open with normal saline; save bag and tubing.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Anaemia** — assess pallor (conjunctiva, palms, nail beds), fatigue, breathlessness; give iron as ordered; teach iron-rich diet.
- **Transfusion** — two identifiers; verify blood group/cross-match; start slow (15 min); stay with patient first 15 minutes; monitor vitals.
- **Bleeding precautions** — avoid IM injections, rectal temperatures; use soft toothbrush; apply pressure after venepuncture.
- **Infection protection** — hand hygiene, monitor temperature (fever in neutropenia = emergency), avoid crowds and sick visitors.
- **Patient teaching** — iron-rich diet, vitamin C with iron, avoid tea/coffee with meals, medication compliance, bleeding precautions.

## 60-second revision

- Blood disorders: anaemia, leukaemia, thrombocytopenia, haemophilia, DIC.
- Anaemia = low Hb/red cells; pallor, fatigue, breathlessness.
- Iron deficiency = most common; iron + vitamin C; avoid tea/coffee.
- Leukaemia = cancer of white cells; infection/bleeding risk.
- Thrombocytopenia = low platelets; bleeding risk; bleeding precautions.
- Transfusion: two identifiers; monitor for reaction; STOP if reaction.
