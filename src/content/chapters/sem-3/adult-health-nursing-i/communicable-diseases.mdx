## In simple words

Communicable diseases spread from person to person or from animals/environment to humans — TB, HIV/AIDS, hepatitis, typhoid, malaria, COVID-19, influenza. Nurses prevent spread through isolation precautions, hand hygiene and patient education.

## Common communicable diseases

| Disease | Transmission | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| Tuberculosis (TB) | Airborne droplets | Chronic cough >2 weeks, weight loss, night sweats, haemoptysis |
| HIV/AIDS | Blood, sexual, mother-to-child | Fever, weight loss, opportunistic infections (late stage) |
| Hepatitis B/C | Blood, sexual, needles | Jaundice, fatigue, nausea, dark urine |
| Typhoid | Faecal-oral (contaminated food/water) | Sustained fever, headache, constipation/diarrhoea, rose spots |
| Malaria | Mosquito bite (Anopheles) | Fever with chills/rigors, sweating, anaemia, jaundice |
| Influenza/COVID-19 | Droplets | Fever, cough, breathlessness, body aches |

## Isolation precautions

- **Standard precautions** — hand hygiene, gloves, PPE as needed, safe sharps, for every patient.
- **Contact precautions** — gown + gloves for room entry (MRSA, C. difficile).
- **Droplet precautions** — mask within 1 metre (influenza, COVID-19, pertussis).
- **Airborne precautions** — N95 respirator, negative pressure room (TB, chickenpox, measles).

## Nursing care

- **Hand hygiene** — before and after every patient contact.
- **PPE** — gloves, mask, gown, eye protection as per isolation category.
- **Specimen collection** — correct technique, label, transport promptly (sputum for TB, blood for HIV/hepatitis/malaria).
- **Patient education** — adherence to treatment (TB DOTS 6–9 months), condom use, needle hygiene, vaccination, completing antibiotics.
- **Contact tracing** — assist with identifying and notifying contacts (TB, HIV, COVID-19).

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Hand hygiene** — before and after every patient contact.
- **Isolation** — follow the isolation sign; correct PPE for the precaution category.
- **Specimen collection** — sputum (TB), blood (HIV/hepatitis/malaria), stool (typhoid); label and transport promptly.
- **Health education** — medication adherence (TB DOTS), condom use, safe injections, vaccination, completion of antibiotics.
- **Reporting** — notifiable diseases (TB, HIV, hepatitis, typhoid, malaria, COVID-19) reported to health authorities.

## 60-second revision

- Communicable diseases: TB, HIV, hepatitis, typhoid, malaria, influenza/COVID-19.
- Transmission: airborne, blood/sexual, faecal-oral, mosquito, droplets.
- Standard precautions for every patient; transmission-based for specific infections.
- TB: airborne precautions; DOTS 6–9 months.
- HIV: condoms, safe needles, ART adherence.
- Report notifiable diseases.
