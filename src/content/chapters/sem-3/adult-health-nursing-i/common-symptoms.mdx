## In simple words

Nurses constantly assess for common signs and symptoms that signal a patient is deteriorating — shock, pain and fluid imbalance. Early recognition and action save lives.

Shock is life-threatening — inadequate tissue perfusion. Pain is whatever the patient says it is. Fluid imbalance means too much or too little fluid.

## Shock

| Type | Cause | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| Hypovolaemic | Blood/fluid loss | Pale, cold, clammy, fast weak pulse, low BP |
| Cardiogenic | Heart failure | Cold clammy, fast pulse, low BP, pulmonary oedema |
| Septic | Infection | Fever or low temp, warm flushed skin early, fast pulse, low BP |
| Anaphylactic | Allergy | Swelling, wheezing, hives, low BP, collapse |
| Neurogenic | Spinal injury | Warm dry skin, low BP, slow pulse |

**Shock signs (universal):** altered consciousness, fast weak pulse, low BP, rapid shallow breathing, cold clammy skin, reduced urine output.

## Pain

- Pain is whatever the patient says it is — always believe the patient.
- Assess with PQRST: Provocation, Quality, Region, Severity (0–10), Time.
- Types: acute (sudden, short-term), chronic (long-term), breakthrough (despite regular analgesia).
- Management: analgesics (WHO ladder), non-drug (positioning, massage, heat/cold, distraction, relaxation).

## Fluid imbalance

- **Dehydration** (deficit): dry mouth, poor skin turgor, low urine output, sunken eyes, dizziness.
- **Fluid overload** (excess): oedema, crackles in lungs, breathlessness, high BP, distended neck veins.
- Monitor intake and output chart daily.

<Callout type="must" title="Shock is an emergency">
  **Shock = inadequate tissue perfusion — life-threatening.** Cold clammy skin, fast weak pulse, low BP, altered consciousness, reduced urine output. Act fast: call for help, position flat, give oxygen, start IV fluids as ordered.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Shock** — lay patient flat, elevate legs (unless contraindicated), keep warm, give oxygen, establish IV access, monitor closely.
- **Pain** — assess with PQRST, give analgesia as ordered, reassess after 30–60 minutes, use non-drug methods.
- **Dehydration** — encourage fluids, IV fluids as ordered, monitor urine output.
- **Fluid overload** — restrict fluids/salt as ordered, sit patient upright, monitor lung sounds and weight.
- **Documentation** — record vital signs, pain score, intake and output accurately.

## 60-second revision

- Shock = inadequate tissue perfusion — life-threatening.
- Shock signs: cold clammy, fast weak pulse, low BP, altered consciousness, reduced urine output.
- Pain = what patient says; PQRST; 0–10 scale.
- Dehydration = dry mouth, poor turgor, low urine output.
- Fluid overload = oedema, crackles, breathlessness.
- Intake and output chart daily.
