## In simple words

Digestive disorders affect the oesophagus, stomach, intestines, liver, gallbladder and pancreas. Common problems include ulcers, gastritis, hepatitis, cirrhosis, gallstones and pancreatitis. Nurses assess, give medications, manage diet and monitor for complications.

## Common digestive disorders

| Disorder | Cause/Features | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| Peptic ulcer | H. pylori, NSAIDs; ulcer in stomach/duodenum | Burning epigastric pain, relieved by food/antacids |
| Gastritis | Stomach lining inflammation | Upper abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting |
| Hepatitis | Liver inflammation (viral A, B, C; alcohol, drugs) | Jaundice, fatigue, nausea, dark urine |
| Cirrhosis | Liver scarring (alcohol, hepatitis) | Jaundice, ascites, bleeding, confusion |
| Cholecystitis | Gallbladder inflammation (gallstones) | Right upper quadrant pain, fever, nausea |
| Pancreatitis | Pancreas inflammation (alcohol, gallstones) | Severe upper abdominal pain, radiating to back |

## Nursing care

- **Peptic ulcer/gastritis** — give medications as ordered (antacids, PPIs, antibiotics for H. pylori); avoid NSAIDs, alcohol, spicy food; small frequent meals.
- **Hepatitis** — monitor jaundice, bleeding tendency; restrict alcohol; hand hygiene (faecal-oral for Hep A/E; blood for B/C).
- **Cirrhosis** — low-salt diet (ascites), monitor bleeding (varices), avoid alcohol; hepatic encephalopathy (confusion) — report.
- **Pancreatitis** — nil by mouth as ordered, IV fluids, pain control, monitor for complications.

## Nursing care points

- **Abdominal assessment** — inspect, auscultate (bowel sounds), palpate lightly, percuss; report rigidity, guarding, rebound tenderness.
- **Vomiting** — record amount and colour; protect airway (side-lying if vomiting).
- **Diet** — follow prescribed diet (soft, low-fat, low-salt as needed).
- **Medications** — antacids, PPIs, antiemetics, antibiotics as ordered.

<Callout type="must" title="Abdominal emergency signs">
  **Rigid abdomen, guarding, rebound tenderness, vomiting blood or black stools = possible surgical emergency (perforation, bleeding).** Report immediately.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Abdominal assessment** — inspect, auscultate bowel sounds, palpate lightly; report abnormal findings.
- **Medications** — antacids/PPIs as ordered; antibiotics for H. pylori; antiemetics for vomiting.
- **Diet** — small frequent meals for ulcers; low-fat for gallbladder/pancreas; low-salt for cirrhosis (ascites).
- **Alcohol counselling** — advise abstinence for hepatitis/cirrhosis/pancreatitis patients.
- **Monitoring** — watch for bleeding (melena, haematemesis), jaundice worsening, confusion (encephalopathy).

## 60-second revision

- Digestive disorders: peptic ulcer, gastritis, hepatitis, cirrhosis, cholecystitis, pancreatitis.
- Peptic ulcer: burning epigastric pain (H. pylori, NSAIDs).
- Hepatitis: jaundice; cirrhosis: jaundice, ascites, bleeding.
- Pancreatitis: severe upper abdominal pain radiating to back.
- Abdominal assessment: inspect, auscultate, palpate lightly.
- Warning: rigid abdomen, rebound tenderness, vomiting blood, black stools = emergency.
