## In simple terms

Genetic services help individuals and families understand and manage genetic conditions. They include genetic counselling, prenatal diagnosis, newborn screening and genetic testing. Nurses play a key role in supporting patients through these services.

## Genetic counselling

Genetic counselling is a communication process that helps individuals and families understand:

- The medical facts of a genetic disorder
- The chance of recurrence in future pregnancies
- Available management and treatment options
- Resources and support groups

## Prenatal diagnosis

Prenatal diagnosis detects genetic or chromosomal abnormalities in a fetus before birth:

| Method | Timing | What it detects |
|---|---|---|
| Maternal serum screening | 15-20 weeks | Down syndrome, neural tube defects |
| Cell-free DNA (NIPT) | 10+ weeks | Chromosomal abnormalities (trisomy 21, 18, 13) |
| Chorionic villus sampling (CVS) | 10-13 weeks | Chromosomal and genetic disorders |
| Amniocentesis | 15-20 weeks | Chromosomal and genetic disorders |

## Newborn screening

Newborn screening tests babies shortly after birth for serious but treatable conditions:

- **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** - inability to metabolise phenylalanine; causes intellectual disability if untreated
- **Congenital hypothyroidism** - low thyroid hormone; causes intellectual disability and growth problems
- **Sickle cell disease** - abnormal haemoglobin; causes pain crises, organ damage
- **Galactosaemia** - inability to metabolise galactose; causes liver damage, intellectual disability
- **Cystic fibrosis** - thick mucus, lung infections, pancreatic insufficiency

## Genetic testing ethics

Genetic testing raises ethical issues:

- **Privacy and confidentiality** - genetic information should be kept confidential
- **Discrimination** - genetic information should not be used to discriminate in employment or insurance
- **Informed consent** - patients must understand the implications of testing before consenting
- **Psychological impact** - genetic test results can cause anxiety, guilt or family conflict

<Callout type="must" title="Informed consent">
  **Genetic testing requires informed consent.** Patients must understand the implications of testing - including psychological impact, privacy concerns and potential discrimination - before agreeing to testing.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Family history** - ask about family history of genetic disorders; document in patient record
- **Genetic counselling referral** - refer patients with family history of genetic disorders to genetics specialists
- **Prenatal screening** - support patients undergoing prenatal genetic testing (amniocentesis, CVS, NIPT)
- **Newborn screening** - support newborn screening programmes; ensure follow-up of abnormal results
- **Patient education** - explain inheritance patterns, recurrence risks, available testing and support groups
- **Emotional support** - provide support to patients and families receiving genetic diagnoses
