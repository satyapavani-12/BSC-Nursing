## In simple terms

Clinical pathology involves laboratory testing of blood, urine, and other body fluids to diagnose disease. Nurses collect specimens, prepare patients for tests, and interpret results to guide patient care.

## Common clinical pathology tests

| Test | Purpose | Key findings |
|---|---|---|
| Complete blood count (CBC) | Blood cell counts | Anemia, infection, bleeding disorders |
| Blood glucose | Blood glucose level | Diabetes diagnosis and monitoring |
| Blood urea nitrogen (BUN) | Kidney function | Kidney dysfunction |
| Serum creatinine | Kidney function | Kidney dysfunction |
| Liver function tests (LFTs) | Liver function | Liver damage, hepatitis |
| Coagulation profile | Blood clotting | Bleeding disorders, anticoagulant monitoring |
| Blood gases | Oxygen, carbon dioxide, pH | Respiratory and metabolic disorders |

## Blood transfusion

| Component | Indication | Administration |
|---|---|---|
| Packed red blood cells | Anemia, blood loss | IV infusion, monitor for reaction |
| Fresh frozen plasma | Clotting factor deficiency | IV infusion, monitor for reaction |
| Platelets | Thrombocytopenia | IV infusion, monitor for reaction |
| Cryoprecipitate | Fibrinogen deficiency | IV infusion, monitor for reaction |

## Nursing care

- **Specimen collection** - collect blood, urine, stool, sputum specimens correctly; label accurately; send promptly to laboratory
- **Blood transfusion** - two identifiers; check blood group/cross-match; start slowly (15 minutes); stay with patient first 15 minutes; monitor vitals
- **Transfusion reaction** - fever, chills, rash, breathlessness, back pain, dark urine; STOP transfusion, inform doctor, keep IV open with normal saline, save bag and tubing
- **Patient teaching** - explain test purpose, preparation requirements, what to expect during procedure
