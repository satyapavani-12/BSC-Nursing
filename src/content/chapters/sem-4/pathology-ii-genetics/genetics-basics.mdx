## In simple terms

Genetics is the study of heredity - how traits are passed from parents to children through genes. Genes are made of DNA and are located on chromosomes. Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs) in each cell.

## Chromosomes, DNA and genes

- **DNA** - deoxyribonucleic acid; the molecule that carries genetic information
- **Gene** - a segment of DNA that codes for a specific protein or trait
- **Chromosome** - a structure of DNA and proteins in the cell nucleus; humans have 46 chromosomes (22 pairs of autosomes + 1 pair of sex chromosomes: XX female, XY male)
- **Genome** - the complete set of genetic material in an organism

## Inheritance patterns

- **Autosomal dominant** - one copy of mutated gene from either parent causes the disorder (e.g., Huntington’s disease)
- **Autosomal recessive** - two copies of mutated gene (one from each parent) cause the disorder (e.g., cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anaemia)
- **X-linked** - gene on X chromosome; more common in males (e.g., haemophilia, colour blindness)
- **Mitochondrial** - inherited from mother only (e.g., some mitochondrial diseases)

## Common genetic disorders

| Disorder | Inheritance | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Down syndrome | Trisomy 21 (extra chromosome 21) | Intellectual disability, characteristic facial features, heart defects |
| Cystic fibrosis | Autosomal recessive | Thick mucus, lung infections, pancreatic insufficiency |
| Sickle cell anaemia | Autosomal recessive | Abnormal haemoglobin, sickle-shaped RBCs, pain crises |
| Haemophilia | X-linked recessive | Prolonged bleeding, joint bleeds, mostly males |
| Huntington’s disease | Autosomal dominant | Neurodegeneration, movement disorder, dementia |
| Turner syndrome | Monosomy X (45,X) | Short stature, webbed neck, ovarian failure (females only) |
| Klinefelter syndrome | 47,XXY | Tall stature, gynecomastia, infertility (males only) |

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Family history** - ask about family history of genetic disorders; document in patient record
- **Genetic counselling** - refer patients with family history of genetic disorders to genetics specialists
- **Prenatal screening** - support patients undergoing prenatal genetic testing (amniocentesis, CVS)
- **Patient education** - explain inheritance patterns, recurrence risks, available testing and support groups
- **Newborn screening** - support newborn screening programmes (e.g., PKU, hypothyroidism, sickle cell disease)

## 60-second revision

- Genetics = study of heredity; DNA → genes → chromosomes.
- Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); XX female, XY male.
- Autosomal dominant = one copy causes disorder; autosomal recessive = two copies.
- Common genetic disorders: Down syndrome, cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anaemia, haemophilia, Huntington’s disease.
- Genetic counselling; newborn screening; patient education.
