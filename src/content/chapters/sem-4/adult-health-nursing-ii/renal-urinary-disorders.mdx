## In simple terms

Renal and urinary disorders affect the kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra. They can cause pain, difficulty urinating, fluid retention, and life-threatening complications if not treated promptly.

Nurses play a key role in monitoring fluid balance, monitoring urine output, and supporting patients undergoing dialysis.

## Common renal & urinary disorders

| Disorder | Cause/Features | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| Acute kidney injury | Sudden loss of kidney function | Decreased urine output, fluid overload, electrolyte imbalance |
| Chronic kidney disease | Progressive loss of kidney function | Fatigue, swelling, nausea, itching |
| Glomerulonephritis | Inflammation of glomeruli | Hematuria, proteinuria, edema, hypertension |
| Urinary tract infection | Bacterial infection of urinary tract | Dysuria, frequency, urgency, suprapubic pain |
| Urinary retention | Inability to empty bladder | Lower abdominal pain, distended bladder |
| Benign prostatic hyperplasia | Enlarged prostate in men | Difficulty urinating, weak stream, nocturia |

## Nursing care

- **Fluid balance** — monitor intake and output strictly; record all fluids in and out
- **Daily weight** — sudden weight gain indicates fluid retention
- **Urinary catheter care** — maintain sterile technique, keep drainage bag below bladder level, measure output hourly
- **Dialysis care** — monitor access site, check blood pressure before/during/after dialysis, assess for disequilibrium syndrome
- **Patient education** — fluid restriction, sodium restriction, medication adherence, signs of infection

<Callout type="must" title="Fluid balance is critical">
  **Strict intake and output monitoring is essential for patients with renal disorders.** Report oliguria (urine output &lt;0.5 mL/kg/hour) immediately.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Fluid management** — record all intake and output; weigh patient daily at same time
- **Catheter care** — maintain closed drainage system; empty bag regularly; measure output
- **Dialysis care** — monitor access site for infection; assess for disequilibrium syndrome
- **Dietary restrictions** — support patient with fluid, sodium, potassium and protein restrictions
- **Patient education** — teach signs of infection, medication adherence, fluid restrictions

## 60-second revision

- Renal disorders: AKI, CKD, glomerulonephritis, UTI, urinary retention, BPH.
- Strict intake and output monitoring.
- Urinary catheter: aseptic technique, bag below bladder level.
- Dialysis: monitor access site, check for disequilibrium syndrome.
- Fluid, sodium, potassium and protein restrictions common.
- Oliguria (&lt;0.5 mL/kg/hour) must be reported immediately.
