## In simple terms

Palliative care is specialized medical care for people living with serious illness. It focuses on providing relief from symptoms, pain, and stress — regardless of the diagnosis or stage of disease. The goal is to improve quality of life for both the patient and the family.

Palliative care can be provided alongside curative treatment and is appropriate at any age and at any stage of a serious illness.

## Goals of palliative care

| Goal | What it means |
|---|---|
| Relief of suffering | Relieve pain and other distressing symptoms |
| Affirm life | Affirm life and regard dying as a normal process |
| Neither hasten nor postpone death | Neither hasten nor postpone death |
| Integrate psychological and spiritual care | Integrate the psychological and spiritual aspects of patient care |
| Support system | Offer a support system to help patients live as actively as possible |
| Support family | Offer a support system to help the family cope during illness and bereavement |

## Common symptoms at end of life

| Symptom | Management |
|---|---|
| Pain | Regular analgesia, WHO analgesic ladder, non-drug methods |
| Dyspnoea | Positioning, oxygen, opioids, anxiolytics |
| Nausea/vomiting | Antiemetics, small frequent meals, avoid strong odours |
| Constipation | Laxatives, fluids, fibre, mobility |
| Anxiety/depression | Counselling, anxiolytics, spiritual support |
| Fatigue | Energy conservation, rest periods, prioritize activities |

## Nursing care

- **Pain assessment** — assess pain regularly using pain scale; administer analgesics as ordered
- **Symptom management** — manage nausea, vomiting, dyspnoea, constipation, agitation
- **Personal care** — mouth care, skin care, positioning, hygiene
- **Emotional support** — listen, be present, validate feelings, provide compassionate presence
- **Family support** — support family through grief and bereavement; provide bereavement resources

<Callout type="must" title="Pain relief is a human right">
  **Every patient has the right to pain relief.** Assess pain regularly using a pain scale; administer analgesics as ordered; reassess after intervention to ensure effectiveness.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Pain assessment** — use validated pain scales; assess location, quality, intensity, duration, aggravating/relieving factors
- **Medication administration** — administer analgesics on schedule (not just PRN); monitor for side effects
- **Symptom management** — anticipate and manage symptoms proactively
- **Emotional support** — listen actively, validate feelings, provide presence and compassion
- **Family support** — provide information, emotional support, bereavement resources; facilitate family meetings

## 60-second revision

- Palliative care = relief of suffering, improvement of quality of life.
- Pain assessment: regular, use pain scale, treat promptly.
- WHO analgesic ladder: non-opioids → weak opioids → strong opioids.
- Common symptoms: pain, dyspnoea, nausea, constipation, anxiety, fatigue.
- Bereavement support: listen, validate, provide resources.
