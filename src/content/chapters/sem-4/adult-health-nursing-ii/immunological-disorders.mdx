## In simple terms

Immunological disorders affect the immune system — either it’s underactive (immunodeficiency like HIV/AIDS), overactive (autoimmune diseases like lupus, rheumatoid arthritis), or misdirected (allergies, anaphylaxis). Nurses play a key role in infection prevention, medication management and patient education.

## Common immunological disorders

| Disorder | Cause/Features | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| HIV/AIDS | HIV attacks CD4 T cells | Weight loss, opportunistic infections, night sweats, fever |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | Autoimmune joint inflammation | Symmetrical joint pain, swelling, morning stiffness |
| Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) | Autoimmune multi-system disease | Butterfly rash, fatigue, joint pain, kidney involvement |
| Anaphylaxis | Severe allergic reaction | Swelling, wheezing, hypotension, hives |
| Multiple allergies | Allergic rhinitis, asthma, food allergy | Sneezing, itching, wheezing, rash |
| Graft-versus-host disease | Donor immune cells attack recipient | Rash, diarrhoea, liver dysfunction |

## Nursing care

- **HIV/AIDS** — administer antiretroviral therapy (ART) on time; monitor CD4 count and viral load; teach safer sex practices; provide emotional support
- **Anaphylaxis** — administer epinephrine IM immediately; monitor airway, breathing, circulation; prepare for intubation if needed
- **Rheumatoid arthritis** — administer NSAIDs/DMARDs as ordered; assist with mobility; teach joint protection techniques
- **Lupus** — monitor for flare-ups; monitor kidney function; teach sun avoidance and sunscreen use
- **Allergy testing** — monitor for anaphylaxis during testing; have emergency equipment ready

  **Anaphylaxis is a medical emergency.** Give epinephrine IM immediately (anterolateral thigh); call for help; monitor airway, breathing, circulation; prepare for intubation.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **HIV care** — administer antiretroviral therapy (ART) on time; monitor CD4 count and viral load; teach safer sex practices; provide emotional support; monitor for opportunistic infections
- **Anaphylaxis** — administer epinephrine IM immediately; monitor airway, breathing, circulation; prepare for intubation if needed; observe for biphasic reaction
- **RA care** — administer NSAIDs/DMARDs as ordered; assist with mobility; teach joint protection techniques; encourage rest during flares
- **Lupus care** — monitor for flare-ups; monitor kidney function (urinalysis, creatinine); teach sun avoidance and sunscreen use; fatigue management
- **Allergy testing** — monitor for anaphylaxis during testing; have emergency equipment (epinephrine, oxygen, airway equipment) readily available

## 60-second revision

- Immunological disorders: HIV/AIDS, rheumatoid arthritis, SLE, anaphylaxis.
- HIV = CD4 cell destruction; ART = lifelong treatment.
- Anaphylaxis = epinephrine IM immediately; monitor airway, breathing, circulation.
- RA = autoimmune joint inflammation; DMARDs; joint protection.
- SLE = autoimmune; butterfly rash; avoid sun exposure.
