## In simple terms

Burns and reconstructive surgery affect the skin and underlying tissues. Burns are classified by depth and extent, and can be life-threatening due to fluid loss, infection risk and airway compromise. Reconstructive surgery restores form and function after trauma, burns or cancer surgery.

Nurses play a critical role in burn assessment, fluid resuscitation, wound care, pain management and rehabilitation.

## Common burn and reconstructive conditions

| Disorder | Cause/Features | Key signs |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal burns | Heat, flame, scalding | Redness, blistering, charring, pain |
| Chemical burns | Chemical exposure | Tissue damage at contact site |
| Electrical burns | Electrical current | Entry/exit wounds, internal tissue damage |
| Chemical burns | Chemicals (acids, alkalis) | Tissue damage, burns |
| Reconstructive surgery | Post-trauma, post-cancer, congenital | Scar revision, reconstruction, grafting |
| Cosmetic surgery | Elective appearance surgery | Elective procedures, recovery care |

## Burn depth classification

| Depth | Appearance | Sensation | Healing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Superficial (1st degree) | Red, painful, no blisters | Painful | 3-7 days, no scarring |
| Partial-thickness (2nd degree) | Blisters, moist, red/white | Very painful | 1-3 weeks, possible scarring |
| Full-thickness (3rd degree) | White, black, leathery, dry | Painless (nerve damage) | Months, grafting needed |

## Nursing care

- **Fluid resuscitation** — Parkland formula: 4 mL × kg body weight × % TBSA burned, half in first 8 hours
- **Wound care** — cleanse, debride, apply topical antimicrobials, dressings
- **Pain management** — burns are extremely painful; provide adequate analgesia
- **Infection prevention** — strict aseptic technique, monitor for sepsis
- **Nutrition** — high protein, high calorie diet for wound healing
- **Patient education** — scar management, sun protection, rehabilitation exercises

</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Burn assessment** — assess depth and extent using rule of nines; document location, depth and size
- **Fluid resuscitation** — calculate and administer IV fluids per Parkland formula; monitor urine output (0.5-1 mL/kg/hour)
- **Wound care** — cleanse, debride, apply topical antimicrobials, change dressings using aseptic technique
- **Pain management** — burns are extremely painful; provide adequate analgesia and monitor effectiveness
- **Patient education** — teach scar management, sun protection, rehabilitation exercises, burn prevention

## 60-second revision

- Burn depths: superficial (1st), partial-thickness (2nd), full-thickness (3rd).
- Parkland formula: 4 mL × kg × % TBSA; half in first 8 hours.
- Urine output target: 0.5-1 mL/kg/hour.
- Full-thickness burns are painless (nerve destroyed); require grafting.
- Infection prevention is critical in burn care.
- Rule of nines: head 9%, each arm 9%, front trunk 18%, back trunk 18%, each leg 18%, perineum 1%.
