## In simple words

Ethics is the study of what is right and wrong. Bioethics is ethics applied to health care — birth, illness, treatment, research and death. An ethical nurse does the right thing because it is right, not only because the law says so.

Law is the minimum standard; ethics is the higher standard. A nurse must satisfy both.

## Principles of bioethics

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | Respect the patient’s right to decide | Taking informed consent before a procedure |
| Beneficence | Do good, promote the patient’s welfare | Relieving pain promptly |
| Non-maleficence | Do no harm | Checking the five rights before giving a drug |
| Justice | Be fair; give equal care | Same care for all castes, religions and incomes |
| Fidelity | Keep promises and be loyal | Returning when you promised to check on a patient |
| Veracity | Tell the truth | Answering the patient honestly within policy |
| Confidentiality | Protect patient information | Not discussing a patient in the corridor |
| Accountability | Be answerable for your actions | Reporting your own medication error |

## Ethical theories

- **Deontology** — duty-based; an act is right if it follows a duty or rule, whatever the result (e.g. never lie to a patient).
- **Utilitarianism** — result-based; the act that produces the greatest good for the greatest number is right (e.g. allocating one ventilator to the patient most likely to survive).
- **Virtue ethics** — focuses on the character of the nurse (honest, compassionate, courageous).
- **Principlism** — uses the four main principles (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice).

## Common ethical dilemmas

| Dilemma | Conflict |
|---|---|
| Informed consent vs family pressure | Autonomy vs family wishes |
| Truth telling about a poor prognosis | Veracity vs cultural practice of protecting the patient |
| Euthanasia or assisted dying | Autonomy and beneficence vs non-maleficence and law |
| Abortion (MTP) | Autonomy vs sanctity of life |
| Do-not-resuscitate order | Beneficence vs sanctity of life |
| Allocating a scarce ICU bed or ventilator | Justice vs individual need |
| Reporting a colleague’s error | Loyalty vs accountability and patient safety |
| HIV disclosure to a partner | Confidentiality vs duty to protect others |

<Callout type="must" title="Informed consent">
  Consent must be **informed** (procedure, benefits, risks, alternatives explained), **voluntary** (no pressure), given by a **competent** person, and **documented**. In India, consent for a child under 18 is taken from the parent or legal guardian.
</Callout>

## Legal aspects of nursing

| Legal term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Negligence | Failure to give the standard of care that a reasonable nurse would give |
| Malpractice | Professional negligence — negligence while practising a profession |
| Battery | Touching a patient without consent |
| Assault | Threatening to touch a patient without consent |
| False imprisonment | Unlawfully restricting a patient’s movement |
| Defamation | Harming a person’s reputation |
| Invasion of privacy | Revealing patient information without permission |

### Laws relevant to Indian nursing

- **Indian Nursing Council Act, 1947** — registration and standards of nursing
- **Clinical Establishments Act, 2010** — standards for clinical facilities
- **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** — patients are consumers; negligence can be challenged
- **Medical Termination of Pregnancy Act, 1971 (amended 2021)** — conditions for safe abortion
- **Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act, 1994** — organ donation rules
- **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012** — mandatory reporting of child abuse
- **Mental Healthcare Act, 2017** — rights of mentally ill persons
- **Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016** — safe disposal of waste

## Nurse’s legal responsibilities

- **Valid registration** with the State Nursing Council
- **Accurate documentation** — write only what you did and saw; never alter records
- **Informed consent** before procedures
- **Confidentiality** of patient records
- **Safe medication practice** — five rights, double check, report errors
- **Mandatory reporting** — child abuse, sexual assault, notifiable diseases, needle-stick injury
- **Follow protocols** — infection control, biomedical waste, blood transfusion
- **Never work beyond competence**; ask for help

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Ward rounds** — honest communication, respect for refusal
- **Consent** — explain, witness, document
- **Records** — complete, timed, signed, factual entries
- **Error** — report immediately, document, cooperate with the investigation
- **Ethics committee** — refer unresolved dilemmas
- **Patient rights** — privacy, information, second opinion, dignity

## 60-second revision

- Ethics = right and wrong; bioethics = health-care ethics.
- Principles: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice (+ fidelity, veracity, confidentiality, accountability).
- Deontology = duty; utilitarianism = greatest good for the greatest number.
- Decision making: recognise, identify, list, choose, act and evaluate.
- Informed consent: informed, voluntary, competent, documented.
- Negligence vs malpractice; battery vs assault.
- Laws: INC Act 1947, CPA 2019, MTP Act, THOTA 1994, POCSO 2012, Mental Healthcare Act 2017, BMW Rules 2016.
- Document accurately; never alter records; report errors and abuse.
