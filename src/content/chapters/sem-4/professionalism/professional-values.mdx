## In simple words

Values are the beliefs that guide what we think is right and important. Professional values are the beliefs shared by the nursing profession that shape how a nurse behaves with patients, families and colleagues.

The six core values of nursing (from the AACN framework) are **caring, altruism, autonomy, human dignity, integrity and social justice**. Indian nursing education also stresses **patient advocacy** and **confidentiality**.

## The six core professional values

| Value | Meaning | Ward example |
|---|---|---|
| Caring | Concern for the wellbeing of others, shown through action | Sitting with a frightened patient and explaining a procedure |
| Altruism | Selfless concern for the patient’s welfare before one’s own | Staying a few minutes extra to comfort a dying patient’s family |
| Autonomy | Respect for the patient’s right to decide | Letting the patient choose the timing of a bath |
| Human dignity | Treating every person as worthy of respect | Covering the patient during bed bath; calling by name |
| Integrity | Honesty, truthfulness and consistency between values and actions | Admitting a mistake instead of hiding it |
| Social justice | Fairness and equal care for all | Giving the same quality of care to rich and poor, rural and urban |

## Values clarification

Values clarification is the process of examining one’s own beliefs to understand why one acts in a certain way.

- **Choosing** — choose freely after considering alternatives and consequences
- **Prizing** — feel proud of the value and be willing to state it publicly
- **Acting** — act repeatedly according to the chosen value

Nurses need values clarification because personal values (religion, culture, family upbringing) can clash with professional values and with the patient’s values.

<Callout type="must" title="Human dignity is non-negotiable">
  Every patient — rich or poor, young or old, infected or clean — deserves the **same respect, privacy and courtesy**. Never let personal likes or dislikes change the quality of care.
</Callout>

## Professional socialisation

Professional socialisation is the process by which a student nurse learns the values, behaviour and culture of the nursing profession.

- **Stages** — novice → advanced beginner → competent → proficient → expert (Benner)
- **Agents** — teachers, clinical instructors, senior nurses, role models, peers
- **Methods** — role modelling, supervised practice, reflection, mentoring, code of conduct
- **Outcome** — a nurse who thinks and acts like a professional

## Values in conflict

| Situation | Professional value | Right action |
|---|---|---|
| Patient refuses a prescribed injection | Autonomy | Respect refusal, inform doctor, document |
| Nurse finds a colleague stealing drugs | Integrity, social justice | Report through proper channel |
| Poor patient cannot pay for a test | Social justice, altruism | Explore free schemes, inform authorities |
| Patient asks about a bad prognosis | Integrity, autonomy | Follow hospital policy and patient’s right to know |

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Admission** — call the patient by name, introduce yourself, explain the plan
- **Personal care** — close curtains, cover the patient, ask permission before touching
- **Decision making** — involve the patient in choices about timing and position
- **Resource use** — distribute time and supplies fairly among all patients
- **Reporting** — be honest about errors and near misses
- **Team behaviour** — speak respectfully about patients and colleagues

## 60-second revision

- Values = beliefs that guide behaviour; professional values are shared by nursing.
- Six core values: caring, altruism, autonomy, human dignity, integrity, social justice.
- Values clarification = choosing, prizing, acting.
- Professional socialisation = learning nursing values and behaviour (novice to expert).
- Conflict rule: patient safety and dignity come first.
- Advocacy and confidentiality support all professional values.
