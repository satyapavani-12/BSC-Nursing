## In simple terms

Prescribing is the process by which authorized healthcare providers (doctors, nurse practitioners, physician assistants) authorize medications for patients. Nurses do not prescribe in most settings but play a critical role in verifying prescriptions, transcribing orders, administering medications, and monitoring for errors.

Understanding prescriptions helps nurses identify errors before they reach the patient.

## Parts of a prescription

A complete prescription includes:

| Element | What it means |
|---|---|
| Prescriber information | Name, address, license number, DEA number (for controlled substances) |
| Patient information | Name, age, weight, address, date |
| Date | Date the prescription was written |
| Superscription | The symbol "Rx" (from Latin "recipe" - take) |
| Inscription | The drug name, strength, dosage form, quantity |
| Subscription | Directions to the pharmacist (how to compound/dispense) |
| Signatura (Sig) | Directions to the patient (how to take the medication) |
| Refills | Number of refills authorized |
| Signature | Prescriber's signature and DEA number |

## Common prescription abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning | Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ac | Before meals | pc | After meals |
| bid | Twice daily | tid | Three times daily |
| qid | Four times daily | qd | Every day |
| po | By mouth | prn | As needed |
| IM | Intramuscular | IV | Intravenous |
| SC | Subcutaneous | stat | Immediately |
| NPO | Nothing by mouth | q4h | Every 4 hours |

## Common prescription errors

- **Illegible handwriting** - unclear drug name, dose, or directions
- **Missing information** - missing dose, route, frequency, or duration
- **Wrong drug** - look-alike/sound-alike medications
- **Wrong dose** - decimal point errors, missing leading zero, trailing zero
- **Missing signatures** - missing prescriber signature or DEA number

## Nurse's role in medication safety

- **Verify prescriptions** - check against the original order, verify patient identity
- **Clarify unclear orders** - clarify any unclear or illegible prescriptions with the prescriber
- **Verify patient identity** - use two identifiers before administering medication
- **Monitor for adverse effects** - monitor for therapeutic effects and adverse reactions
- **Document administration** - document medication administration accurately and timely
- **Report errors** - report medication errors and near misses through incident reporting systems

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Prescription verification** - check prescriptions against orders, verify patient identity before administration
- **Medication administration** - administer medications as prescribed, monitor for effects
- **Error prevention** - clarify unclear prescriptions, report medication errors and near misses
- **Patient education** - teach patients about their medications, how to take them, what to expect
- **Documentation** - document medication administration accurately and timely

## 60-second revision

- Prescription parts: prescriber info, patient info, date, Rx symbol, inscription, subscription, signatura, refills, signature.
- Common abbreviations: PO (by mouth), PRN (as needed), BID/TID/QID (twice/three/four times daily), IM/IV/SC (routes).
- Prescription errors: wrong drug, wrong dose, wrong route, missing information, illegible handwriting.
- Nurse's role: verify prescriptions, clarify unclear orders, verify patient identity, monitor for adverse effects, document administration.
- Decimal point safety: never use trailing zero (5.0), always use leading zero (0.5).
