## In simple terms

Drugs acting on the nervous system affect the brain and spinal cord. They include sedatives/hypnotics (sleep/anxiety), anticonvulsants (seizures), analgesics (pain), and CNS stimulants. Nurses administer these drugs, monitor for effectiveness and side effects, and teach patients about safe use.

## Common nervous system drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Sedatives/hypnotics | Diazepam, lorazepam, zolpidem | Anxiety, insomnia, sedation |
| Anticonvulsants | Phenytoin, carbamazepine, sodium valproate, lamotrigine | Epilepsy/seizures |
| Analgesics (opioids) | Morphine, fentanyl, pethidine | Severe pain |
| Analgesics (non-opioid) | Paracetamol, ibuprofen | Mild-moderate pain, fever |
| CNS stimulants | Caffeine, modafinil | Alertness |

## Sedatives and hypnotics

- **Benzodiazepines** (diazepam, lorazepam) - anxiety, seizures, alcohol withdrawal, premedication; cause sedation, respiratory depression, dependence
- **Non-benzodiazepine hypnotics** (zolpidem) - insomnia; shorter-acting
- **Barbiturates** (phenobarbitone) - older anticonvulsant/sedative; respiratory depression risk

## Anticonvulsants

- **Phenytoin** - narrow therapeutic range; monitor blood levels; gingival hyperplasia; do not stop suddenly
- **Sodium valproate** - broad-spectrum; teratogenic (avoid in pregnancy); monitor liver
- **Carbamazepine** - epilepsy, trigeminal neuralgia; monitor blood counts
- **Status epilepticus** - IV diazepam/lorazepam as emergency treatment

## Analgesics (WHO analgesic ladder)

- **Step 1** - paracetamol, NSAIDs (ibuprofen) - mild pain
- **Step 2** - weak opioids (codeine, tramadol) - moderate pain
- **Step 3** - strong opioids (morphine, fentanyl) - severe pain; monitor respiration (respiratory depression), constipation, nausea; naloxone is opioid antagonist

## Nursing care

- **Sedatives/hypnotics** - give at bedtime; fall risk (elderly); monitor respiratory depression; avoid alcohol; do not stop suddenly
- **Anticonvulsants** - give on time (missed dose = seizure risk); monitor blood levels (phenytoin); watch for rash (serious); do not stop suddenly
- **Opioids** - monitor respiration, sedation, constipation; give naloxone for opioid overdose; bowel regimen for constipation
- **Non-opioid analgesics** - paracetamol (max daily dose; liver toxicity if overdose); NSAIDs (take with food; monitor for GI bleeding and kidney)

<Callout type="must" title="Opioid safety">
  **Opioids cause respiratory depression - monitor respiration rate and depth; keep naloxone (antidote) available; do not give if respiratory rate is below normal without medical advice.**
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Sedatives/hypnotics** - give at bedtime; fall risk (elderly); monitor respiratory depression; avoid alcohol; do not stop suddenly
- **Anticonvulsants** - give on time (missed dose = seizure risk); monitor blood levels (phenytoin); watch for rash (serious); do not stop suddenly
- **Opioids** - monitor respiration, sedation, constipation; give naloxone for opioid overdose; bowel regimen for constipation
- **Non-opioid analgesics** - paracetamol (max daily dose; liver toxicity if overdose); NSAIDs (take with food; monitor for GI bleeding and kidney)

## 60-second revision

- Nervous system drugs: sedatives/hypnotics, anticonvulsants, analgesics, CNS stimulants.
- Sedatives (diazepam, lorazepam, zolpidem) - anxiety, insomnia; respiratory depression risk.
- Anticonvulsants (phenytoin, valproate, carbamazepine) - epilepsy; give on time; do not stop suddenly.
- WHO analgesic ladder: non-opioid → weak opioid → strong opioid (morphine).
- Opioids: respiratory depression; naloxone antidote; constipation - bowel regimen.
- Paracetamol: maximum daily dose; liver toxicity if overdose.
