## In simple terms

Urinary system drugs treat conditions of the kidneys and bladder - fluid overload, hypertension, urinary tract infections, urinary retention and benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). Nurses monitor output, electrolytes and blood pressure when giving these drugs.

## Common urinary drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Loop diuretics | Furosemide, bumetanide | Heart failure, oedema, hypertension |
| Thiazide diuretics | Hydrochlorothiazide | Hypertension, mild oedema |
| Potassium-sparing diuretics | Spironolactone, amiloride | Retain potassium; heart failure, ascites |
| Urinary antiseptics | Nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim | Urinary tract infection (UTI) |
| Urinary analgesics | Phenazopyridine | Urinary pain relief (symptomatic) |
| Alpha-blockers | Tamsulosin, terazosin | Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) - urine flow |
| 5-alpha reductase inhibitors | Finasteride | BPH - shrink prostate |

## Diuretics

- **Loop diuretics** (furosemide) - act on loop of Henle; powerful; cause potassium loss (hypokalaemia); monitor potassium and blood pressure
- **Thiazides** (hydrochlorothiazide) - mild; hypertension; cause potassium loss
- **Potassium-sparing** (spironolactone, amiloride) - retain potassium; used with other diuretics to prevent hypokalaemia; monitor for hyperkalaemia

## Nursing care

- **Diuretics** - give in the morning (avoid night-time urination); monitor blood pressure, electrolytes (potassium), weight, urine output
- **Urinary antiseptics** - nitrofurantoin for UTI; take with food; complete the course; report dark urine (harmless discolouration from nitrofurantoin)
- **Alpha-blockers** (tamsulosin) - first dose may cause postural hypotension; advise rising slowly; take at bedtime initially
- **Urinary retention** - monitor for inability to void; report to doctor (may need catheterisation)

- **Alpha-blockers** (tamsulosin) - first dose may cause postural hypotension; advise rising slowly; take at bedtime initially
- **Urinary retention** - monitor for inability to void; report to doctor (may need catheterisation)
