## In simple terms

Emergency drugs are used in life-threatening situations (cardiac arrest, anaphylaxis, hypoglycaemia, opioid overdose). Antidotes reverse specific poisons or drug overdoses. Anticancer drugs (chemotherapy) treat cancer but have significant toxicity.

Nurses administer these carefully, monitor closely and know the relevant antidotes.

## Emergency drugs

| Drug | Use | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Adrenaline (epinephrine) | Anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest | IM for anaphylaxis; IV/IO for cardiac arrest |
| Atropine | Bradycardia, organophosphate poisoning | Increases heart rate |
| Sodium bicarbonate | Metabolic acidosis, tricyclic overdose | Sodium/alkali |
| Dextrose 50% | Hypoglycaemia | Rapid IV glucose |
| Naloxone | Opioid overdose | Reverses respiratory depression |
| Glucagon | Severe hypoglycaemia (no IV access) | Raises blood glucose |

## Antidotes

| Poison/drug | Antidote |
|---|---|
| Opioid overdose | Naloxone |
| Benzodiazepine overdose | Flumazenil (use cautiously) |
| Paracetamol overdose | N-acetylcysteine (NAC) |
| Warfarin overdose | Vitamin K |
| Heparin overdose | Protamine sulfate |
| Organophosphate poisoning | Atropine |
| Cyanide poisoning | Sodium nitrite + sodium thiosulfate |
| Iron overdose | Deferoxamine |
| Anticholinergic overdose | Physostigmine (rarely) |

## Anticancer drugs (chemotherapy)

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Alkylating agents | Cyclophosphamide, cisplatin | Various cancers |
| Antimetabolites | Methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil | Various cancers |
| Plant alkaloids | Vincristine, paclitaxel | Various cancers |
| Topoisomerase inhibitors | Doxorubicin, etoposide | Various cancers (doxorubicin — cardiotoxicity) |

## Chemotherapy nursing care

- **Extravasation** — vesicant drugs (e.g., doxorubicin, vincristine) cause tissue damage if they leak; stop infusion, aspirate, notify doctor.
- **Personal protection** — gloves, gown, mask, goggles; dispose of waste in cytotoxic bins.
- **Monitoring** — bone marrow suppression (infection, bleeding, anaemia), nausea/vomiting (antiemetics), alopecia (temporary).
- **Patient teaching** — infection avoidance (hand hygiene, avoid crowds), oral care, report fever (>38°C) immediately.

<Callout type="must" title="Chemotherapy extravasation">
  **Chemotherapy extravasation (vesicant leakage into tissue) causes tissue necrosis.** Stop infusion immediately, aspirate, do not flush, notify doctor, elevate limb, apply cold/warm compress as ordered, document.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Anaphylaxis** — adrenaline IM immediately; call for help; monitor airway, breathing, circulation.
- **Cardiac arrest** — adrenaline IV/IO as part of CPR algorithm.
- **Hypoglycaemia** — dextrose IV (if IV access) or glucagon IM (if no IV access).
- **Opioid overdose** — naloxone IV/IM/IN; monitor for recurrence (naloxone duration shorter than some opioids).
- **Chemotherapy** — PPE (gloves, gown, mask, goggles); double gloves; dispose of waste in cytotoxic bins; monitor for infection (fever >38°C = emergency).
- **Antidotes** — know common antidotes and their indications; keep naloxone accessible in opioid areas.

## 60-second revision

- Emergency drugs: adrenaline (anaphylaxis, arrest), atropine (bradycardia), dextrose 50% (hypoglycaemia), sodium bicarbonate (metabolic acidosis), naloxone (opioid overdose), glucagon (hypoglycaemia no IV).
- Antidotes: naloxone (opioid), NAC (paracetamol), vitamin K (warfarin), protamine sulfate (heparin), atropine (organophosphate).
- Anticancer drugs — chemotherapy; cause bone marrow suppression, nausea/vomiting, alopecia.
- Chemotherapy extravasation — stop infusion, aspirate, notify doctor, elevate limb.
- Chemotherapy safety — PPE; avoid skin contact; cytotoxic waste; fever >38°C = emergency.
