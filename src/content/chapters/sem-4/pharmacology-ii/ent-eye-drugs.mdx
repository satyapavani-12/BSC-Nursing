## In simple terms

ENT, eye and mouth drugs treat conditions of the ear, nose, throat, eyes and mouth - congestion, allergy, infection, glaucoma, dry mouth and mouth ulcers. Most are applied locally (drops, sprays, lozenges, mouthwashes).

Nurses administer these drugs, teach correct technique (especially eye/ear drops) and monitor for side effects.

## Common ENT drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Decongestants | Xylometazoline, oxymetazoline (nasal) | Blocked nose - short-term |
| Antihistamines | Cetirizine, loratadine, chlorpheniramine | Allergy, rhinitis, sneezing |
| Antiseptic lozenges/sprays | Cetylpyridinium, benzocaine | Sore throat, mouth infections |
| Antibacterial ear drops | Ciprofloxacin, ofloxacin | Otitis externa (ear canal infection) |

## Common eye drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Antibiotic eye drops | Chloramphenicol, ciprofloxacin | Conjunctivitis (bacterial) |
| Anti-glaucoma drops | Timolol, brimonidine, latanoprost | Lower intraocular pressure (glaucoma) |
| Lubricants (artificial tears) | Hypromellose, carboxymethylcellulose | Dry eyes |
| Mydriatics | Tropicamide | Dilate pupil for examination |

## Common mouth drugs

| Class | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Antiseptic mouthwash | Chlorhexidine | Gingivitis, post-dental surgery, plaque |
| Analgesic lozenges | Benzocaine, lidocaine | Sore throat, mouth ulcer pain |
| Antifungal mouth paint | Nystatin | Oral thrush (candidiasis) |

## Eye and ear drop administration

- **Eye drops** - wash hands, tilt head back, pull lower lid down, instill drop into conjunctival sac (not on cornea), press inner corner (punctum) 1 minute, wait 5 minutes between different drops
- **Ear drops** - warm to room temperature; adults: pull pinna up and back; children: pull pinna down and back; instill drops; keep head tilted 1-2 minutes

<Callout type="must" title="Eye drop technique">
  **Instill eye drops into the conjunctival sac (lower lid pocket), not directly on the cornea.** Press inner corner (punctum) 1 minute after instilling to reduce systemic absorption.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Eye drops** - wash hands, check correct eye/drug, instill into conjunctival sac, press punctum, wait 5 minutes between different drops; teach patient self-administration
- **Ear drops** - warm to room temperature; adults: pull pinna up and back; children: pull pinna down and back; instill drops; keep head tilted 1-2 minutes
- **Nasal drops/sprays** - clear nose first, tilt head forward, avoid overuse of decongestant sprays (more than 5-7 days causes rebound congestion)
- **Mouthwash** - chlorhexidine after dental surgery; do not eat/drink for 30 minutes after; staining of teeth with prolonged use
- **Patient teaching** - demonstrate technique, ask patient to demonstrate back, warn about rebound congestion with prolonged decongestant use

## 60-second revision

- ENT drugs: decongestants (blocked nose), antihistamines (allergy), lozenges (sore throat).
- Eye drops: antibiotics, anti-glaucoma, lubricants; instill into conjunctival sac.
- Mouth drugs: chlorhexidine mouthwash, benzocaine lozenges, nystatin (thrush).
- Ear drops: warm first; adults pull pinna up-and-back; children pull pinna down-and-back.
- Nasal decongestant sprays: max 5-7 days (rebound congestion).
