## In simple terms

Drugs in pregnancy and labour must be used carefully because they affect both mother and baby. Common drugs: oxytocin (labour induction/PPH), ergometrine (PPH), magnesium sulphate (pre-eclampsia/eclampsia), antenatal corticosteroids (fetal lung maturity), and tocolytics (preterm labour).

Nurses monitor mother and fetus, give medications safely and recognise complications.

## Common obstetric drugs

| Drug | Use | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Oxytocin | Labour induction/augmentation, PPH | IV infusion; monitor contractions, fetal heart |
| Ergometrine | Postpartum haemorrhage | Uterotonic; avoid in hypertension/pre-eclampsia |
| Magnesium sulphate | Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia (seizure prevention) | Monitor reflexes, respiration, urine output; calcium gluconate is antidote |
| Antenatal corticosteroids | Betamethasone, dexamethasone | Fetal lung maturity (preterm labour under 34 weeks) |
| Tocolytics | Nifedipine | Delay preterm labour |
| Misoprostol | Labour induction, PPH, MTP | Oral/sublingual; uterine contractor |

## Magnesium sulphate (pre-eclampsia/eclampsia)

- **Use** — prevents and treats eclamptic seizures; also fetal neuroprotection (preterm under 32 weeks).
- **Monitoring** — respiratory rate (>16/min), deep tendon reflexes (present), urine output (>25 mL/hr) — if any fall, stop and inform doctor.
- **Antidote** — calcium gluconate 10% IV for magnesium toxicity (respiratory arrest, loss of reflexes).
- **Therapeutic range** — 2-4 mmol/L (therapeutic); toxicity >3.5-4 mmol/L.

## Antenatal corticosteroids

- **Betamethasone/dexamethasone** — given to mother before preterm birth (under 34 weeks) to accelerate fetal lung maturity and reduce RDS.
- **Timing** — best if given 24 hours to 7 days before delivery.

## Nursing care

- **Oxytocin infusion** — monitor contractions (frequency, duration, intensity), fetal heart rate, BP; titrate as ordered; stop if tachysystole or fetal distress.
- **Magnesium sulphate** — monitor reflexes (patellar), respiration rate, urine output hourly; calcium gluconate is antidote.
- **PPH management** — uterotonics (oxytocin first-line), fundal massage, IV access, blood replacement; monitor for shock.
- **Patient teaching** — explain purpose, side effects, report symptoms (headache, visual changes, severe headache — pre-eclampsia warning).

<Callout type="must" title="Magnesium sulphate monitoring">
  **Monitor reflexes, respiration rate and urine output hourly during magnesium sulphate.** Loss of reflexes or respiration below 16/min = toxicity — stop infusion, inform doctor, calcium gluconate is the antidote.
</Callout>

## Where the nurse uses this

- **Labour induction** — oxytocin infusion; monitor contractions and fetal heart rate; titrate as ordered; stop if tachysystole or fetal distress.
- **Pre-eclampsia/eclampsia** — magnesium sulphate; monitor reflexes, respiration, urine output; calcium gluconate at bedside; seizure precautions.
- **PPH** — uterotonics (oxytocin first-line), fundal massage, IV access, blood replacement; monitor for shock.
- **Preterm labour** — antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone) to mother; tocolytics (nifedipine) to delay labour; prepare for neonatal care.
- **Patient teaching** — explain purpose and side effects; report symptoms (headache, visual changes, severe headache — pre-eclampsia warning signs).
